# SERL Recovery Strategy

**Status:** Implemented and bounded-evaluated; no promoted recovery improvement
**Last updated:** 2026-09-23

This note explains how failed trajectories contribute to reinforcement learning,
how to warm-start online training from the current behavior-cloned transformer,
and two candidate exploration strategies for recovering from misalignment,
high-force contact, and cable snags.

The proposed probabilistic actor and the distinction between a noisy Gaussian
and a multimodal trajectory distribution are documented in
[Probabilistic trajectory actor for SERL](SERL_PROBABILISTIC_ACTOR.md).

## September 23 video evidence

The implemented SFP-to-NIC recovery uses 0.25 mm measured-path commands, at
least 1 mm clearance, and a 12-step near-lateral retry. In the recording-enabled
comparison, BC and selected RL each scored 1/8 and RL plus recovery scored 3/8.
This does not establish a recovery improvement: the earlier metrics-only BC/RL
results were 3/8, recovery-mode telemetry was not retained, and most reviewed
failures were lateral divergence rather than cable snagging. A small retreat
can plausibly release port-lip contact because 1 mm exceeds the 0.5 mm success
corridor, but it is not a demonstrated solution for cable routing around cards.

For multi-card SC-to-SC, compare the current local recovery against predeclared
larger clearances, larger bounded lateral moves, and an observation-driven
detour option. First reproduce natural snags and retain compact force, measured
motion, recovery mode, blocked direction, and cable/contact telemetry. The full
ordered continuation is in [SC_CABLE_SNAG_RECOVERY_PLAN.md](SC_CABLE_SNAG_RECOVERY_PLAN.md).

The current expert data was generated by a CheatCode controller. It primarily
contains successful approach and insertion behavior. It does not contain enough
examples of backing away from a failed contact, changing direction, and trying
again. Consequently, reweighting the existing expert trajectories alone cannot
teach recovery.

## 1. Learning from failed trajectories

RL learns from failed trajectories primarily through rewards and a critic.

Suppose one transition contains:

```text
State:  plug is misaligned
Action: push forward
Result: force rises, plug barely moves, episode fails
```

The replay entry should preserve:

```text
observation before acting
complete executed action chunk
force/contact result
measured TCP and plug motion
next observation
reward
terminal status and terminal observation
```

The critic should learn a low value for pushing forward in this situation:

$$
Q(\text{misaligned contact},\ \text{push forward}) \rightarrow \text{low}.
$$

Candidate reward events include:

- A large positive reward for verified full insertion.
- A smaller positive reward when blocked contact is successfully cleared.
- A positive reward for improving alignment after clearance.
- A penalty for excessive force.
- A penalty for sustained commanded motion with little measured motion.
- A penalty for a detected cable snag.
- A penalty for timeout or an unrecovered terminal failure.

### Reward propagation

Consider a failed sequence:

```text
push while blocked       reward -1
push while blocked       reward -1
force becomes excessive  reward -3
episode ends             reward  0
```

Temporal-difference learning propagates the poor eventual result backward. The
critic can learn that the first push was a poor decision even though the larger
penalty arrived later.

Now compare it with a successful recovery:

```text
backtrack                reward  0
contact clears           reward +2
move laterally           reward +0.5
reapproach               reward +0.5
insert successfully      reward +10
```

The critic can learn that backtracking has high long-term value even though it
temporarily increases distance from the target.

### What a failed trajectory cannot teach by itself

A failed trajectory establishes that an action in a situation was poor. It
does not identify the correct alternative. The agent needs one of the following
sources for the alternative:

- Exploration that eventually produces a successful recovery.
- A scripted recovery controller.
- A recovery demonstration or intervention.

The reward should not penalize all contact because correct insertion naturally
involves contact. A better blocked-contact condition combines multiple signals:

```text
high force
+ meaningful forward command
+ very little measured plug/TCP motion
+ persistence for several measurements
```

## 2. Warm start and the role of offline training

There is no required algorithmic stage called "offline SERL." SERL is a robot
learning system that combines an off-policy RL method, prior data, rewards,
resets, and contact-compatible control. Its RLPD-style training can start online
while drawing each update from both stored prior data and new online data.

A typical balanced update is:

```text
50% prior/offline replay
50% newly collected online replay
```

The [RLPD paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02948) reports that this approach can
work without a separate offline RL pretraining stage. Its relevant ingredients
include balanced sampling, critic normalization, critic ensembles, and multiple
updates per environment step.

### Existing warm start

The current warm-start policy is the behavior-cloned transformer. It already
contains the basic approach, alignment, and insertion behavior. Offline RL is
not needed merely to produce an initial actor.

Substantial offline actor-critic training on the current CheatCode-only dataset
has limited value because:

- Nearly all retained trajectories demonstrate success.
- Recovery actions are missing.
- There is little success/failure contrast.
- The critic may assign unreliable values to actions outside the narrow dataset.
- Actor updates based on such a critic may damage useful BC behavior before
  online interaction begins.

### Recommended transition into online learning

#### Stage 1: preserve and validate BC

Save an immutable copy of the BC checkpoint. Validate it on the intended normal
episode starts and full insertion depth.

#### Stage 2: collect failures with a frozen policy

Run the frozen BC policy and retain complete examples of:

- Success.
- Misaligned contact.
- Excessive force.
- Cable snag.
- Timeout.
- The terminal observation before reset.

#### Stage 3: bounded offline critic warm-up

Train the critic briefly on verified successes plus frozen-policy failures.
Initially keep the actor fixed. The critic must demonstrate on held-out episodes
that it ranks successful progress above blocked pushing and high-force failure.
Discard or repair the critic initialization if this ranking is unreliable.

This is a critic initialization and validation stage rather than a large
offline policy-training stage.

#### Stage 4: online SERL/RLPD-style training

Initialize:

- The actor from BC.
- The critic from the validated warm-up, if it passes.
- The prior replay buffer from expert demonstrations and frozen-policy rollouts.
- A separate, initially empty online replay buffer.

Then begin interaction while sampling both buffers. This preserves a strong
initial actor without depending on an unvalidated offline critic.

## 3. Two recovery and exploration branches

### Branch A: correlated exploration

The learned actor is responsible for the entire recovery:

```text
detect blocked state
        |
choose a coherent retreat and reorientation trajectory
        |
clear contact
        |
retry
```

This is the more general approach, but discovering the full sequence may need
substantial interaction.

"Correlated" means that the four action waypoints form one physically coherent
maneuver. It avoids applying independent random noise to all 24 coordinates.

### Branch B: backtracking followed by near-lateral correlated exploration

A deterministic controller handles immediate clearance:

```text
policy follows a path
        |
blocked condition detected
        |
controller retraces recent path
        |
contact clears with sufficient margin
        |
RL policy explores mostly laterally
        |
policy reapproaches
```

This reduces the learned decision to: after the obstruction is cleared, which
nearby direction, orientation, and reapproach trajectory should be tried?

### Backtrack using measured motion

Maintain a rolling history such as:

```text
time   commanded pose   measured pose   force/contact
t-4    ...              ...             ...
t-3    ...              ...             ...
t-2    ...              ...             ...
t-1    ...              ...             ...
t      ...              ...             ...
```

The controller should reverse the recent **measured** path rather than merely
negating commands. During a collision, the robot may command 5 mm of motion and
physically move only 0.5 mm.

Execute the reversed path slowly and smoothly. End backtracking after all of
the following hold:

- Force falls below a lower clearance threshold.
- Blocked contact disappears.
- A minimum clearance distance has been traversed.
- Enough free space is available for lateral motion.

Use hysteresis: a higher threshold triggers backtracking and a lower threshold
confirms clearance. This prevents rapid mode switching due to noisy force.
Stop or modify the retreat if force increases while reversing.

### Detect blockage conservatively

A candidate trigger is either sustained high force,

$$
F > F_{\mathrm{high}}
\quad \text{for } k \text{ consecutive measurements},
$$

or a combination of commanded progress, motion disagreement, and persistent
contact:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{meaningful forward command} \\
&\land\ \text{very small measured forward motion} \\
&\land\ \text{persistent contact}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Cable snag detection may need more than TCP motion because the TCP can continue
moving while the cable stretches. Use observable cable motion or a simulator
cable-tension proxy for reward and training labels when available. Do not pass
privileged simulator signals to the deployed actor.

### Near-lateral exploration

Let $\mathbf u$ be the normalized direction in which the robot was moving when
it became blocked. Initial retreat follows approximately $-\mathbf u$.

Given an exploration direction $\mathbf v$, project it onto the plane
orthogonal to $\mathbf u$:

$$
\mathbf v_{\perp}
=
\left(I-\mathbf u\mathbf u^\top\right)\mathbf v.
$$

In plain language, this removes the component that points back into the
obstacle and retains the sideways components. Correlated exploration can then
produce a smooth maneuver:

```text
move sideways while clear
rotate slightly if useful
hold briefly
begin a cautious reapproach
```

Use a cone around the perpendicular plane instead of enforcing a perfectly
lateral direction. Cable recovery may require a small axial or vertical
component.

### Deployment state machine

```text
NORMAL_POLICY
      |
blocked trigger
      v
BACKTRACK
      |
clearance confirmed
      v
LATERAL_EXPLORATION
      |
safe reapproach
      v
NORMAL_POLICY
```

Provide the learned policy with the current mode and a compact summary of the
backtrack displacement and recent force/motion. This helps it interpret why the
robot is now farther from the target.

### Which segments train the actor?

If deployment always uses the deterministic controller during `BACKTRACK`, do
not train the actor to imitate those intermediate controller actions.

- Record every step for diagnostics and safety analysis.
- Mask hardcoded backtracking steps out of the actor loss.
- Begin policy-controlled RL transitions after clearance.
- Give the actor the post-backtrack observation and relevant recent history.
- Train it to select the lateral, reorientation, and reapproach trajectory.

Otherwise, replay would incorrectly claim that the actor selected an action
which was actually selected by the hardcoded controller.

A clean control boundary is:

```text
backtracking                 environment recovery mechanism
lateral search and retry     learned policy decisions
```

### Risks of hardcoded backtracking

- A cable can move during a snag, so exact path reversal may not unwind it.
- A false blockage trigger can interrupt valid insertion contact.
- Too little retreat leaves insufficient room for lateral motion.
- Too much retreat wastes time and can create a new cable configuration.
- The deployment state machine must be validated for each task family.

### Branch recommendation

Evaluate both branches, beginning with Branch B:

1. Backtracking plus near-lateral correlated exploration.
2. Fully learned correlated recovery.

Branch B should generate successful recoveries sooner. Those trajectories can
later help Branch A learn a complete recovery policy.

Compare:

- Recovery success rate.
- Full insertion success after recovery.
- Peak force.
- Backtrack distance.
- Number of retries.
- Time to clear contact.
- Results by failure type.
- False backtracking during valid insertion.

## 4. Algorithm comparison

### PPO

PPO is on-policy:

```text
collect a fresh rollout
update from that rollout
discard or greatly limit later reuse
repeat
```

This makes extensive reuse of expert demonstrations and failed trajectories
awkward. Robot interaction is expensive, so this is a poor primary fit for the
current goal.

### TD3

TD3 uses a deterministic actor, twin critics, a replay buffer, external action
noise, and delayed actor updates. It fits the deterministic transformer
mechanically. Exploration around one action can struggle to discover distinct
behaviors such as pushing, retreating, moving around a card, and retrying. A
critic error can also pull the actor toward incorrectly valued action chunks.
See the [TD3 paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/fujimoto18a.html).

### SAC

SAC uses a stochastic actor, twin critics, replay, and an entropy objective:

```text
TD3: choose the single action currently believed best
SAC: retain and sample several plausible actions while favoring reward
```

This supports online discovery of alternative recovery behavior. See the
[SAC paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v80/haarnoja18b.html).

A single independent Gaussian over 24 raw action coordinates would be too
crude for the present trajectory. A better stochastic actor would sample a
low-dimensional maneuver or a mixture of complete coherent trajectories:

```text
continue
retreat-left
retreat-right
retreat-rotate
gentle reapproach
```

The transformer should continue to output a complete trajectory rather than a
residual correction added to another policy's action.

### RLPD

RLPD is a recipe for using offline data during online off-policy RL. Its
reference implementation builds on SAC. It keeps prior demonstrations and new
online experience in separate buffers, samples both during every update, and
uses critic design choices intended to limit value divergence.

```text
BC and expert experience
        +
online successes and failures
        |
        v
continuous actor-critic improvement
```

The [RLPD paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02948) found that balanced sampling
and critic regularization matter; simply placing all old data in an ordinary
SAC buffer was less reliable.

### SERL

SERL is the surrounding robotic system. It combines:

- RLPD-style off-policy learning.
- Image observations.
- Demonstration replay.
- Reward implementations.
- Automatic resets.
- Separate actor and learner processes.
- Contact-compatible low-level control.

Original SERL trained PCB insertion and cable routing from 20 demonstrations
and online interaction, and reported correction and recovery behavior. See the
[SERL paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16013).

### IQL and AWAC

IQL is useful for extracting behavior from a fixed dataset while limiting
unsupported action evaluation. AWAC weights recorded actions by estimated
advantage and was designed for prior-data initialization followed by online
improvement. See the [AWAC paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.09359).

The current data lacks recovery actions, so these methods have limited ability
to improve recovery offline. AWAC becomes more relevant after online
exploration has generated successful recovery trajectories.

## 5. Recommended experiment

Use a SERL/RLPD-style SAC system initialized from the current BC transformer:

```text
BC transformer
      |
frozen-policy success/failure collection
      |
optional critic-only offline warm-up
      |
online RLPD/SAC
  - prior replay
  - online replay
  - structured trajectory distribution
      |
compare
  A. correlated exploration
  B. hardcoded backtracking
     + near-lateral correlated exploration
```

For Branch B, the deterministic controller handles reliable clearance.
SAC/RLPD learns the near-lateral direction, reorientation, reapproach, and retry
behavior.

This meets the two main requirements:

1. **Warm start:** initialize the actor from the BC transformer and continue
   sampling prior replay so that online training retains the basic skill.
2. **Smooth reward-driven improvement:** continuously incorporate new failures
   and successful recoveries while using structured stochastic exploration.

## 6. What was implemented

The implementation is in
`aic_utils/aic_isaac/aic_isaaclab/scripts/serl/measured_path_recovery.py`.
It stores the measured TCP path, reverses that path during clearance, requires
both force and stalled measured motion to declare a blockage, uses separate
trigger and clearance thresholds, and projects the first post-clearance
exploration commands onto the plane near perpendicular to the blocked motion.

The first live version exposed two useful failures:

1. Force by itself fired during ordinary insertion contact.
2. A safety abort remained active for the rest of an episode.

The detector now requires a meaningful command, little realized motion, and
high force for consecutive decisions. The abort is a short safety hold and
then permits lateral recovery. A deliberately sensitive smoke test retraced
2.34 mm. Controller-owned actions entered critic replay but produced exactly
zero actor gradient, which verifies the ownership mask.

In the revised five-episode stochastic comparison, no episode met the full
force plus stall trigger. The dominant failures were lateral drift and timeout,
so backtracking could not correct them. This is a valid negative result: the
controller is a response to actual blockage, not a general replacement for
alignment control. It must not be tuned to fire on normal contact merely to
increase its activation count.
