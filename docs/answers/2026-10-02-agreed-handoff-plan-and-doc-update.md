## The plan in order

1. **Keep a frozen comparison.** Preserve the current all-candidate port locator, transport stopping rules, and insertion actor. Use the same episode-grouped development scenes, including the known wrong-SFP-port and unsafe SC-route examples. Do not open the reserved final scenes.
2. **Make transport propose a pause near the requested destination.** Repair or replace its existing arrival head using images, measured robot state, task code, and causal history. Its output means **“hold and inspect,” not “start insertion.”** Test whether it rejects the known false stops while still finding valid arrivals. If transport cannot reliably reach and recognize the correct neighborhood, fix that before attributing failures to perception.
3. **Create real visibility labels.** For both SFP-to-NIC and SC-to-SC, label whether the **requested opening** can actually be identified in each of the three images, including cable/card occlusion and wrong-port distractors. Audit rendered/derived labels visually. The existing projected-in-frame labels are insufficient.
4. **Train one selected-port pose/visibility model.** Input: close-up RGB, measured TCP pose, and the public task code. Output: the **requested port's pose or invalid**, plus per-camera identifiable visibility. Train on the same image with different target requests and on cases where another port is visible but the requested one is hidden. Use original-resolution crops selected from RGB. **Do not train a separate confidence head now.** Compare this model against the frozen all-candidate baseline on the same held-out near-target scenes, measuring wrong-port acceptance and accepted pose error.
5. **Test the two-stage handoff without insertion.** When transport proposes arrival, hold the measured TCP, run fresh selected-port perception, and accept or reject the proposal using target identity, usable views, pose stability, safe waiting position/orientation, route, and force/contact. Test known false stops and new episode-grouped development starts. The 10 mm / 3° arrival criterion is scored after the run against truth, not read from the simulator at runtime. Require complete inference p95 below 300 ms.
6. **Attach and repair the insertion actor.** Once the handoff is reliable, fix the actor's initial retreat on its own near-port states. It should use the vision-estimated TCP-in-port pose and predict complete port-frame targets. Evaluate whole autonomous Gazebo episodes from normal SFP and SC starts, reporting full insertion, wrong-target events, contact/force, and latency. Teacher-assisted starts are kept separate from autonomous results.
7. **Only then advance recovery and RL.** After reliable supervised full insertion, test recovery from force, misalignment, and cable blockage, with hierarchical backtracking as a later controlled option. World-model and Seer directions remain parked.

**Current status:** The integrated switch has not passed. The existing SC visual stop made five valid and one false handoff on seven saved routes; the transport-only head made two valid and two false, missing three arrivals. SFP still lacks a validated observation-only stop, and a wrong-port estimate remains a known failure. True visibility labels and calibrated confidence do not exist in the current checkpoints; learned confidence is now explicitly deferred. Final scenes remain sealed.
