# Requested-card visual gate and active-view pilot

I tried the proposed visual identifiability check, followed by one bounded camera-view diagnostic.

I froze **40** original-RGB, three-camera panels covering all ten SFP requests, route/approach/near stages, saved autonomous starts, and known wrong-neighbor cases. Eleven panels have formal annotations saved **before** consulting simulator projections; I could not confidently name the requested numbered card and opening in any of those eleven. The other 29 were inspected as unmarked images, but their per-item annotations were not frozen before the truth file was opened for the initial eleven, so I do **not** claim a blinded 40-item accuracy score. Several panels show identical-looking card faces; early views sometimes show the board backside, and 30 mm of earlier motion often adds little identity information. This is an observation limit in the reviewed views, not proof that a trained model cannot solve every scene.

I then tested a distinct, guarded view on a reused **nonfinal** card-1/port-0 scene. Starting 180 mm from the port, the privileged diagnostic rose 40 mm and moved toward the opposite side of the row by 80 mm, then returned. Measured motion was −77.8 mm sideways and +38.9 mm upward; the return was within 0.3 mm of baseline. There was no force/contact guard abort. The ordinary privileged CheatCode continuation inserted and scored tier 3 = 75. That score belongs to the teacher diagnostic, **not** to our autonomous actor.

The new view failed the actual purpose of the test: it showed **less** of the card row, and two cameras still did not reveal a reliable numbered-slot reference and requested port side. I stopped after the predeclared one-scene pilot. I did not train another identity head or enable handoff.

Transport is a separate failed prerequisite. Its latest matched autonomous comparison remains **0/3 safe buffered arrivals**; closest lateral offsets in the requested axial band were 23.3, 31.6 and 14.4 mm, against the 10 mm limit. Paired-data training improved held-out action error but the actor's early actions changed very little when only the requested card/port code changed. That points toward weak target-specific routing, not a proven insertion-lip failure.

**Next bounded experiment:** Test one observation posture that exposes a *fixed board/slot reference while preserving the full card row*—for example, a guarded wrist reorientation at the existing safe standoff, if a geometry and collision feasibility check permits it. Predeclare at most two reused nonfinal pilots, the current 5 N/35 N force guards and 5 mm return tolerance, and stop unless the requested numbered opening is identifiable from at least two unmarked cameras in both scenes. Only then gather new grouped training/development views for an association head. In parallel, keep the existing transport actor frozen and audit its early task-code action sensitivity against same-board teacher route differences. A spatial transport fit must wait: its earlier preregistered identity prerequisite still fails (3/10 early anchors on hard scenes), and the latest autonomous actor is 0/3 at safe arrival. Keep final scenes sealed and insertion/RL disabled until both transport and visual identity gates pass.
