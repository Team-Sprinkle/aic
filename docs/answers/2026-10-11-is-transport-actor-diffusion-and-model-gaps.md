# Is the transport actor diffusion-based? Could the model cause the failures?

**Short answer:** The actor in the recent SFP actor-to-teacher and route tests is **not a diffusion model**. It is a deterministic, behavior-cloned Transformer that emits four complete TCP-relative target poses at a time. Model design plausibly contributes to poor route selection, but the evidence does not isolate architecture as the sole cause. The actor also encounters states unlike teacher-only training, and safe teacher continuations have not yet supplied causally clean, cable-safe labels at every actor boundary.

## Which model actually ran?

The October 10 rollout [plan](/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-10_sfp_stage_labels_card4_port0_v1/plan.json) names `/var/tmp/chmin_aic_20260918_act/sfp_history_context_live_20261007/corrected.pt` as the temporal actor. Its checkpoint says `history_enabled=True`, `feature_dim=1536`, 17 sparse history ages up to 60 s, and `route_conditioning=False`, `route_goal_conditioning=False`, `spatial_request_attention=False`, `stage_aux_loss=0`, and `handoff_aux_loss=0`. I read these values from the checkpoint, not only from a status note. The rollout also loads an older shared-port checkpoint for normalizers and runtime setup; the temporal model overrides its action prediction. Its source is `scripts/train_temporal_transport_probe.py` and runtime is `aic_example_policies/aic_example_policies/ros/RunTemporalTransport.py`.

At a decision, it encodes the **current** center/left/right 224×224 images with a frozen ImageNet ResNet-18, globally pools each image to a 512D vector, and combines the three vectors with current measured 32D robot state, public 10D task code (family, selected card/port), prior proposed chunk, measured TCP motion, image ages, and selected past feature vectors. A two-layer, 256D temporal Transformer outputs one **36D** vector: four 9D complete TCP-body pose targets. The low-level controller receives bounded target updates at 50 ms spacing; the actor replans at the next chunk. It has no diffusion denoising loop or sampled action mode. The visual backbone is about **11.18M parameters** and the temporal head about **1.59M**, roughly **12.77M total**. The older direct `SharedPortTCPActor` is about **13.61M** parameters and also deterministic. Neither is the original full ACT conditional VAE, although both use the idea of action chunks.

The trained pose estimator, native-image refiner, and shadow camera verifier are **not an effective closed-loop route input to this checkpoint**. In the latest checkpoint, route-goal and spatial-request switches are off, and its action-only head does not use the predicted stage/subgoal. It therefore has to infer the requested route implicitly from the compressed image features, task code, state, and history. The saved complete target actions are in the TCP/body frame, not absolute base coordinates or residual corrections. Code and frame audits found no sampled target-frame conversion error in this path; that does not certify all runtime behavior.

## Was diffusion tried?

Yes, as a separate **transport probe**, not the deployed actor. `scripts/train_diffusion_transport_probe.py` used the same frozen visual-feature cache and 17-point history, a 2-layer context Transformer and 3-layer action denoiser, 100 training noise levels and 16 inference steps. Its checkpoint was selected at update 2,250 of 3,000. On the matched held-out teacher sample, first-target translation p95 was **12.71 mm SFP / 10.96 mm SC**; the deterministic long-history head was **2.62 / 2.27 mm**. Even a privileged best-of-four diffusion-sample diagnostic remained far worse than the deterministic head. The diffusion head plus shared ResNet-18 had about **14.66M** parameters. A warmed local latency test gave **39.9 ms p95**, excluding ROS/controller scheduling. The diffusion model failed its offline gate and **did not receive a live autonomous Gazebo transport rollout**. These are the results of this implementation, split and 3,000-update budget; they do **not** rule out diffusion generally. See the [five-way comparison](../experiments/2026-10-01-transport-five-way-comparison.md) and saved `outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_transport_five_way/diffusion/{manifest,summary}.json`.

## Model gaps, ranked against our evidence

| Gap | Why it matters here | Evidence / limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Requested-card grounding is weak** | Selecting card 0 versus card 1 can demand a different route around obstacles. A deterministic head can learn a common motion that looks good on ordinary teacher frames while missing this branch. | On saved same-image request swaps, the spatial-attention candidate changed the first action by only **0.903 mm median**, versus **12.44 mm** separation between paired teacher routes at their later branches. The exact later observations differ, so 12.44 mm is a route-scale reference, not a valid same-state action label. [Spatial test](../experiments/2026-10-07-sfp-spatial-request-transport.md). |
| **Current images lose spatial layout** | Global average pooling discards where a card edge or cable loop lies in a camera. Past images are stored only as pooled vectors, so the long history cannot recover detailed position if the encoder discarded it. | A 4×5 per-camera spatial-grid attention ablation improved held-out first-action p95 by only **1.9%** and did not materially improve request sensitivity. This particular frozen-grid addition failed; it does not prove all spatial designs fail. [Spatial test](../experiments/2026-10-07-sfp-spatial-request-transport.md). |
| **No explicit route/progress state in the selected head** | Four 50 ms targets are a short local command horizon for a route that goes around a row. The selected head has no trained long-range goal, stage or handoff objective controlling those commands. | A separate stage/subgoal branch was tried, but its held-out complete subgoal error was **44.46 mm p95**, above its 20 mm gate. The representation/labels were inadequate for promotion. [Five-way comparison](../experiments/2026-10-01-transport-five-way-comparison.md). |
| **Off-policy history shift** | Training history contains previous *teacher* targets; runtime history contains the actor's own proposed chunks. A small early error changes later camera views and prior-action tokens. | The code makes this substitution explicitly; autonomous runs left the route despite low teacher-frame action error. This is a plausible mechanism, not an isolated causal finding. [Temporal record](../experiments/2026-10-01-temporal-three-model-continuation.md), [DAgger paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a/ross11a.pdf). |
| **Weak conditioning on predicted pose** | A useful port estimator cannot improve transport if the action head ignores its pose input. | Matched pose-head fits changed predicted actions by only **0.038–0.044 mm p95** when the pose input was zeroed. Resetting the decoder worsened validation. Pose acceptance at the first corrective decision existed in only **3/6 train** and **1/3 development** takeovers. [Pose-use diagnostic](../experiments/2026-10-08-sfp-pose-actor-training-method.md). |
| **Single deterministic output can average routes** | If two genuinely valid detours exist for the *same* observation and request, MSE regression can predict an unsafe intermediate command. | This is a model risk, not yet demonstrated as the main failure. Many apparently different routes correspond to different requested ports or changed observations, which should be disambiguated by conditioning. Diffusion or a discrete route-mode head could represent alternatives but cannot choose the safe one without visual/force grounding and appropriate labels. [Diffusion Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04137), [Behavior Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11251). |

There are two important non-model limits. First, several teacher continuations do not give a clean action target at the exact actor takeover: six cached runs moved more than 5 mm before the later sustained-motion segment. Second, an officially successful new teacher route later produced a sampled **55.52 N cable/NIC-card point contact**, so full insertion is not enough to certify a route as cable-safe. The [October 10 label audit](../experiments/2026-10-10-sfp-stage-aware-transport-label-gate.md) preserves both facts. These make a simple architecture swap on the same labels a poor first repair.

## What the papers suggest, and what I would test

[ACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.13705) supports action chunking and overlapping temporal ensembling for smoother control, but our four-step deterministic actor does not include ACT's conditional VAE or temporal ensemble. [Diffusion Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04137) shows diffusion can model multimodal action sequences with receding-horizon control. [Behavior Transformer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.11251) offers a lighter way to model discrete behavior modes. [DAgger](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a/ross11a.pdf) directly addresses the distribution shift caused by a policy visiting states absent from demonstrations. Recent [Spatially Conditioned Diffusion Policy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14535) also emphasizes spatial features along proposed trajectories; the transfer of its single-camera manipulation result to our three wrist views and long cable route is unproven. These are design clues, not evidence that any one method wins on AIC.

My priority is a **matched diagnostic**, not an immediate switch to diffusion: (1) on existing held-out actor traces, locate the first point where the chosen card-side route diverges; test whether the frozen features plus task code can predict the correct route class/short safe waypoint before fitting actions; (2) if that fails, build labels and spatial observation support for the *requested* route, including causal actor-visited states, and compare a small discrete route-mode/waypoint head with the current deterministic head on the same groups; (3) only if the input and route labels are sound and true multimodality remains should we revisit diffusion, with the same features, split, target representation, budget, inference cap and live gate. A diffusion win must be measured as safer autonomous arrivals, not only attractive samples or teacher-frame loss. Keep final scenes sealed and the existing handoff/insertion/RL gates in force.
