# Transport actor and handoff continuation — October 5

I collected 30 new SFP same-board alternate-request teacher runs across one to five installed cards. **29 fully inserted**; the one partial insertion was excluded from expert labels. The successful runs added **7,584 complete TCP-body action chunks**. The training set now has **326 SFP episodes**, including **35 same-board request pairs**, plus the existing **83 SC episodes**. All five card counts and all ten SFP card/port request codes appear, but coverage is still uneven: **235/326** SFP episodes request card 0/port 1. Successful teacher routes also do not cover off-route actor views, occlusion and contact recovery, so this is not exhaustive scenario coverage.

The corrected action-only transport actor reduced held-out first-action translation p95 from **2.717 to 2.153 mm** for SFP and from **2.240 to 1.987 mm** for SC. That offline gain did **not** turn into reliable autonomous transport. On three new, independent one/two-card starts, the old and new actors both reached the requested port's safe buffered approach on **0/3** runs. The new actor came closer in two runs and avoided one scored contact, but its closest lateral offsets *while in the correct axial band* were **23.26, 31.58 and 14.40 mm**; the required corridor is **≤10 mm**. Actor inference p95 was about **99–102 ms** on these runs. The new actor was **not promoted**.

I trained the handoff output separately while keeping the action weights frozen. With the strict rule of a sigmoid score ≥0.95 for three consecutive decisions, the added data left it at **2/10 timely teacher handoffs** and **8/10 misses**. Lower thresholds produced early stops. SC handoff labels last only one or two decisions, so they cannot train or validate that three-decision rule as written. Automatic learned stopping remains **disabled**.

The new location rule is in the runtime: a learned handoff requires a train-fitted, task-specific TCP region in robot base coordinates, with a 60 mm margin. It **vetoes** proposals outside the plausible port neighborhood; it never creates a proposal or verifies the right port. It retained every labeled held-out safe-standoff sample but remained broad, including much of one previously failed autonomous route. The evaluation launcher now passes and hashes the fitted region file when learned stopping is enabled.

I also tested a route-conditioned head, a task-selected complete-action head, and extra training weight on the early paired route decision. Each failed its declared gate. At 0.4/0.6/1.0 s, the final early-weighted head's median action spread across all ten request codes was only **1.166 mm**, below the 3 mm diagnostic threshold. The next targeted experiment is a **spatial, request-conditioned visual representation** that identifies the requested card/port region before choosing the route. More generic teacher runs or RL would not address the current observed bottleneck. No final scenes were opened, no RL ran, and the existing Isaac job was left alone.
