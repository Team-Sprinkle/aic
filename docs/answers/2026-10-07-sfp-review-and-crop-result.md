# SFP transport and coarse-perception follow-up

I continued the agreed bottleneck investigation on saved, nonfinal data. **Neither blocker is resolved.** The transport actor previously reached **0/3** safe buffered arrivals on new sparse-card starts. The hard requested-port acquisition check reached only **3/10** early correct cases. A reliable insertion handoff needs both.

I found a limitation in the earlier visual review: its 40 unmarked RGB panels contained task code and camera history but omitted measured TCP pose. That pose is a public observation the deployable coarse checker can use. The 11 blind abstentions from that packet therefore cannot establish that the *complete* observation lacks enough information. I prepared a disjoint 30-panel follow-up with current/history images from all three cameras, task request, measured current/earlier TCP pose, and the fixed CAD slot-order convention. No true per-scene target geometry is in the reviewer form. A second independent reviewer has **not** scored it, so there is no new human identity-accuracy number. Packet: `/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-07_sfp_blind_review_followup_v1/`.

I tested one fixed image-crop hypothesis before any training: use the frozen RGB rail prediction to center a **320 px** original-image crop instead of **480 px**, hoping to exclude neighboring cards while keeping the requested opening. Simulator projections were used only for *post hoc scoring*, never to choose the crop. On the same ten grouped development scenes, the 320 px crop gave an early, clean two-camera view in **6/10** scenes; 480 px gave **3/10**. “Clean” means the requested opening is inside and other same-port card openings are outside both crops. The predeclared continuation gate was **9/10**, so I stopped this branch without fitting a model or checking the newer ten-scene set. The selected opening was inside two 320 px crops in 9/10 scenes, but this alone does not identify it. Results: `/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-07_sfp_crop_feasibility_v1/`.

I also checked the prior nominal-coordinate idea against its existing audit rather than repeating it. Its development median port error was **44.6 mm**, and it caused false autonomous stops, so it cannot replace visual identity. No GPU training, Gazebo collection, actor change, handoff, insertion, or RL was started in this follow-up. The four reserved final configurations remain sealed.

**Next decision:** independently score the observation-complete 30-panel review. If reviewers can identify the requested card/port from public observations, use that evidence to specify a bounded full-row slot-association model with complete-reset splits and a zero-wrong-port acceptance gate. If they cannot, revise the observation or route so a board-origin cue becomes visible before attempting another identity fit. In parallel, the transport actor still needs a distinct targeted route-branching diagnostic; more ordinary action-BC updates without an explicit route/target hypothesis are not justified by its 0/3 live arrivals. Do not enable the handoff or insertion actor until both gates pass.
