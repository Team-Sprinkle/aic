# Why is the transport-to-insertion gate 10 mm?

The **10 mm lateral / approximately 70–100 mm axial standoff / 3° orientation** rule is a conservative *experiment gate* for a planned near-port handoff. It is **not a measured physical limit** of the port, nor proof that the current PoseInsert-style actor succeeds whenever the rule passes. My previous answer should not be read as saying that a 19 mm lateral miss is intrinsically too far for a pose-conditioned controller to correct.

The concern has two separate parts:

1. **Can vision identify the requested opening and estimate its pose?** Sometimes yes, even before the TCP reaches the narrow buffer. The original-image selected-port crop results on clear development views show useful position estimates. But neighboring ports, occlusions, and wrong card identity can yield plausible yet incorrect predictions. The visual verifier must reject those cases; a small predicted distance or good three-view agreement alone does not certify the right port. A wrong-port estimate is a separate failure from being 19 mm off the correct port.
2. **Can the local actor use that pose to correct its own position and orientation, avoid contact, and insert?** Expressing actions in the estimated port frame helps describe the desired motion, but it does not supply a controller or guarantee behavior outside the starts represented in its training data. The current actor has not demonstrated a broad correction range or reliable full insertion. In a controlled SFP start about **36 mm before the opening and 1.45 mm laterally off**, the visual pose error was only about **0.11 mm lateral, 0.28 mm axial, and 0.086°**, yet the actor advanced with about **20 mm lateral error** at its furthest axial point and scored no insertion. A matched frame-label repair improved held-out near-port target error only modestly and did not pass its gate. [Local actor diagnosis](../experiments/2026-10-02-two-family-perception-local-bc.md#october-2-immediate-lock-failure-and-matched-actor-frame-repair).

Thus the strict gate is **mainly an unvalidated local-policy capture-range and safety precaution**, with correct-port identity and orientation as additional independent requirements. It does not imply that PoseInsert cannot recover from 19, 30, or 40 mm. Those starts simply have not been shown to work with the current trained actor; 10 mm itself has not been shown sufficient either. The roughly **0.5 mm lateral** final insertion corridor is a *different* requirement at the opening, after the approach correction.

The better design is to let coarse perception propose an inspection whenever the requested port can be identified safely, even at a larger lateral offset. Then use a fresh precise pose and measured TCP to estimate axial/lateral/orientation error. A correction-capable pose-conditioned approach policy could take over at 20–40 mm **if a measured capture-range test supports it**. For this test, hold the perception and policy fixed, start from checked safe standoffs spanning lateral offsets such as 2, 5, 10, 20, and 40 mm plus orientation variants, and measure full insertion and contact by family and port. Include wrong-port and hidden-port negatives in the verifier test. Only then choose a handoff envelope from actual success/force curves rather than retaining or widening the arbitrary 10 mm number by intuition.

The October 13 12/6 transport reached SFP median closest lateral miss **19.07 mm** across just three scenes, but no safe buffer or insertion; SC reached the approach band in zero of three scenes. That result makes a wider visual *inspection* worth testing, not a direct switch to the existing insertion actor.
