# SFP route continuation: result and next gate

I worked through the route-target and real-cable checks from the [six-step plan](2026-10-09-next-steps-after-handoff-diagnosis.md). The first two steps produced useful evidence, but **the safety and actor-state-label gates did not pass**, so I did not train a new actor or connect automatic handoff, insertion or RL.

The frozen selected-port perception found the requested port at all six saved actor takeovers. The previous two-second history limit missed one useful pair of views; using measured robot travel to bound the pairing recovered it. On these six reused scenes, an 85 mm approach goal made from the visual port position and a fixed vertical board-normal prior was within 0.9–1.3 mm laterally after posthoc scoring. This is a promising **offline target**, not proof that a robot can safely drive there or that the prior handles every randomized orientation.

I then replayed three preselected, privileged MoveIt transport routes in Gazebo with the flexible cable. The two- and three-card routes reached the buffer within 4.36 and 3.58 mm. The five-card route stalled during descent: measured TCP height barely changed while command height kept falling, and force change climbed to 12.64 N. Its [five-camera video](</home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-09_sfp_route_observability_v1/gazebo_case3_retreat/video/all_views_20fps.mp4>) shows cable passing over a card. The official score reported no prohibited contact, but that score does not identify all cable contacts.

I ran a bounded contact-instrumented diagnosis of the five-card scene. The first repeat reached the buffer from a different actor takeover and recorded cable/task-board contact. Of the next three starts, one reproduced a **high-force cable/NIC-card interference**: sampled cable/card point force reached about 98 N, wrist-force change reached 12.2 N, and the TCP ended 10.44 mm from the buffer goal. The other two reached within 4.37 and 3.05 mm with low wrist force. None exactly matched the original failed takeover, so the original contact pair remains unproven, but the failure mechanism clearly recurs under this route. The [high-force contact-enabled video](</home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-09_sfp_route_observability_v1/gazebo_case3_oracle_repeat_1/video/all_views_20fps.mp4>) and the successful [two-card](</home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-09_sfp_route_observability_v1/gazebo_case0_align/video/all_views_20fps.mp4>) and [three-card](</home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-09_sfp_route_observability_v1/gazebo_case1_retreat/video/all_views_20fps.mp4>) recordings are saved. These routes used privileged planning for diagnosis; they were **not autonomous policy success** and did not attempt insertion.

The next bounded repair is to test at most **two** cable-aware side-clearance route variants against the current retreat route, with a force budget declared before testing. Each candidate gets at most three new contact-instrumented, nonfinal five-card starts; it must achieve at least 2/3 buffered arrivals without a high-force stall or unacceptable cable/NIC-card contact. If neither passes, stop route-actor work and revise planning. Then stress the visual buffer goal on new grouped scenes and gather only route labels with executed safe continuations: at least 20 distinct training reset states and six held-out states, with at least 5/6 held-out correction directions agreeing with safe measured movement. Only after those gates pass should the matched route-aware actor be trained and tested on new autonomous 2/3/5-card starts. The reserved final scenes remain sealed.
