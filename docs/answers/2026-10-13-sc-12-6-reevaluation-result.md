# SC-to-SC 12/6 reevaluation: what failed?

I reran the frozen 12-predict/6-execute transport actor on the same three nonfinal SC scenes, then tested three previously unused development scenes: one, two, and five cards, covering both target ports. All six matching expert teachers fully inserted without scored contact. The actor reached **0/6 approach bands, 0/6 safe handoff buffers, and 0/6 insertions**, also with **no scored contact**. Combined with the original three runs, this is nine failures on six distinct configurations; three are repeats, not independent new scenes. No final scene was opened and no new model was trained.

The clearest result is in the [measured axial-progress plot](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-13_sc_horizon12_reeval_v1/axial_progress.png). The black dashed expert paths first travel around the cards, spend time roughly 150 mm before the port, then make a final advance. The actor partly follows the sideways route but misses that last transition. It begins to depart from the expert TCP path around **6–9 simulation seconds** and never enters the −100 to −70 mm axial approach band. At its closest point to the requested port, its **TCP** is still about **160–266 mm away in 3D** across the six reruns.

This is **not a demonstrated cable snag or blocked insertion** in these trials. Both one-card and five-card actor runs failed the same upstream approach, no official contact was scored, and sampled five-camera frames do not show a sustained gripper/card or cable block. [One-card actor video](/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-13_route_horizon12_execute6_sc_newdev_v1/rollouts/llb_0090/five_cameras_10fps.mp4) · [five-card actor video](/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-13_route_horizon12_execute6_sc_repeat_v1/rollouts/llb_0074/five_cameras_10fps.mp4) · [successful teacher on the same five-card scene](</data1/chmin/yj/ws_aic/src/aic/outputs/experiments/2026-09-30_sc_llb_collection/continuation170/runs/llb_0074/five_cameras_10fps.mp4>).

**Why it appears to fail:** once the expert would turn into the final approach, the model keeps requesting Cartesian targets away from the opening. In the six new runs, **2,176 of 2,177** issued commands after 40 s targeted an axial position farther away than the measured TCP at that command. The controller did sometimes move differently while tracking these targets, but the references show the policy's decision. This is an incorrect route-phase/progress behavior, not a need for fine insertion correction.

Two other contributors were measured. The model was trained on a 300 ms decision history, but in live Gazebo its decisions were typically **550–600 ms apart**, including a **250–300 ms gap** after each six-command chunk while the next inference ran. And it strongly depends on its previous command: on two frozen early SC traces, replaying the saved images/state/task reproduced the logged first action exactly; replacing only the previous *actor* command with the previous *expert* command reduced a representative next-action gap from **15.2 to 2.5 mm** in one scene and **8.3 to 2.6 mm** in another. The model saw previous expert actions in most training contexts, but its own actions at deployment. This is strong evidence of a feedback shortcut, though the time-matched expert counterfactual is approximate once the routes diverge. It does not prove that this alone causes every late failure.

The task code was present and selected the right requested port in the trace; both SC port codes are represented in the training data. Saved-input replay matched the live model output, arguing against a sampler/frame-conversion mistake on those checked decisions. These observations do **not** prove that the compressed visual backbone always recognizes the correct port internally.

**Decision:** 12/6 remains an exploratory SFP candidate, but it is **not a successful SC transport actor**. The next SC repair should target route progress on actor-visited states and the training/runtime command-history mismatch, then test complete autonomous arrival again. Switching to the precise perception or insertion actor cannot rescue these rollouts yet because transport never reaches a usable approach.
