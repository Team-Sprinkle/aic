# Spatial transport follow-up

I continued with the agreed spatial transport experiment's first gate. I replayed two frozen, request-conditioned RGB port locators on **110 three-camera observations from three saved, autonomous one/two-card routes**. They each found the correct requested port within three seconds on **3/3 episodes**, with **zero accepted wrong-port predictions**. The selected-port model accepted 38/110 triplets (3.00 mm accepted 3D p95); the structured-slot model accepted 64/110 (4.23 mm p95). These are coarse transport-scale errors, not insertion accuracy. Simulator port locations were used only to score the saved replay.

The same transport actor still reached the safe requested-port approach region on **0/3** of those starts. So the ports were visually localizable on these sparse routes, but the actor did not reliably use that information to navigate. This is a useful diagnosis, not a successful policy result.

I did **not** train or deploy the spatial actor yet. The agreed prerequisite was broader than these three easy cases: the earlier ten-request, harder held-out replay obtained only **3/10** early selected-port anchors, and an earlier five-card autonomous crop path accepted a target on only **1/3** routes. Training an actor on these imperfect, intermittently missing target cues would obscure whether the failure was visual identity or control. I inspected an unmarked four-card failure frame; part of the board row is clipped, making the requested slot's ordinal reference ambiguous in that view. That supports a specific slot-identity/observation-coverage repair, though it does not prove the target is always hidden.

The harder-case audit is more specific: **none of the six card-1/2/3 requests** got an early anchor, although the requested opening projected into all three image frames on every one of their seven early samples. Projection alone does not prove visibility, but an unmarked card-2 example shows all five card faces in the center image. Its detector emitted high in-frame scores while the triangulated point was **26 mm** from the requested opening and failed the acceptance rule. That is evidence for a slot identity or cross-camera correspondence problem, alongside the separate route-control failure.
