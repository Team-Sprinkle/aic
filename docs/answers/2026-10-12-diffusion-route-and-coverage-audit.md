# Diffusion transport: training gaps, teacher route, and start coverage

Date: 2026-10-12. This audits the latest public-anchor coverage retrain. It does not claim the reserved final scenes were opened.

## 1. What is wrong with the diffusion model experiment?

The strongest direct result is a **teacher-observation versus live-rollout gap**. On recorded teacher observations, the new checkpoint's median first-action error in the new SC near-transport region improved from 4.8 to 1.0 mm. In autonomous tests it reached the requested safe transport buffer on **0/3 SFP and 0/3 SC** development starts. Its lateral miss in the target axial band was 16.7–26.2 mm on SFP and 29.4–68.4 mm on SC. Thus fitting demonstrated actions did not make the actor recover when its own earlier actions put it off the demonstrated path. The older deterministic transformer also scored 0/3 for both families on those starts, although the diffusion actor generally got closer. This comparison does not isolate architecture because the checkpoints saw different data/objectives.

There are several distinct gaps:

1. **SC route labels conflict.** The older 83 SC training episodes use the planned Low Lateral Bypass (LLB). The 22 newly admitted SC training prefixes came from official CheatCode. Both were pooled under one actor, with the new SC rows assigned 25% of SC sampling mass. Route style was not an actor input. This can create ambiguous behavior where the same task demands different paths. It is a plausible contributor to drift, not a proven sole cause.
2. **The new SC collection did not enforce the desired cable route.** It accepted prefixes for measured TCP safe-buffer arrival, low force, no scored contact, and command tracking. It did not measure whether the orange cable stayed below card-top height or beside the row. The five-card `sc_trial3_train_04` side frames at 10 and 20 seconds show a raised cable segment around card-top height while approaching; the three-card exact-anchor frames also show a raised loop. The sampled views cannot prove every cable segment crosses over a card in 3D, but they cannot verify the requested never-over-cards property. Preserve these as **CheatCode transport diagnostics**, not verified LLB experts.
3. **Sparse new-layout examples.** Only 16 new SFP and 22 new SC episodes entered training. The general SC set has two scenes per card-count/selected-port combination in its full 20-scene plan, split between training and development; the extra eight scenes all use one exact three-card, port-1 layout. The SFP addition has only two requested card/port codes. The full union has 602 training episodes, but this count hides the thin coverage at the new board orientations.
4. **Training was bounded, not saturated.** The coverage fit stopped at its declared 6,000-update cap, and its best validation score occurred at update 6,000 (19.13 versus 20.97 at 4,500). More iterations might lower teacher-state error, but the live failure and route-label problem mean another identical fit is not justified on this evidence alone.
5. **Deployment latency.** Twelve diffusion denoising passes yielded complete SC inference p95 of 302–352 ms on the matched development runs, above the 300 ms limit. SFP was 250–273 ms. Fewer denoising passes would need an action/route check, not only a speed check.
6. **Representation and recovery remain hypotheses.** The model uses frozen global ImageNet ResNet18 views, temporal context, a one-second TCP-body motion goal, and a 4-action diffusion head. The live traces show targetward commands on teacher observations but wrong-sign lateral commands in some actor-visited SC states. We have not isolated whether spatial information was lost in global features, whether the history is insufficient, or whether the main missing ingredient is actor-visited correction data. No verified action-frame bug explains the checked teacher-state labels.

The training summary is at `outputs/experiments/2026-10-11_route_aware_diffusion_transport_v1/official_anchor_retrain/summary.json`; the same directory contains `history.json`, `trace_diagnosis.json`, `live_comparison.json` and `three_arm_comparison.json`.

## 2. Were teachers consistent with the requested routes?

**SC: no, the newest 22 were not generated with LLB.** The desired route is back → left while descending below the card tops → forward beside the row → right → tilt in clearance → back to the selected port → guarded insertion. The older `sc_llb_cleaned_v3` data came from a privileged MoveIt plan replayed in Gazebo and was built around that route. The new collector explicitly runs `aic_example_policies.ros.CheatCode` for SC as well as SFP. It does not call the LLB planner or check a below-card corridor. The new 22 SC episodes are **transport prefixes** cut at the first safe buffer, not 22 full insertion experts. Some original rollouts inserted; others did not. Their admission into this transport dataset says nothing about route fidelity.

Two directly inspected new SC examples:

- Five cards: `/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-11_official_anchor_route_coverage_v1/rollouts/sc_trial3_train_04/frames/1791757528374680145_10000000000_side.jpg` and `.../1791757544703459338_20000000000_side.jpg`.
- Three-card exact anchor: `/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-11_official_anchor_sc_exact_v1/rollouts/sc_trial3_exact_train_00/frames/1791759902233911189_10000000000_side.jpg` and `.../1791759918797997471_20000000000_side.jpg`.

The visible orange cable is raised near the cards in these samples. Full route certification would require checking the 3D cable path against card volumes throughout every prefix. That check was absent. The new SC rows should be excluded from any future **LLB-consistent** fit unless they pass such a route check; the already trained coverage checkpoint is retained only as a failed diagnostic.

**SFP-to-NIC: yes for the new episodes.** The new 16 SFP training examples were generated with CheatCode, matching the planned SFP teacher style. The older SFP union draws on verified SFP sources and task-coded requests; its complete provenance is broader than this two-anchor collector. This audit verified the new collector policy and admission, not every historical SFP trajectory frame. All SFP rows use the same action-frame materialization, but that alone does not establish visual route consistency of every old row.

## 3. Did initial conditions cover the released evaluation possibilities?

**Only partly.** The new collector varied board x/y by ±15 mm, board yaw by ±0.05 rad, card rail positions, and SC card presence/count (one to five) and selected SC port (0/1). It added 40 general scenes and eight exact SC scenes. Its new SFP scenes covered only the two requests in the three [released qualification anchors](https://github.com/intrinsic-dev/aic/blob/main/aic_engine/config/eval_config.yaml): one card at SFP card 2/port 0 and two cards at card 4/port 1. The older SFP training union contains all ten card/port codes, but several cells have only 3–9 episodes and the new opposite-side board pose received only 12 new training episodes. The rotated SC anchor was absent from the old LLB board-yaw range, so the new scenes added important appearance coverage but via a different route.

The new scene generator copied the official `robot.home_joint_positions` into every generated scene. It did **not** randomize robot starts. The first 30 older SC LLB pilot runs also had fixed actual starts; its 170 continuation cases explicitly varied the actual starts and checked observed joints against the planner start. Thus the overall dataset has some start variation, but the new board-orientation anchors were paired with a fixed robot home. The qualification file shows three public trial examples and the [official description](https://github.com/intrinsic-dev/aic/blob/main/docs/qualification_phase.md) says future trial mixes may change. We cannot claim these local jittered starts span an unseen final/production distribution.

**Decision:** do not interpret the 602-episode total as proof of route or reset coverage. Keep the latest checkpoint unpromoted. Before any new fit, separate SC LLB from new SC CheatCode labels; require a measured cable/card route gate for SC, and make a reset-by-request coverage table that includes initial robot joints, board orientation, card mask, selected port and grasp. Then use a small matched autonomous development gate before scaling, with final scenes sealed.
