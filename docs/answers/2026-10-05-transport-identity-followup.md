# Transport and selected-port identity follow-up

I checked the saved autonomous SFP runs and found two upstream failures.

1. **Transport does not respond enough to the requested port.** On the exact same held-out camera images and robot history, changing the task code among all ten SFP card/port requests changed the actor's first movement target by only 0.489 mm median across the entire set. The old successful training episodes are strongly unbalanced and usually have one requested port per scene. A bounded task-specific complete-action head trained on 50 balanced episodes improved held-out first-target error from 3.236 to 3.013 mm p95, only 6.9%, below the 20% gate. I did not connect it to live control.
2. **The coarse visual crop does not reliably acquire the requested port on the actor's own views.** On 111 synchronized original-image rows from three saved autonomous episodes, it proposed 27 crops. The frozen crop model accepted five, all from the card-0/port-0 episode; it got no accepted target on the other two episodes. There were zero accepted wrong ports in this small reused sample, but the acquisition requirement fails. Posthoc geometry was used only to score proposals; it did not select crops or control the actor. Projected openings and diagnostic markers are not independently verified visual visibility labels.

The saved actor-versus-teacher camera montages at 4, 8 and 12 seconds and the exact machine results are under [`outputs/experiments/2026-10-05_transport_identity_audit`](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-05_transport_identity_audit/). The full [experiment record](../experiments/2026-10-05-transport-identity-task-code-audit.md) gives commands, metrics, artifact paths and limits.

**Next bounded test:** I prepared six training-only scenes that keep the original board and robot reset but ask for a different card/port. Their original requests already scored full insertion. These paired requests will test whether the transport model can learn different routes from the *same scene* when the task changes. The sequential pilot stops after three if fewer than two fully insert; it needs at least four of six full insertions before a new actor fit. A later actor must improve held-out first-target p95 at least 20% and safely arrive on at least two of three new development starts before a larger promotion test. The visual stage separately needs reviewed true visibility/identity labels and zero wrong accepts with at least nine of ten early acquisitions on new development groups. Final scenes stay sealed, and handoff, insertion and RL stay off.
