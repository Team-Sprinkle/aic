# How I would improve the transport-to-insertion handoff

The immediate objective is a **repeatable, observation-only arrival at the requested port**, followed by a stable transfer to the insertion actor. I would improve it in this order. The corrected SFP actor made only 1/3 fresh safe buffered arrivals. The plain SC BC comparison made 0/16 stable 10 mm handoffs, although the later SC visual-standoff controller accepted 4/4 new two-port development shadow handoffs; a subsequent one-port run missed a valid arrival. These are different controllers and scene sets, so the SC numbers should not be pooled. ## 1. Separate three decisions that are currently easy to conflate

1. **Should transport pause for an inspection?** This is a broad, reversible decision. The transport actor's proposal head, measured robot position and task-dependent broad region can suggest an inspection; none alone proves that the robot is at the correct opening.
2. **Did we identify the requested port?** The coarse model should either identify that one port in the current three-camera images and history or abstain. The precise refiner then measures its pose. A neighboring port with a plausible pose must be rejected.
3. **Is the robot in a state the insertion actor can use?** Check measured plug-to-port axial and lateral displacement, orientation, view quality, low motion, and contact/force. Require these conditions over several actual control decisions before switching. If they are not met, continue transport or make a small visual correction; do not treat a good crop alone as permission to insert.

The existing SFP pause proposer was timely on 26/27 grouped **teacher** routes but missed the broad pause window on one of three saved actor routes. The selected-port crop worked well on an 18-scene stress set, but other identity checks were less consistent. This supports using pause as a request to inspect, with a separate accept decision, while continuing to test both on autonomous routes. ## 2. Fix arrival before tuning the switch threshold

For **SFP-to-NIC**, the recent five-card actor approached the wrong card with no contact penalty. Its task code was wired correctly, but its action changed too little when the requested port changed in same-image tests. I would first test an explicit *requested-card/port visual goal and route-progress output* on actor-visited images, then train transport to respond to that goal. The goal is a complete route/subgoal decision, **not** a delta correction added to the present BC action. Ordinary teacher-route action error is insufficient: the learned action must choose the right early branch and remain safe after small deviations. [Wrong-branch evidence](../experiments/2026-10-07-sfp-later-state-causal-repair.md#fresh-autonomous-live-result-gate-failed)

For **SC-to-SC**, the successful Low Lateral Bypass teachers establish a useful route, but the actor often loses its route stage and drifts. I would supervise the next *safe route segment* and whether that segment has been completed using measured robot travel and images, rather than elapsed time. History matters around the tilt/return stage. The earlier SC stage/waypoint probe and the SFP short-waypoint probe failed their gates, so I would **not** just enlarge those same datasets or repeat their labels. First verify that the proposed next segment is correct at states the actor actually visits; if it is not, revise the observation or route-label method before a full actor fit. [SC failure and previous route proposal](../experiments/2026-10-01-sc-llb-cleaned-bc.md#proposed-explicit-plan-revision-route-progress-and-subgoal-control) · [SFP waypoint failure](../experiments/2026-10-08-sfp-short-route-waypoint-feasibility.md)

The recent privileged MoveIt SFP run shows a path to one requested 85 mm buffer is physically executable without contact, but its geometry-assisted planner is not a deployable visual goal source. Its cable-screen result also failed the six-scene safety gate. I would use such paths as **diagnostic or training labels only after validating their cable route in Gazebo**, never as evidence that autonomous handoff already works. ## 3. Add a short visual docking phase before the actor switch

Once the requested opening is visually verified, use its **predicted** pose to make small, bounded, measured corrections toward a safe standoff. Re-estimate from fresh images after each correction. This is the same basic closed-loop idea as [position-based visual servoing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0967066122001940), applied here only after target identity has been checked. It uses no simulator geometry at deployment. It should not attempt forceful insertion: its purpose is to remove the residual position/orientation error and create a stable handoff state.

This docking phase is particularly relevant because one SFP actor reached the broad buffer but had about **10.9°** orientation error and stalled. A single pause signal does not repair that pose. The precise refiner may be accurate in clear views, but if the requested opening is hidden or the views disagree, docking must abstain or obtain another safe view. ## 4. Run a handoff-only gate before insertion or RL

Keep the current insertion actor disconnected. On new nonfinal, episode-grouped Gazebo starts for **both** families, log every proposed inspection, selected-port identity, predicted pose, measured plug-to-port error, orientation, motion, force/contact, and whether the conditions remain true for consecutive decisions. True port geometry is for **posthoc scoring only**. Save video around accepted and rejected proposals. Report all runs and a separately marked no-contact subset; do not erase contact runs.

I would first require zero accepted wrong ports, at least 9/10 correct early requested-port acquisitions, and stable arrival at the previously used 10 mm transport handoff region on most new starts, with acceptable measured orientation and full inference p95 below 300 ms. The exact final docking tolerance should be set from independently measured states that the insertion actor can actually recover from; the current broad transport buffer is **not** the 0.5 mm insertion corridor. If the robot misses the neighborhood, fix transport. If it reaches the neighborhood but the proposal misses, fix the pause head. If the proposal is timely but selects the wrong port, fix identity or obtain a better view. If identity is right but the pose is unusable, fix the refiner or docking controller. Only after this gate should we connect the insertion actor, and only after that should contact recovery/RL be evaluated.

**My recommendation:** make the next experiment a frozen, handoff-only autonomous comparison on new nonfinal SFP and SC starts, while developing an explicit route/goal representation for the actor's demonstrated wrong-branch and drift failures. It is the shortest test that tells us whether collisions are masking a good handoff or whether the handoff itself still fails.
