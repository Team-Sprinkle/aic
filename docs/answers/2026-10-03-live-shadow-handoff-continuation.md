# Live handoff continuation result

I implemented a live SC pause-and-check step. When transport thinks it has reached the requested port, the robot holds its measured pose, takes fresh images from all three wrist cameras, and checks the requested opening, board direction, TCP posture, standoff, and force. If the views disagree or posture is wrong, it refuses to hand off and resumes transport. After a reliable posture rejection, it can use the observed board direction to correct its orientation and then inspect again. The runtime check uses camera images, measured robot state, and the task request; simulator geometry is used only afterward to score the recordings.

The first live pilot found a real posture problem: the robot was about 4.5° off despite being close to its transport waiting point. The visual check correctly rejected it. With the targeted orientation correction, a matched repeat reduced the error to about 0.2° and accepted the handoff without scored contact.

I then tested four new SC development starts: both requested ports, with one, four, and five cards. All **4/4** reached accepted **transport-only** handoffs. Their post-run opening estimates were 0.11–0.30 mm from the recorded openings, and all had no scored contact. The original image path was too slow on its first check (332–364 ms). I batched the crop network, searched the three independent camera images concurrently, and used original RGB directly in the shadow verifier. Matched repeats on the **same four starts** again accepted **4/4**, with 0.10–0.25 mm opening error and **176 ms p95** verifier time over six inspections. I checked saved predicted-opening overlays for port 0 and port 1; the yellow markers sit on the requested blue SC openings. These runs **did not attempt insertion**.

I also checked why SFP cannot use this handoff yet. The frozen arrival head was trained on **SC-only** labels and is disabled for SFP by default. In one opt-in, transport-only SFP diagnostic, it proposed stopping at 16.95 s while the measured TCP was about **162 mm from the selected opening**. That is a false early arrival. I left SFP stopping disabled and stopped that branch after the bounded diagnosis.

The next work is to challenge the SC verifier with truly hidden openings and coherent wrong-port predictions, and to create and test an SFP-specific arrival signal from existing grouped trajectories before collecting new SFP starts. The complete transport-to-insertion switch also needs the attached insertion actor and ROS-to-command latency measured together. Insertion training, RL, and final scenes remain gated.
