# Global-coordinate proxy and coarse-perception status — October 5, 2026

Yes: the policy receives the robot TCP pose in `base_link`, so it can track where its gripper is in a fixed robot coordinate system. It receives the requested card/port name. The exact global position of that port comes from simulator ground truth in CheatCode and cannot be used at autonomous evaluation. I tested whether the task name plus a **training-set average port position** could substitute for it.

The port location varied too much to be a precise target. On ten separate, nonfinal SFP reset configurations, that nominal location was **47.6 mm** from the real port at the median, with **55.7 mm** error at the 90th percentile. The published evaluation setup also randomizes the board pose and card/SC rail placements. SC rail translation alone spans −60 to +55 mm. Thus a fixed world/base coordinate cannot supply insertion pose or reliable port identity.

I replayed a 100 mm nominal-distance stopping rule on ten saved CheatCode teacher rollouts and three saved autonomous policy rollouts. It coincided with a safe buffered standoff in **9/10 teacher** rollouts because those routes had already been steered toward the true port. It gave **0/3 safe standoffs autonomously**: one actor never reached the nominal neighborhood, and the other two would have stopped with the tool **31 mm** or **99 mm** laterally misaligned. A tighter 80 mm threshold also failed to give a safe autonomous stop. This is why the apparent teacher success does not justify replacing visual perception.

The coarse visual experiments also remain below the predeclared safe-handoff bar. Their best direct-row plus rail-fallback crop model accepted 299/391 inspected proposals but selected the wrong port once and acquired the target early in only 7/10 episodes. The required bar is zero wrong accepted ports and at least 9/10 early episodes, followed by complete live latency under 300 ms. Automatic transport-to-insertion handoff and RL remain disabled; the four reserved final IK scenes remain sealed.

My recommendation is to use robot coordinates only as a **cheap cue to look more carefully** when the arm enters a broad region. The decision to stop and insert still needs observation-based port identity, a precise selected-port pose, measured standoff and force checks. The next targeted repair is to establish which physical card slot the cameras show and curate real visible/occluded and safe-stop labels on autonomous transport views. Then test on new episode-grouped development starts. Additional generic crop fits on the same data are unlikely to resolve the demonstrated slot-identity ambiguity.

I did not start fresh Gazebo jobs: the rootless Docker daemon was healthy but its user kernel key quota was full at **117/117**. The replay used already recorded, complete nonfinal rollouts. No active job was interrupted, no new model was trained for this coordinate audit, and no final scene was opened.
