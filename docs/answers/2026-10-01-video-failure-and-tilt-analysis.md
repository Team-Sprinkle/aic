# What failed in the videos? Does the tilt hit the cards?

**Short answer:** The latest temporal transport model has **no rollout video**: it failed an offline prediction gate and was never run autonomously in Gazebo. In the earlier learned-policy videos, the main failure is that the arm takes the wrong route or drifts away before reaching the intended near-port handoff. The videos do **not** show the planned tilt striking a card as the reason those policies failed. A separate, older **teacher** route did sometimes fail near insertion: an instrumented repeat shows the **plug catching on the selected port**, not a cable snag on a card.

## What I saw in the videos

I sampled the five-view autonomous videos at 0, 8, 16, 24, 32, 40, 50, 60, and 69 seconds and inspected the teacher route's side/overhead contact sheets and the oracle close views. The saved [video audit montages](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_temporal_transport/video_failure_audit/) make those samples easy to compare.

- In [motion-safe, port 0, two cards](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_sc_llb_cleaned_bc/autonomous_transport/motion_safe_port0_two/video/all_views_20fps.mp4), the arm moves away from the intended route early and ends far to the side of the board. The final handoff error is 372 mm. The side and overhead views do not show a cable held against a card while the arm struggles to advance; this is primarily a wrong-path failure.
- In [clean, port 0, two cards](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_sc_llb_cleaned_bc/autonomous_transport/clean_port0_two/video/all_views_20fps.mp4), the gripper stays visibly above/away from the port region for much of the episode and never makes a stable local-insertion handoff. Final handoff error is 103 mm.
- In [warm start, port 1, three cards](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_sc_llb_cleaned_bc/autonomous_transport/warm_port1_three/video/all_views_20fps.mp4), it comes closer transiently (28 mm from the planned standoff) but then moves away and finishes 110 mm out. That is still too far for the local insertion actor. The large viewpoint changes in wrist cameras are caused partly by arm motion and occlusion; they are not evidence that the plug struck a card.
- The [60 mm warm-start port-0 run](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_sc_llb_cleaned_bc/autonomous_transport/warm_cap60_port0_two/video/all_views_20fps.mp4) also moves away and finishes 516 mm from handoff. A larger per-command cap amplified a bad direction; it did not reveal an insertion-phase collision.

The learned policy can rotate its wrist in free space, but it has not demonstrably executed the planned **tilt in the clear, return, then insert** sequence. The score and path traces agree: the failures occur before full insertion. The five-view images can rule out an obvious sustained snag in the reviewed examples, but they cannot prove that no brief cable/card contact occurred; these runs did not record named high-rate contact pairs.

The successful [teacher five-view video](../../outputs/experiments/2026-09-30_moveit_tilt_before_return/port1_tilt_hold_success_video/all_views_20fps.mp4) is different. The arm sweeps past the card row, **tilts outside the row**, returns, and inserts. Around the tilt/return section, the cable is close to and sometimes appears to touch a card, yet the arm continues. A touch is not by itself a snag. The measured TCP moves only 1.59 mm during the tilt while the plug tip sweeps 8.78 mm because the wrist rotates about the TCP rather than the tip. The tip is roughly 33–39 mm below the highest card top during this section. This does not certify clearance for the *whole cable*, but the scored successful repeat and continuous motion do not support a tilt-induced card blockage in that run. See the [teacher route analysis](../experiments/2026-09-30-sc-tilt-before-return.md) and [side-view contact sheet](../../outputs/experiments/2026-09-30_moveit_tilt_before_return/port1_tilt_hold_success_video/side_20fps_contact_sheet.jpg).

The [matched older-route failure video](../../outputs/experiments/2026-09-30_moveit_tilt_before_return/port1_old_tilt_matched_failure_video/all_views_20fps.mp4) reaches the port area, then stalls while trying to insert. The wide view alone cannot name the contact surface. The later oracle repeat, with projected contacts in two close views, identifies plug/selected-port contact at the wrist-force spike. That supports **local misalignment/port-lip catch** as the reproduced failure, not an over-card cable snag. The exact historical run had no contact-pair recorder, so the oracle repeat does not prove its exact pair retroactively.

## Why `tilt_clear_of_cards` is the worst offline stage

The route includes a five-second hold after the tilt. At the recorded boundary between that hold and the return stage, the robot and all three camera images can look almost unchanged. Across 21 held-out boundary pairs, median TCP motion was 0.026 mm, median target-command change 0.160 mm, and mean center-image difference only 0.061/255 at 64×64. Thus the model can predict “return now” when the teacher label still says “hold tilt,” or vice versa. Intermediate route subgoals also vary by scene. This explains the **offline stage/subgoal confusion** more directly than any observed collision; the exact contribution of each cause to the error has not been isolated.

The practical diagnosis is: repair route-progress and handoff control before looking for an autonomous tilt collision. If a later policy actually reaches the tilt and becomes blocked, record named contacts, measured TCP/tip motion, and close side/overhead video before attributing it to the card or cable.
