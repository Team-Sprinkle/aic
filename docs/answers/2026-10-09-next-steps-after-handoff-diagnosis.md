# Next steps after the handoff diagnosis

**Execution update (October 9):** Steps 1–2 were audited on six saved actor states and three preselected privileged Gazebo routes. The observation-only target was available, but the cable safety screen remains uncalibrated and a five-card route stalled under force. Thus the safe-route and actor-state-label gates did not pass; steps 3–6 remain conditional and were not run. See the [measured continuation and revised stopping rule](../experiments/2026-10-09-sfp-route-target-and-gazebo-gate.md).

The main bottleneck is **getting the robot to the requested port**. The SC visual checker approved 4/4 new two-port arrivals, but one-port performance is not established. For SFP, only one of six saved autonomous routes entered the stop corridor, even though the visual check recognized that route's two sampled visits. Better stopping logic cannot rescue a route that never gets near the target. The SC saved-orientation fallback remains off: its synthetic failure check passed, but no natural rollout showed a memory-only improvement.

## Recommended order

1. **Make a safe SFP route target from the actor's actual state.** Revisit the six saved actor takeovers with an obstacle-aware planner and a cable check calibrated against *actual* Gazebo contacts. Gazebo geometry may label or score development routes, but the deployed goal must come from public task code, camera/history and measured robot state. Audit whether the frozen requested-port estimate is accepted at each actor takeover; the earlier first-correction audit found it available in only 1/3 development episodes. When it is unavailable, the actor needs visual route context or a new observation-only acquisition step, not a filled-in oracle pose. The old rigid-cable screen cleared only 3/6 plans and also rejected successful teacher motion, so it cannot be treated as a contact oracle. Stop this planner branch if it cannot produce at least 5/6 plausible routes **and** an observation-only route target on those groups; do not generate another broad dataset merely to compensate.

2. **Test a few routes with the real simulated cable.** If the offline route gate passes, execute at most three matched, nonfinal Gazebo transport replays, including a difficult multi-card case. Require at least 2/3 safe arrivals at the requested port's roughly 85 mm buffer, no worse contact than the frozen actor, and measured command tracking. One earlier privileged five-card replay reached its buffer without scored contact, but one case is not a reliability result. If the new routes snag or miss, diagnose those traces before creating policy labels.

3. **Teach one transport actor to follow a route.** Only after route feasibility and observation-only target availability pass, create actor-visited training examples with a short route target, progress and recovery direction. The planner may use privileged geometry to create *training labels*; the deployed actor must use cameras, task code and measured robot state, plus a requested-port pose only when the visual estimator accepts it. Preserve the existing actor as a matched control. Do not repeat the simple “append predicted pose to BC” recipe: it barely changed actions, and earlier short-waypoint predictions pointed along recovery on 0/6 actor-boundary cases.

4. **Gate the new actor before more live rollouts.** On complete held-out episodes, compare route choice, correction direction, correct card/port sensitivity, smoothness and contact risk with the frozen actor. Then run a small new nonfinal 2/3/5-card Gazebo test. The existing live gate is at least 2/3 safe buffered arrivals without increased contact; if it fails, return to the measured actor-boundary error, not routine data scaling.

5. **Connect stopping, then insertion.** Keep the visual stop in shadow mode until the actor regularly reaches the buffer. At those *autonomous* arrivals, require correct requested-port identity, predicted axial/lateral corridor, fresh precise pose and acceptable tool orientation/force, with no wrong-port accepts and complete p95 decision latency below 300 ms. Only then attach the already trained pose-based insertion actor on new development starts. Measure full insertion, not just handoff. Keep reserved final scenes sealed.

6. **Add recovery and RL after a working supervised path.** Once transport, handoff and insertion work together, compare no backoff, local backoff and hierarchical backoff on reproducible blockage episodes. Use offline/online RL only if the supervised pipeline reaches enough real failure states to give meaningful reward and safety signals. Keep one shared actor across normal motion and recovery; hardcoded backtracking can remain a separate, explicitly measured controller action.

The **next bounded experiment is step 1**, followed by at most three Gazebo route replays if its gate passes. SC two-port handoff can remain frozen while this SFP transport bottleneck is addressed. Revisit the SC one-port orientation-memory idea only when a *natural* native-image rail failure is captured with a causally qualified earlier view; the current synthetic test alone does not justify enabling it.
