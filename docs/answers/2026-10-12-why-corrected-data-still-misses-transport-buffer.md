# Why the corrected transport data did not solve autonomous arrival

**Yes:** the route-conflicting SC training examples were removed and the actor was retrained, but it still reached the safe transport buffer on **0/3 SFP** and **0/3 SC** matched nonfinal Gazebo starts. The buffer requires the measured TCP to be 70–100 mm before the selected port and within 10 mm laterally. The closest new SFP run was 10.97 mm lateral while in that axial band, just outside the rule, and had a scored gripper/card contact. None of the three SC runs entered the axial band. The held-out teacher-state action error improved, but the actor's own slightly wrong actions change the next view and state; those errors can accumulate along a long route. These results do not identify one exclusive cause.

**“Collision-free teacher” needs a precise meaning.** The teacher runs used normal Gazebo physics; the plug must eventually contact the receptacle to insert. The relevant filter is no *prohibited/scored* collision or excessive force, especially in the transport prefix. I checked all **104 source SC LLB successes** behind the 83/21 split: every official score says `No contact detected.` and `No excessive force detected`. The **40 newly collected SFP** randomized-start episodes were admitted only with no scored contact; 38 fully inserted, and two partial-insertion runs contributed only the safe, pre-insertion transport prefix. Earlier SFP sources were verified by their own score/action admission rules, but I have **not rerun a uniform contact audit over every one of the 533 SFP training episodes**, so it would be too strong to claim that every source had no physical collision. A successful official insertion score alone does not prove that.

**Start coverage improved but remains incomplete.** The 40 new SFP episodes really changed the first-trial robot joints and verified the measured starts; the perturbations were only up to 0.02 rad per joint. The all-request pilot used one-card layouts for its new card/port combinations, while older SFP data cover one to five cards with uneven combinations. SC LLB data span one to five cards and both ports, but their board yaw range omits the released rotated SC layout used by the public development stress test. The corrected data therefore cover more starts and target codes than before, but not the full cross-product of starts, cards, board poses, and requested ports. The SC coverage gap likely matters for the rotated SC failures. The SFP failures cannot be attributed to start breadth alone: the model also makes off-route decisions on actor-visited states, despite better imitation on recorded teacher observations.

The [retrain report](../experiments/2026-10-12-route-consistent-diffusion-retrain.md) contains the exact comparison and recordings. The next discriminating experiment should gather a small number of safe, route-consistent teacher corrections from **states actually reached by this actor**, with a preset success/contact gate, before another fit. Rotated-board SC also needs a verified Low Lateral Bypass route; the excluded SC CheatCode shortcut should not be restored as training data.
