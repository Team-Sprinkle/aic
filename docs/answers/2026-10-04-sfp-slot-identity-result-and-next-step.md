# SFP port-identity repair: result and next step

I carried out the bounded repair discussed after the fresh SFP test. The ten-scene failure list is frozen, and the new labels use 59 complete training reset groups plus ten separate model-selection groups. Simulator port geometry supplied labels and posthoc scoring only. The deployed decision code receives images, camera calibration and the requested card/port code; it does not receive true port positions. Four reserved final scenes stayed sealed.

I trained one model on GPU 1 for the full 2,000-update cap. It predicts the five numbered card slots and both ports on each card from original camera images, then applies fixed three-camera and physical-spacing checks. I found and fixed two startup defects before the meaningful fit: absent cards were initially labeled as visible slots, and multiprocessing image loading stalled after CUDA initialized. The stopped starts and logs were kept; the corrected `fit_v3` is the reported run.

**It did not pass.** On the same 136 development observations, the best new checkpoint accepted 1 and acquired an early anchor in 0/10 episodes. The frozen selected-only head accepted 51 and acquired early anchors in 4/10, with no wrong accepted port for either. Later new-model checkpoints accepted none. The new training loss improved, but the camera predictions did not agree well enough to locate the socket safely. Port-keypoint error had long tails: 127 px p95 for port 0 and 117 px for port 1. Most requested slot and port in-frame scores were high, so the problem is point localization/correspondence rather than simple absence prediction.

I also checked a small observation-only history idea on the ten already frozen fresh scenes: require two or three accepted estimates to agree in space, optionally after the measured tool moves 10 mm. For the structured head, two frames reduced early first anchors from 5/10 to 3/10 but still left one wrong first anchor. Adding the movement condition removed that wrong first anchor and left only 3/10 coverage. A wrong neighbor can remain stable across frames, so simple temporal agreement is not a sufficient identity check.

Because development failed, I did not open 20 new evaluation scenes, measure complete live inference latency, connect transport to insertion, or start RL. The older Isaac process remained running and was not interrupted. The detailed [experiment record](../experiments/2026-10-04-sfp-slot-pair-identity.md), [machine summary](../experiments/2026-10-04-sfp-slot-pair-identity.summary.json), and [artifact guide](../../outputs_README.md) contain commands, checkpoint paths, predictions and diagnoses. Main status and handoff docs were updated.

**Next bounded experiment:** Test whether a deliberate side-on view separates adjacent cards and the two ports visually. Use at most four nonfinal, previously troublesome SFP configurations, each with a collision-checked standoff and at most two small lateral camera moves; abort on unsafe force/contact. Budget at most two hours of sequential Gazebo capture and analysis, with at most one GPU and no training. Save original images and ask, for each view, whether the requested opening can be identified without geometry overlays. Then score the frozen predictors on those views. If at least three of four become visually identifiable but the frozen heads still choose neighbors, train one view-conditioned identity model on a separate grouped split. If the openings remain ambiguous or the motion is unsafe, change the observation strategy rather than collect more routine trajectories. This is a diagnostic proposal, not a promoted deployment behavior.
