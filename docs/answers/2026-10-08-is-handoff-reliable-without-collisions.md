# Is the handoff reliable if we ignore collisions?

**Short answer: not yet, for either SFP-to-NIC or SC-to-SC.** We can sometimes estimate the requested opening accurately when the robot is already in a good viewing position. We have not shown that the autonomous transport actor consistently reaches that position, stops at the right time, and transfers a usable state to the insertion actor. Those failures can occur without a cable snag or card collision.

By *handoff* I mean the complete sequence: (1) transport approaches the **requested** opening with a usable position and orientation, (2) the stop/proposal logic pauses at a safe point, (3) visual perception verifies the requested opening and estimates its pose, and (4) the insertion actor takes over with consistent inputs. A good pose estimate on a teacher-placed robot tests only part of this sequence.

## What seems to be going wrong?

The strongest current explanation is **transport route and progress error**, plus an incompletely validated stop/identity decision. In an SFP five-card run, the actor branched near card 0 although card 1 was requested. It ended roughly 69 mm sideways from the requested opening, and that run had no official contact penalty. Another SFP run reached the broad buffered region but had about 10.9° orientation error and then stalled. The causal source of the wrong branch is not proven; weak use of requested-port information, visual grounding, and route progress are candidates. The corrected SFP actor reached the safe buffer in **1/3** new autonomous starts, versus **0/3** for its control, below the declared 2/3 gate. [SFP live diagnosis](../experiments/2026-10-07-sfp-later-state-causal-repair.md#fresh-autonomous-live-result-gate-failed).

For SC-to-SC, the plain BC transport actors produced **0/16 stable 10 mm handoffs** in a controlled comparison; reviewed videos showed route drift without a sustained cable snag. A *different*, later visual-standoff controller did much better: its frozen SC verifier accepted **4/4** new two-port development starts with accurate post-run pose and no scored contact. But the same frozen stack subsequently **missed a valid one-port port-0 arrival** within about 1 mm of the standoff. Thus SC handoff can work in favorable settings, but its recall and wrong/hidden-port safety are not yet reliable across settings. [Plain BC comparison](../experiments/2026-10-01-sc-llb-cleaned-bc.md#final-bounded-supervised-result-and-step-5-decision) · [Four-start visual-standoff screen](../experiments/2026-10-03-live-sc-shadow-handoff.md#four-new-development-starts) · [One-port miss](../experiments/2026-10-03-sc-shadow-oneport-stress.md#native-rgb-one-port-tests).

Collision recovery can address a plug catching a port lip, a gripper hitting a card, or a cable snag **after the robot encounters a blockage**. It cannot by itself tell the actor which requested port to approach, correct an early wrong route in free space, or decide when an unseen/incorrect port should be accepted. Hierarchical backoff and RL therefore remain useful later, but they do not certify this handoff.

The recent MoveIt SFP video does **not** establish automatic handoff. It used simulator geometry to plan after an actor prefix, ended about 85 mm before the port, had no contact, and deliberately did not invoke the insertion actor. It shows that a collision-free planned approach is possible on that one scene; it does not test whether the learned actor or stop logic would choose that approach. ## What test would separate collision from handoff?

Freeze the current transport and handoff components and replay new nonfinal starts without enabling the insertion actor. Score each *proposed* switch using ground truth only **afterward**: requested-port identity, first/last stop time, axial/lateral distance, orientation, view validity, and measured contact/force. Report all starts and a separately marked no-contact subset. Include teacher/planner-placed good views as positive controls, but judge autonomous handoff on autonomous starts. This would tell us whether failures are due to blockage, route arrival, stop timing, or visual identity. Keep final scenes sealed. Until this passes, the automatic switch should remain off.
