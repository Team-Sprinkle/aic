# Wrong-route diagnosis and diffusion transport result — October 11, 2026

The old transport actor sometimes took the wrong side of a five-card SFP-to-NIC board. On a saved nonfinal card1/port1 run, a small early command difference grew into a wrong route. The older training cache also heavily favored one requested card/port code. We repaired the sampling balance and trained a larger **route-aware diffusion actor**: 14.29 million trainable Transformer parameters plus 11.18 million frozen three-camera ResNet-18 parameters, about **25.47 million total**. It predicts a one-second route goal from observation history, then generates four complete TCP-relative actions. The measured future route is a *training label only*; no true port pose, teacher route or simulator geometry enters the deployed actor. A failed unstable epsilon sampler was kept as evidence and replaced with velocity prediction. The base fit ran 12,000 updates; a paired-request fit ran 4,000 more.

Offline, the paired-request model changed its route in the teacher's direction on all 13 held-out separable same-image request pairs; the median change magnitude was 71% of the teacher's. In Gazebo, it still missed the safe transport buffer. A privileged teacher could recover and insert from an actor-deviated state, so we tested causal actor-to-teacher correction data under a matched training budget.

| Corrective branch | Verified training data | Reused card1/port1 trial 549: closest lateral error in the -100 to -70 mm axial band | Safe buffer? |
| --- | --- | ---: | --- |
| Matched no-correction control | ordinary teacher data | 22.10 mm | no |
| Broad correction, 10% SFP mass | 9 safe teacher recoveries; **no card1/port1 correction** | 70.14 mm | no |
| Early card1/port1 correction, 3% mass | 10 safe recoveries, mostly older `n130` starts | 52.31 mm | no |
| Matched-difficulty `n400` correction, 3% mass | 10 safe five-card `n400` hard recoveries | **4.55 mm** | **yes, 14 samples** |

The early card1/port1 fit learned its teacher continuations offline: early first-action p95 on those *training correction states* fell from 9.65 mm for the control to 3.88 mm. It still failed live. Nine of those ten resets came from the older `n130__card0_port1_resume` source. Their median initial requested-port-frame lateral coordinates were (71.6, 24.9) mm; the failed `n400` development start was (67.4, 81.2) mm. The joint starting state was poorly covered.

We then ran a **distinct bounded matched-difficulty experiment**, selecting ten training-only five-card `n400__ec2_02_sfp_hard` resets with initial lateral-y from 29.0 to 90.6 mm. The frozen actor ran four seconds; a privileged teacher then fully inserted in **10/10**, without scored contact. Strict causal materialization admitted all ten: **2,355 teacher action chunks**, 605 actor-prefix context-only chunks, maximum episode target-reference p95 1.65 mm, and zero training/development reset overlap. The actor prefix was never an expert action target. A new diffusion fine tune used the same architecture, initialization, optimizer, seed, batch and 4,000-update cap as the matched control, with those corrections receiving 3% of SFP sampling mass. Its best checkpoint was update 3,000.

The candidate achieved **1/3** safe buffered arrivals against the preset **2/3** requirement and added two scored card contacts where matched controls had none. The successful buffer sample on trial 549 was at axial -98.13 mm and lateral 4.55 mm, with **4.89° orientation error** relative to the teacher's standoff orientation; it is a transport result, not insertion readiness. None of these transport-only rollouts inserted; automatic handoff and insertion were intentionally disabled. All candidate complete inference p95 values met the 300 ms limit, with Gazebo on physical GPU 3 and the actor on GPU 4. The final split remained sealed. The first scene shows a real autonomous transport improvement, while the low-offset second start and different requested-card third start show a coverage tradeoff. These observations support overspecialization from a narrow corrective distribution; they do not prove the model's internal cause or establish that diffusion itself is unsuitable.

**Decision:** The wrong-route problem is better diagnosed and locally improved, but **not reliably fixed**. Do not promote any corrective checkpoint or start RL. The next supervised data design should balance **starting geometry, route difficulty, and requested card/port code together**, with new complete-reset development starts reserved before fitting. If that still fails the autonomous buffer and contact gate, change to an explicit observation-driven route/standoff controller or an action-conditioned spatial representation instead of repeating the same narrow fine tune. The diagnostic ground-truth TF flood that slowed privileged takeovers was fixed separately by delaying TF listener startup; it does not change these autonomous outcomes.
