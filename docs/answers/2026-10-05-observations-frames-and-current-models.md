# What the robot observes, which coordinates are stable, and what we trained

## 1. Observations and coordinate frames

The official `Observation` message contains **three wrist-camera images** (left, center, right), **camera calibration information for each**, a **wrist force/torque measurement**, **joint states**, and a **controller state**. The controller state includes measured TCP pose and velocity, the controller's reference TCP pose, current-to-target TCP error, reference joint command, execution mode, and force-sensor tare offset. The public `Task` request is separate: it names the cable/plug and the requested module and port; for example `nic_card_mount_2` and `sfp_port_0`. It names a target, but does **not** supply the target's 3D position or orientation. The ordinary learned actor packs part of the observation into a **32-number state**: measured TCP position (3), TCP quaternion (4), linear/angular TCP velocity (6), controller TCP error (6), seven joint positions (7), and wrist force/torque (6). It also gets the three images and a separate 10-number public task code.

The measured TCP position and orientation are in the robot's **`base_link` frame**, with metres for position. This base is fixed across the Gazebo scenes we audited, so `(-0.43, 0.20, 0.27)` means the same place relative to the robot in different resets. Joint angles, pixel locations, wrist wrench, and the controller's pose error are different kinds of quantities; the existence of base-frame TCP coordinates does not make every input a world position. Each wrist camera has its own image/optical frame and moves with the hand. The transport actor predicts short **TCP/body-frame pose targets**, which the runtime/controller converts to commands. The selected port's exact `base_link` pose is available to the **training-only CheatCode/scene labels** through ground-truth geometry, but is not in the official deployable observation.

The crucial point is that a **fixed coordinate system is not a fixed target**. Board/rail/card placement changes at reset. In the saved training audit, the *same requested* SFP card 0/port 0 had opening positions in two different resets of approximately:

| Reset | Actual opening position in `base_link` (m) |
| --- | --- |
| A | `(-0.4486, 0.1387, 0.1793)` |
| B | `(-0.4216, 0.2041, 0.1793)` |

Those openings are about **71 mm apart** in the same robot-base frame. Card 4/port 1 also appeared near `(-0.4254, 0.3516, 0.1793)` and `(-0.4199, 0.4064, 0.1793)` in different train resets, about **55 mm apart**. A training-only median for card 0/port 0 was `(-0.4375, 0.2005, 0.1793)`; it missed reset A's actual opening by **62.8 mm**. Across ten separate nonfinal diagnostic resets, the per-request nominal position missed by **47.6 mm median**. These numbers are from the [saved coordinate audit](../experiments/2026-10-05-global-coordinate-arrival-proxy.md), not a guess from the randomization settings.

To know whether the tool is correctly waiting in front of the requested opening, we need its position **relative to that opening and its orientation**:

$$
\mathbf p_{\mathrm{TCP}}^{\mathrm{port}} =
R_{\mathrm{port}}^{\mathsf T}
(\mathbf p_{\mathrm{TCP}}^{\mathrm{base}}-\mathbf p_{\mathrm{port}}^{\mathrm{base}}).
$$

We directly observe the first base-frame position, but must infer the second position and orientation from the cameras (or use privileged geometry during training and scoring only). A rough base-frame location can still tell us **when to look more carefully**. It cannot certify a safe transport-to-insertion handoff. Our buffered handoff diagnostic requires axial TCP position between **70 and 100 mm before** the selected opening and lateral error **≤10 mm**; insertion ultimately needs much finer, about **0.5 mm lateral** alignment. A 50 mm port-location uncertainty overwhelms these gates. Orientation, contact/force, and correct-port identity matter too.

The saved autonomous replay makes the distinction concrete. A rule that paused when the TCP entered a **100 mm sphere around the nominal** requested location would have paused on card 2/port 0 while the TCP was **31.0 mm laterally off** the actual opening and nearer another card's port. On card 4/port 1, the nominal opening estimate itself was only **14.1 mm** from the actual opening, yet the actor crossed the nominal sphere at **99.2 mm lateral error** and on the wrong axial side. Neither is a safe handoff. The rule happened to agree with a safe waiting pose on **9/10 privileged CheatCode teacher routes**, because those routes were already steering toward the exact target; it agreed on **0/3 saved autonomous actor routes**. We should use base coordinates as a *broad inspection cue*, with a visual selected-port check making the actual decision.

## 2. Which models exist, and what was trained most recently?

Your intended pipeline is substantially right, with two corrections: the current transport checkpoint has a *handoff-output slot* but **no trained, deployable handoff probability**, and the current SFP coarse perception is still a set of diagnostic candidate/crop models rather than a validated one-port stop decision.

| Stage | Intended job | Current state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Transport actor** | From three images, measured robot state, task code, and causal history, predict four complete short TCP/body-frame pose targets to travel toward the requested port, even when it is offscreen. Propose a pause near the destination. | The ordinary temporal actor has a two-layer transformer over frozen camera features and an action head. Its checkpoint's `handoff_aux_loss` is **0**, so the handoff head is not trained for use; the runtime refuses an enabled learned stop for that checkpoint. It has not reliably reached the safe SFP buffered region on saved autonomous tests. |
| **Coarse selected-port perception** | After a possible arrival, use current and distance-sampled camera history plus the task request to answer: “is this the requested port, is a pause worthwhile, and where should the original-resolution crop be?” It may reject or ask for another view. | The intended shared crop/stop model is not validated. Current SFP baselines include an all-candidate detector, direct/rail crop proposals and selected-port crop checks. They often miss or confuse the requested port on actor-generated views. Their projected-in-frame labels are not true visual-visibility labels. A coarse positive can only authorize a held inspection, not insertion. |
| **Refiner and final verifier** | Crop the **original high-resolution camera images at the same observation**, refine the requested opening's 3D pose using multiple calibrated views, then check identity, stable pose, TCP posture, force and safety. | Near-port refiner prototypes exist and can be precise on clear SC views, but the full SFP/SC visual identity, orientation, occlusion and autonomous handoff gates are still open. No automatic coarse-to-refiner-to-actor switch is approved. |
| **Insertion actor** | Take the verified, visually estimated port pose and nearby images/state; predict complete port-frame TCP targets for final alignment and insertion. | Separate BC prototypes exist, but none has demonstrated reliable autonomous full insertion from a normal start. This stage is not currently connected to transport. |

In the **most recent training**, I changed **only the transport actor's final action mapping**. I froze its image and temporal backbone and let the requested SFP card/port select one of ten small final linear maps. Each map predicts the full four-target chunk; this was **not** a delta correction, and it did **not** train the handoff head, coarse model, refiner, or insertion actor. The reason was diagnostic: on exactly the same held-out image/state/history, changing only the requested card/port changed the old actor's first target by just **0.489 mm median across all ten requests**. We wanted to see whether stronger task-specific action capacity and *same-board, different-request* teacher examples would make the route follow the public task.

The corrected rootless Docker check allowed six such paired teacher trials. Five fully inserted and reached the measured buffered region. Their labels and the original requests were added to the existing balanced transport training data. The matched final-head fit reduced held-out first-target translation error from **3.236 to 2.937 mm p95**, a **9.3%** improvement against the declared **20%** gate. The model's same-observation task-code output diameter rose to **1.258 mm**, still small. The teacher pairs separated in measured TCP position after roughly **0.3–0.55 s**, so there is a real early route branch to model; after they diverge, their images and states differ, so the simple code-swap metric alone is not a complete explanation. This actor was **not** deployed or run autonomously. The next targeted model experiment is to condition the temporal **route decision itself** on the requested port and test whether it reproduces the early branch and reaches safe buffered poses on new nonfinal starts. The coarse/refiner work stays gated behind reliable arrival and independently checked requested-port identity.
