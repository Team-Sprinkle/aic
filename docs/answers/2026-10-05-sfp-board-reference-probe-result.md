# SFP board-reference test result

I completed the bounded RGB rail-reference experiment. The model used three wrist-camera images to predict the first and last fixed board rails and which of five card slots were occupied. Simulator geometry supplied training and scoring labels only; it was not an inference input. The run used **one GPU**, all 70 eligible train reset groups, **2,000 updates**, and stopped at the planned update cap. Development and three saved autonomous actor reset groups remained separate; reserved final scenes stayed sealed.

The result is mixed. On ten held-out development resets, exact five-slot occupancy improved from **1/10** for the all-occupied baseline to **5/10** for the trained model. It made **three confident false occupied-slot predictions**, so it missed the predeclared requirement of at least **8/10** exact maps and zero confident false occupied slots. On three sparse-card autonomous actor routes it got **3/3** occupancy maps correct, but that small diagnostic cannot override the development failure. The model's first/last rail image-position error against projected CAD labels was **12.0 px median / 26.4 px p90** on development and **17.5 / 95.0 px** on actor routes. I inspected saved red-prediction/green-projection overlays at original image size; the points often land near the same groove, but endpoints can be clipped or occluded. These labels and scores do not establish that the requested port is visibly identifiable.

I am **not connecting this model to automatic handoff**. The next distinct test should use a local board/groove cue on actual autonomous approach images and require reviewed evidence that the requested numbered slot, rather than a neighbor, can be identified or safely rejected. True visibility and safe-pause labels are still needed. The transport arrival gate also remains open, so insertion and RL remain disabled.
