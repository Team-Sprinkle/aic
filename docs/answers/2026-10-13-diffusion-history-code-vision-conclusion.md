# What worked in SC transport diffusion, and what to try next

## Conclusion in plain words

The changes made the actor imitate recorded demonstrations better, and some made it travel farther toward the SC port. **None made it reliably complete the transport route on its own.** On matched development starts, the strongest older dense-history actor and the new cadence-matched actor both reached **0/3 safe approach bands and 0/3 insertions**. The new actor's three closest distances to the port opening were about 142, 170 and 143 mm; the older actor's were 141, 190 and 129 mm. These are 70-second transport checks, not full-length tests on every teacher-duration scene.

## Did the code prevent effective diffusion training?

I found **two code defects**, and separated them from the older model's failure:

1. For the *new* 0.9 s cache, the auxiliary “about one second ahead” label skipped the 0.9 s observation and selected 1.8 s, then rejected it. That made the initial 0.9 s diagnostic train with **zero** valid progress labels. I fixed selection of the nearest future observation in the allowed interval and added a coverage guard, then trained again to a plateau and ran the valid Gazebo comparison. The earlier 0.6 s models had valid progress labels; this does **not** explain their failure.
2. An empty optional sampler-step environment variable caused the first new Gazebo launch to fail before its policy issued commands. I fixed the runtime fallback and reran all three scenes. I also fixed a stale previous-command token after long cache gaps; these SC models deliberately mask previous commands, so that correction does not change their results.

The important paths I checked appear internally consistent: recorded 12-step SC target poses are relative to the **decision-time TCP frame**, the first four targets reconstruct with essentially zero translation error, and live execution converts targets back to current-body commands. Replaying saved live images and state reproduced ten model proposals within 0.065 mm maximum. The diffusion noise/velocity equations and sampling update are algebraically consistent. This does not prove all control behavior correct, but a gross action-frame or live-preprocessing bug is unlikely in the checked run.

The architecture has a limitation: it receives three **globally pooled** frozen ResNet18 camera vectors. Pooling removes much of *where* a card or port appears. It has a 384D history transformer and 384D action denoiser, about 14.29M trainable parameters, but no explicit route-stage or safe-waypoint prediction used to steer the actor. The auxiliary short-route goal is learned from teacher motion and predicted at runtime. Repeated sampling seeds changed the first target by only 0.81 mm median on a held-out check; this learned policy is almost deterministic for a given observation despite using diffusion. The model reacts to vision (scrambling images moved actions by 9.76 mm median), but that does not tell us whether it reacts in the *right* direction.

## Would DINOv2 be a better vision encoder?

Possibly for a future spatial policy, but the current evidence says **do not make it the default yet**. I used the local official pretrained DINOv2-S weights on the same three SC images as the ResNet cache. A bounded, episode-grouped test had 83 training and 21 held-out episodes. For predicting the next roughly one-second TCP route from one observation, a simple state-and-task probe had 24.4 mm p95 error; adding pooled ResNet gave 31.9 mm, pooled DINO 35.3 mm, and a 4×4 DINO patch grid 31.5 mm. This is a linear, single-frame probe: it cannot rule out a nonlinear temporal DINO policy. It does show no ready-made gain from a simple encoder swap. Prior DINO selected-port perception experiments likewise did not establish reliable native-view target identity.

The local DINOv2-S checkpoint has 22.06M parameters versus 11.18M for ResNet18; substituting it would grow the total route actor from about 25.47M to 36.35M parameters. Encoder-only p95 on three processed views was about 6.66 ms DINO versus 3.43 ms ResNet. Both exclude preprocessing and denoising, and the current full 12-step actor already exceeds the 300 ms goal. The [official DINOv2 model card](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov2/blob/main/MODEL_CARD.md) describes its ViT-S/14 patch representation; our route numbers come from the [local matched probe](</home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-13_sc_diffusion_code_audit/dinov2_vs_resnet_route_probe_spatial.json>).

## Next direction

The first substantial teacher/actor route departure still occurs at **6–9 seconds**, even when the actor is later able to memorize teacher frames. The next high-value test is a **small, safe actor-state continuation pilot**: let the frozen actor reach an early off-route state on three *training* resets; from each measured state, ask the SC Low Lateral Bypass planner for a cable-safe route around and below the cards, execute it in Gazebo, and retain only successful no-contact continuations. Actor-prefix images are context; only the continuation commands become expert action labels. Stop the pilot unless at least two of three complete the safe route. If feasible, vary card count, port and start, train **one shared actor** on the old routes plus corrective continuations versus a same-budget control, and test on new complete-reset development scenes. Require at least two of three safe approach arrivals and p95 inference below 300 ms before scaling up. If this fails, test explicit observed route-progress and short safe-waypoint control before another larger visual backbone or more generic BC.
