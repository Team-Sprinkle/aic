# Result of the five-step handoff decision tree

I ran the original-resolution crop comparison and followed its failure branch through the bounded diagnosis and one targeted repair. **The gate still fails, so I stopped before the integrated handoff, insertion actor and full rollouts.** Those later steps require a verified safe handoff; running them now would not test the planned system.

For **SC**, a 160×160 crop from each original camera image was chosen by the RGB locator. A small orientation head improved the matched four-reset near-port p95 error from **4.62° to 3.70°**, but the required limit is **3°**. Moving the crop to the *true* port center for a posthoc diagnostic made p95 **3.97°**, so centering is not the demonstrated cause. The one planned repair kept spatial image features instead of only pooled features; it scored **3.95°**. The most difficult held-out resets remained above 4°.

For **SFP**, a 320×320 original-image crop around both ports on the requested card improved a same-card candidate verifier. On two separate five-reset evaluation halves, it accepted **66/87** and **147/159** correct constructed three-camera candidate triplets, versus **41/87** and **71/159** for the frozen low-resolution verifier. It still accepted **one wrong triplet in each half**. Requiring both verifiers removed those false accepts but retained only **39/87** and **62/159** correct triplets. Marked images show one camera sometimes confidently associates a candidate **150–243 pixels** from the requested opening while the other two views are correct. These are offline candidate tests, not autonomous handoffs.

**Status of the five steps:** Step 1 failed; Step 2 identified that SC crop centering and moving labels do not explain the gap, and its single spatial-feature repair failed. Steps 3–5 remain gated. There was no new Gazebo collection, automatic switch, insertion-actor attachment, RL training or final-scene evaluation. Complete live latency was not measured because accuracy and identity had already failed.
