# Transport: what we already tried, and a different next experiment

**Yes, we already tried collecting demonstrations after the actor failed.** My earlier wording made that sound new; it was not. The missing part is a *reliable training signal at the decision where the route first goes wrong*, with evidence that following it is safe for the cable. More of the same actor-to-CheatCode takeovers would repeat a failed experiment.

## What the prior failure-case work actually tested

| Attempt | What happened | What it established |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actor runs first, CheatCode takes over, then BC learns the continuation | A causally checked 10-train/3-development, five-second-prefix fit reduced held-out correction-action p95 **2.897→2.338 mm**, but **both actors reached 0/3 safe buffers** on truly new SFP starts. A later six-train/three-development 15-second cycle improved late-action p95 only **3.086→2.978 mm**, below its 10% gate. | We did expose the learner to teacher actions after actor drift. The improved teacher-frame imitation did not repair early autonomous route choice. [Actor-prefix experiment](../experiments/2026-10-07-sfp-early-branch-support.md). |
| Give the actor a predicted requested-port offset | Two matched fits showed effectively no gain; zeroing the pose input changed actions only **0.032 mm p95**. At the first corrective action the pose was accepted in only **3/6 training and 1/3 development** takeovers. | The current action loss can ignore the pose, and the usable pose was often missing at the exact intervention point. [Pose-use diagnosis](../experiments/2026-10-07-sfp-early-branch-support.md#route-feedback-ablation-result-the-pose-is-visible-but-this-actor-ignores-it) · [coverage audit](../experiments/2026-10-08-sfp-pose-actor-training-method.md). |
| Predict a short future waypoint from teacher motion | One-second waypoint error improved on ordinary teacher frames, but its executed-command direction agreed with teacher recovery on **0/6 actor-to-teacher boundaries**. This is related to the classic [DAgger idea](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html): train on states the learned policy visits. Our actor-prefix work sampled those states, but did **not** establish a safe, informative expert decision at the first wrong branch. The teacher's first command often held position while its controller initialized. Calling the prior work “no failure-case demonstrations” would be inaccurate.

## Ideas that are genuinely different

### 1. Compare *short route choices* at the same actor state, rather than copy one next action

At a saved actor state just before a wrong branch, generate a small set of **complete 2–4 second route candidates**: the actor's proposal, a teacher-derived continuation after settling, and one or two physically distinct detours. Recreate the same robot and cable state in Gazebo, execute each candidate, and label its **measured progress toward the requested port, contact force, and cable/card interference**. Train a small route-ranking or progress-and-risk head from public images, task code, measured state/history, and the candidate route. At deployment it ranks candidates and the **same low-level actor** executes the chosen route; it is not a separate actor for transport versus recovery. Privileged geometry can score training rollouts, but must not enter the runtime ranker.

This differs from our prior paired-request loss, which compared **different requested ports** and first actions at similar observations. Here the request and starting state stay fixed while **alternative routes and their actual outcomes** differ. It also differs from rigid MoveIt approval: Gazebo cable/contact outcome is part of the label. A ranker could learn that a slightly longer route is preferable when the direct route catches the cable, without requiring the simulator to provide a unique “correct” first action.

The risk is reproducibility: if the actor prefix cannot reproduce comparable cable shape across candidate replays, the labels are confounded. **First gate:** on six existing nonfinal actor states, verify replayed TCP and visible cable shape are comparable; find at least four states with two meaningfully different route outcomes and no unsafe candidate called safe by the scoring rule. If this fails, do not train a ranker. Then train/test with whole reset groups separated and compare candidate choice with a state-only/shortest-route control. A later live gate requires ≥2/3 safe buffered arrivals on three new nonfinal starts, no extra contact, and complete inference p95 <300 ms. This is my most distinct proposal; it needs no video-prediction world model.

### 2. Make the already planned actor-state label test *stage-aware and cable-checked*

Before a new BC fit, use the saved successful CheatCode continuations to identify the **first sustained, meaningful measured movement after settling**, then label a short *route segment* rather than the teacher's first hold command or a point at exactly one second. Preserve the preceding actor camera/state history. Check that the segment stays on the intended side of cards and does not create cable/NIC-card force. Require **20 training and six held-out complete reset states** with safe continuations and at least **5/6** held-out first corrective directions aligned with measured safe motion before any fit. This is already the [October 9 planned gate](../experiments/2026-10-09-sfp-cable-side-route-repair.md#decision-and-next-bounded-hypothesis), **not a wholly new idea**. It is still the cheapest immediate test and may make the route-ranking experiment unnecessary if it produces clean labels and live improvement.

### 3. Test whether cable state is actually observable in our policy input

The October 9 replays showed similar rigid-arm routes with different cable/card force outcomes. If the selected action depends on cable slack or which side of a card the cable lies, RGB/state history must carry that information. A small **frozen-feature diagnostic** could predict the next few seconds' high-force cable/card event from the existing three-camera history and a proposed route, compared with robot-state-only and route-only controls. If the visual model cannot distinguish outcomes on episode-grouped tests, a larger actor alone will not fix that ambiguity; try a better side view, a cable keypoint/occupancy head, or a brief safe observation maneuver. This is a diagnostic before new architecture training, not a commitment to a world model. It is motivated by the local force/contact evidence; no cable-aware outcome predictor has been validated in this repo.

## My recommendation

Run **idea 2 as a bounded label-quality gate first**, because it reuses existing recordings and is explicitly distinct from the failed one-second/20-mm labels. If it passes, compare one stage-aware full-action actor with the frozen control on new starts. If it fails, **stop collecting ordinary takeovers** and move to **idea 1**, beginning with the six-state replay-fidelity and route-outcome gate. Run idea 3 as a small read-only observability check in parallel with the route-outcome audit. Do not turn on automatic handoff, insertion or RL until autonomous transport clears the safe-buffer/contact gate. No final scenes should be opened.

These are proposed experiments, not demonstrated improvements. The historical counts and gates are in the linked records.
