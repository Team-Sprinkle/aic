# Next steps after the live shadow handoff

The immediate priority is to turn the promising **SC transport pause** into a trustworthy handoff to insertion. Four new SC starts, then four matched faster repeats, all reached an accepted pause near the requested opening. That is a useful screen, but it did not test hard occlusion, a consistently mistaken neighboring port, or insertion. No full episode inserted. The SFP stop is a separate known failure: its current handoff head was trained on SC labels and falsely paused 162 mm from the SFP target in the bounded diagnostic.

## Recommended order

1. **Challenge the frozen SC verifier with hard negatives.** Reuse saved native RGB first. Review examples where the requested mouth is hidden by the gripper or cable, offscreen, physically absent, or confused with the other SC port. Include the *same images with different requested-port codes* so a visible neighbor cannot stand in for the target. Score accepted wrong/unsafe pauses and retained clear correct pauses by complete reset group. Keep simulator geometry out of the runtime verifier; use it only for post-run scoring. Stop and repair any reproducible false accept before connecting insertion. If this replay passes, run a small new-scene shadow screen across both ports and card counts. Zero known false accepts, useful clear-arrival recall, safe force/contact, and complete live timing below 300 ms are the gate; a small zero-false sample is a screen, not proof of reliability.

2. **Measure the full SC pause-to-command path.** The 176 ms p95 result times the selected-port verifier only. The transport proposal, fresh-frame hold, handoff conversion, insertion actor, and controller command have not yet been timed together. Profile p50/p95/p99 on live runs. If the combined decision exceeds 300 ms, remove duplicated visual encoding or move work to the pause interval without accepting stale images. Preserve the current fast verifier as the reference.

3. **Attach the SC insertion actor in a bounded comparison.** Start from *actual autonomous transport handoff poses*, not privileged near-port resets. Replay the frozen insertion actor's first commands there. Earlier local actors sometimes retreated or moved laterally away even with an accurate port estimate, so repair that specific mapping if it recurs. Compare frozen actor versus a targeted pose-conditioned continuation with the same starts and update budget. The actor receives only the verified image-derived requested-port pose. Measure full insertion depth, port identity, axial/lateral/orientation error, force, contact, and timing. A good transport handoff is not an insertion success.

4. **Repair SFP arrival separately, using existing data first.** Keep one shared transport actor but give its stop decision task-appropriate supervision. Derive SFP positive pauses near safe selected-port standoffs and negatives from early route states, wrong-port views, and failed approaches in existing episode-grouped trajectories. Fit or calibrate a small SFP-specific stop head on the frozen shared representation, then test it on held-out complete resets. Reject another early pause like the observed 162 mm error. Only after SFP arrives near the selected port should the frozen same-card identity and cross-camera verifier be tested live. Do not collect routine new SFP rollouts until a specific data gap is shown.

5. **Test complete episodes and recovery only after supervised insertion works.** Once both families have reliable autonomous transport, selected-port verification, and insertion, compare normal policy, local backoff, and hierarchical backoff on actual blocked/contact episodes. Then consider warm-started online RL for behavior after backoff. Keep final scenes sealed until the development gates pass; do not use RL to cover a broken arrival or identity signal.

I would start with **Step 1, then Step 2**. They are bounded checks on the current frozen models and decide whether SC is ready for insertion-actor work. In parallel, the SFP label audit in Step 4 can proceed from existing data, but SFP live switching remains disabled until its arrival head passes a held-out gate.
