# Ordered SFP transport continuation — 2026-10-07

## What I did, in order

1. **Checked whether failed actor routes were recoverable.** On three already-open development scenes, the unchanged actor ran for 15 seconds and privileged CheatCode took over. All three takeovers fully inserted without contact penalties. This shows the states were recoverable; it does **not** count as autonomous actor success. I also collected six separate training takeovers across 2/3/5 cards and both requested ports; all six fully inserted. Actor-prefix observations were retained as context, while only post-takeover teacher actions were supervised.
2. **Tested more late correction data under a matched BC fit.** Both arms trained for 2,000 updates. On three held-out late continuations, first-action p95 error improved from 3.086 to 2.978 mm, or 3.5%. The fixed gate required at least 10%, so this checkpoint was not promoted and the reserved new live starts were not opened.
3. **Checked whether vision could find the requested port while the actor was off route.** Frozen selected-port perception, using public task identity, RGB and measured TCP state, accepted the correct port before the safe approach band in all three saved actor routes, with no wrong-port accept in the sampled observations. Accepted 3D position error was 3.10 mm p95. The warmed saved-image pipeline took 91.9 ms p95; a cold first call took 4.29 s, and complete live ROS latency has not been verified. [Visual overlay: green prediction, red posthoc ground truth](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-07_sfp_route_branch_support_v1/offroute_port_shadow_v1/first_accepted_prediction_vs_truth.jpg). Ground-truth geometry was used only after inference to score and draw the overlay.
4. **Tested the predicted port offset as an actor input.** A frozen perception pass produced 19,955 original-image SFP cache rows. A fixed audit of 100 accepted estimates across 90 episodes found zero wrong requested-port accepts and 2.39 mm 3D error p95. Two matched 2,000-update transformer comparisons used the same full-action architecture, data and update budget in each pair. The control received zero in the pose channel; the candidate received only the frozen predicted offset. The first comparison worsened held-out late-action p95 from 2.849 to 2.906 mm. A second comparison included the same six late teacher continuations in both arms and changed p95 from 2.95322 to 2.95296 mm, effectively no gain. Zeroing accepted pose inputs changed candidate actions by only 0.032 mm p95, below the fixed 0.1 mm use-of-input gate.

## Conclusion and limits

The visual model can often estimate the requested port on these saved routes, but this simple additive pose input did not make the supervised transport actor correct its route. The actor still barely responds to the pose. Only 112 of 333 cached new training teacher rows and 50 of 166 held-out late teacher rows had an accepted pose; the actor-prefix rows have no expert action targets. These may contribute, but the experiments do not isolate one cause. The last independent autonomous comparison remains **0/3 safe buffered arrivals for both arms, and 0/3 insertions**. No new autonomous rollout was run after the failed offline gates. Automatic handoff, insertion and RL remain disabled; final scenes remain sealed.

## Next bounded experiment

Before collecting more routine episodes, test one **structural route-control objective** using the existing grouped data: make the full-action actor explicitly predict target-relative route error or a short route waypoint/progress variable, then decode its complete TCP-body action chunk from that prediction. This is a new full-action design, not an action-delta adapter. First verify that the first privileged correction action at takeover joins causally to the actor's preceding images, task code and measured state. Use the same frozen perception predictions and a matched action-only control, with at most 2,000 updates per arm and one predeclared held-out episode split.

Continue to at most three unopened nonfinal live starts **only if** held-out late-action p95 improves by at least 10%, accepted-pose ablation changes actions by at least 0.1 mm p95, useful-action direction stays at least 80%, ordinary SFP/SC action p95 regresses by no more than 10%, and complete warmed inference p95 is below 300 ms. Promote only for at least 2/3 safe buffered arrivals with no worse contact. If the offline gates fail again, change the supervision/route architecture rather than gathering more copies of the same trajectories. Keep final scenes sealed.
