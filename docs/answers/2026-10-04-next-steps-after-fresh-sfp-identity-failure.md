# Next steps after the fresh SFP perception check

The immediate problem is **choosing the correct numbered socket**, not getting a high-resolution crop. In ten fresh scenes, an observation-based crop contained the requested opening early in all ten, but learned heads sometimes chose a neighboring card or the other port on the same card. Three cameras can agree on the *same wrong socket*. The four-head union is therefore disabled. The later same-card check was designed on those scenes and reached only 5/10 early anchors, so it is diagnostic only.

## One bounded perception repair

1. **Freeze the failure list.** Save representative correct and wrong predictions by card, port, camera, route phase and whether the opening is actually identifiable. Preserve the ten recent scenes as development examples; do not call a model tuned on them independently validated.

2. **Train identity before precision.** Use full original images and the public task code to identify the requested *rail slot*, including empty rail positions, then identify port 0 versus port 1 on that card. Explicitly supervise the board/rail layout and both openings of every present card. Train neighboring-card and other-port examples as hard negatives. Only after the slot is selected should an original-resolution crop refine the opening. This differs from the previous all-slot head: it must prove the card/port association, rather than merely output ten keypoint channels. Simulator geometry supplies training labels only; deployment receives images, measured robot state, calibration and task code.

3. **Keep the attempt small.** Start with the existing verified RGB and the ten diagnostic scenes, one GPU, at most about **2,000 updates or two hours**, and one matched ablation against the frozen selected-only head. No broad hyperparameter sweep or routine new training collection. If development errors remain coherent neighbor swaps, stop this design instead of adding more weak heads to an OR union.

4. **Use a genuinely new evaluation split.** Freeze weights, crop/acceptance rules and thresholds first. Then generate at most **20 new non-final resets**, two per requested card/port code, with different complete scene and robot-start hashes; leave reserved final scenes sealed. Require **zero accepted wrong ports**, at least **18/20** anchors within 3 s with coverage for every request code, and accepted coarse 3D p95 ≤20 mm. Report abstentions and results by card, port, phase and occlusion. If the target is not identifiable, the model must return invalid; a safe observation-only view change or sparse visual history is the next bounded hypothesis if this repair fails. Do not relax the gate on these same scenes.

5. **Only then connect the control stages.** Measure complete live camera-to-decision p50/p95/p99; p95 must be <300 ms. Separately repair autonomous SFP transport, which previously reached **0/3** safe requested-port buffers. A good perception score does not fix that route problem. Once transport reaches the correct nearby port and the new identity gate accepts it safely, retrain and evaluate the near-port PoseInsert actor. Start RL only after supervised autonomous transport and insertion work often enough to provide useful recovery states and rewards.

The practical next action is **Step 1 followed by one Step 2 model attempt**. Its stop rule is explicit: if it cannot remove neighbor swaps while preserving early coverage, shift to active viewing or sparse identity memory rather than adding more models to the union. The current results and artifacts are in [the fresh SFP experiment](../experiments/2026-10-04-sfp-fresh-anchor-shadow.md).
