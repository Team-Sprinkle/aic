# Next suggestion after the card-identity repairs

**Run a visual identifiability gate before training another perception model.** The failure is now specific: different cards can look alike, and a wrong neighboring card can produce a confident, three-camera-consistent, temporally stable pose. Adding paired point losses or a TCP/rail coordinate prior did not fix it. The trained single-point crop detector remains the safest offline baseline, but its early availability varied between scene sets (6/10 and 10/10). Automatic handoff is still off.

1. Freeze a small, balanced set of existing **original RGB** approach views: all ten requested SFP card/port codes, sparse and full-card boards, the known neighbor swaps, early and near-port times, and a few autonomous transport frames. Give reviewers the requested code and three cameras, first as a single time point and then with distance-spaced history. Ask them to mark the requested card, its two openings, and whether each is visible, occluded or genuinely ambiguous. Keep simulator projections hidden during this first pass; reveal them afterward to audit the labels. This tests whether the needed evidence is in the observations at all. Budget: about 30–50 triplets, using saved images only.
2. **If reviewers can reliably identify the card**, train a small candidate-association classifier using full-row context plus the existing native crop. Its question is “does this proposed opening belong to the requested numbered card?” Train with same-board neighboring-card candidates as hard negatives; allow an *uncertain/abstain* output. Keep the existing single-point pose head for precise localization. Use complete reset splits and new nonfinal groups for one frozen evaluation. Require zero accepted wrong cards, an early usable pose in at least 9/10 groups, accepted 3D p95 ≤20 mm, and complete live inference p95 <300 ms before trying automatic handoff.
3. **If the card cannot be identified from the current views**, first test a collision-checked observation move that exposes a board end and the candidate card row. Compare before/after identity on the same resets and measure added time/contact. A camera mounted on the moving tool is more constrained than the separate movable camera used in [Vision in Action](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.15666), so the view motion must be tested in our simulator rather than copied from that paper. Only train on active-view data if the view actually resolves the ambiguity.

Separately, repair **transport safe arrival**: the latest autonomous actor reached the requested buffered region in 0/3 starts. A perfect port detector cannot hand off a route that never reaches the region. I would use the visual review to decide the perception branch first, then run a bounded transport diagnostic on balanced autonomous starts before combining the two. I would continue to defer insertion-policy RL until both prerequisites pass.
