# Transport versus learned recovery: priority after released qualification evaluation

I recommend improving transport and its safe rule-based retreat first, then training recovery from short episodes. The frozen official evaluation scored98.83/300: trial1inserted, while trials2/3never reached precise insertion. Trial2stopped at an immediate force guard when an intervening NIC card contacted a cable link, so a learned retry would not have been invoked. Trial3did invoke measured-path backtracking, but a **visual-acquisition timeout** was incorrectly classified as blockage; it retraced322waypoints in about44.75simulation seconds and exhausted the controller wall-clock budget during its alternate route.

A useful first comparison is a frozen baseline versus a more robust route selector and a bounded, faster measured-path return, on new varied development scenes chosen from the pre-release task specification. Measure correct requested-port acquisition, insertion, force, complete time, route choice and retreat safety. Do not adjust rules using the released official scenes. If card-route failures remain, collect learner-induced recovery starts with preceding path/cable-camera history, force, checkpoint, attempted corridor and successful alternate route; a few contact-only frames may omit the information needed to choose a different way around cards. Train and evaluate by complete episode/reset groups. Near-port rim-contact recovery episodes become useful after the route reliably reaches the port.

This is a project-specific inference from the causal run; it is not a claim that learning recovery cannot work. Research supports combining obstacle-aware transport with a learned insertion skill ([Yamada et al. 2024](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v242/yamada24a.html)), aggregating expert actions at states the current policy actually reaches ([Ross et al. 2011](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v15/ross11a.html)), and sample-efficient off-policy RL with demonstrations and reset mechanisms for later contact recovery ([SERL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.16013)). None of these papers validates our specific cable route or recovery threshold.
