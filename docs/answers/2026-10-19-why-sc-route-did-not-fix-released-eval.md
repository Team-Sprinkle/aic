# Why the SC route change did not fix the released evaluation

We tested `sc skip back` with early tilt three times in a **local replay** of the publicly released three-trial qualification configuration. The route was active inside the container. Scores were **98.73, 98.75 and 98.62 out of 300**. In every run, SFP trial 1 inserted; SFP trial 2 and SC trial 3 did not. The previous replay was 98.75/300, so full-task performance did not improve. [Frozen run and artifacts](../experiments/2026-10-19-sc-early-tilt-released-three-repeat.md).

The route had passed a narrower test: **22/24** SC cases generated from the predeadline public sample reached a correct-port visual handoff. Those trials stopped at handoff, around 150 mm from the opening. They did not test the remaining approach or insertion. The replay also used faster V56 controller gains than the development pilot. That difference may matter, but the score does not establish it as the cause.

The SC score says **“Task not completed”** and reports brief force peaks above 20 N: 26.23, 22.69 and 74.92 N, each for less than one second. The separate “No contact detected” score category does not rule out these force events. The engine log only says the task remained pending. We therefore cannot tell from official feedback whether the cable caught, visual handoff failed, or the final insertion was blocked.

**Repair plan:** on new sample-derived development scenes, compare the same route at the slower pilot gains and the faster V56 gains, measuring public force, TCP progress and the stage reached. Cover starts among the cards, multiple card counts and both SC targets. If route progress fails, adjust SC-only control or use a measured-force/progress-triggered alternate route. If the route succeeds but visual handoff or insertion fails, repair that stage instead. Demand full insertions on representative development cases before another frozen released-config score check. Handle SFP trial 2 separately; an SC route cannot fix it.

**Officially available feedback:** the [toolkit's local guide](https://github.com/intrinsic-dev/aic/blob/main/docs/getting_started.md) says the terminal shows trial progression, per-trial scores and a final summary, saved under `$HOME/aic_results/` or `$AIC_RESULTS_DIR`. Our local `scoring.yaml` gives validity, completion/insertion, force, duration, efficiency and smoothness categories. The [submission guide](https://github.com/intrinsic-dev/aic/blob/main/docs/submission.md) says the portal's “My Submissions” page offers results and logs, and the leaderboard offers results. It does not promise exact port error, contact pairs, policy stage, camera recordings or a diagnosis of non-completion. Our local videos, oracle contacts and detailed runtime traces are **extra instrumentation, not official portal feedback**, and were not used to tune this released-scene route.
