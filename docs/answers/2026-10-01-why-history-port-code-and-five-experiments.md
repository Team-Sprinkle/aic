# Why the longer history was not deployed, and what the port-code test means

Date: 2026-10-01. Completed answer and bounded experiment result.

## Longer observation history

The earlier shared transport policy used the current three camera images and state, with no route-length observation history. I **did** subsequently train an offline temporal head on up to 16 seconds of observations, actions, and measured motion. I did **not** deploy it: the held-out SC next-route-subgoal translation error was about 39.5 mm at the 95th percentile, against the agreed 20 mm gate, so it had not demonstrated reliable route awareness. I should have said this plainly when reporting the earlier work. The earlier temporal head also reused the failed policy's fused 256-dimensional visual context, which could hide information before temporal fusion. I have now trained and **deployed for bounded development evaluation** a new 60-second causal history model with separate ImageNet features from all three cameras.

## What 0.4–0.5 mm means

On the **same held-out teacher image and measured robot state**, I changed only the selected SC port in the public one-hot task code and compared the checkpoint's **first 50 ms TCP target**. The median translation change was only about 0.4–0.5 mm. This does **not** mean its final intended destinations are separated by 0.5 mm. We did not roll both task codes to their endpoints in that measurement. It does mean the first step is only weakly sensitive to which SC port was requested. The measurement covered the start, midpoint, and 90% through all 21 held-out SC teacher episodes; medians were 0.37, 0.49, and 0.48 mm, respectively. Persistent weak response late in the route makes the concern stronger than an early shared-movement explanation, but a same-image code swap alone cannot prove the model would choose the wrong final port in closed loop. See `outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_sc_llb_cleaned_bc/transport_task_sensitivity_teacher_frames.json`.

## Decisive frozen-checkpoint check

On an existing, non-final SFP-to-NIC validation scene, the shared checkpoint moved from 184.4 mm to a nearest 28.8 mm TCP distance from the selected opening, then ended 80.0 mm out. The teacher's **pre-insertion TCP standoff** is itself about 56 mm from the opening, so opening distance alone should not be treated as a transport pass/fail measure. The original actor finished **129.9 mm away from that teacher standoff**, and task completion was zero. The live actor used only ordinary images, state, and task encoding; fixed port geometry was used afterward to measure distance. Inference p95 was 94.1 ms. See the [experiment record](../experiments/2026-10-01-transport-five-way-comparison.md), `outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_sfp_transport_decisive/decisive_metrics.json`, and `clean_cap20_run/decisive_contact_sheet.jpg`.

## Results from all five requested experiments

All candidates used the verified 83/21 SC and 236/32 SFP complete-episode split and the same three-camera frozen ImageNet features, TCP-body complete action targets, state, task code, and timestamps. Teacher route labels were used for training only. No final scenes, simulator geometry input, RL, or new training data were used.

The shared no-history head had held-out first-target p95 errors of **2.32 mm SC / 2.31 mm SFP**. The 60-second history head had **2.27 mm SC / 2.62 mm SFP**. Thus offline action imitation alone showed no clear overall win. The explicit route head recognized SC stages 92% of the time but missed the next route subgoal by **44.5 mm p95**, above the 20 mm gate, and made action imitation worse. The diffusion transformer produced about **12–13 mm p95** first-target error; even a hidden-label oracle choosing the best of four samples would remain at about 8–10 mm p95. Its latency was acceptable, but its actions were not. At equal total updates, task-specific actors did worse; at 3,000 updates per actor they approached but still did not beat the shared head. The new no-history/history heads reacted even less to an SC selected-port code swap at the same image/state (roughly 0.10–0.14 mm at late teacher frames), so task grounding remains weak.

The important result came from **live** matched development runs. On one SFP scene, the 60-second-history head finished **8.0 mm** from the teacher's pre-insertion TCP standoff, compared with **315 mm** for the matched new no-history head and **130 mm** for the original frozen actor. It still had **6.9° orientation error**, about **8.0 mm lateral error**, and zero insertion. It sat near the pre-insertion plane without completing local motion. A separately trained SFP-only head finished **34 mm** from that same standoff. On an already-open SC five-card scene, the shared history model got **worse**: 158 mm terminal standoff error versus 85 mm for its no-history match; an SC-only head finished **182 mm** out. All SC models failed insertion. The history benefit is therefore **SFP-specific in these tests**, not evidence that shared transport is solved. Live p95 inference stayed below 300 ms (about 98 ms SFP, 187 ms SC for the shared history actor).

I also fixed a live temporal-input bug exposed in an initial failed test: runtime supplied one 9D previous action while training expected the prior 36D four-command proposal. The failed attempt is preserved; corrected SFP and SC comparisons used the full previous chunk. Full commands, checkpoints, metrics, plots, traces, videos, and limits are in the [experiment record](../experiments/2026-10-01-transport-five-way-comparison.md).

**Current decision:** keep history as a candidate for SFP transport, continue to block autonomous insertion and RL, and repair the late SC route decision and SFP orientation/lateral handoff before promotion. The route, diffusion, and task-specific ablations do not yet justify replacing the shared actor.
