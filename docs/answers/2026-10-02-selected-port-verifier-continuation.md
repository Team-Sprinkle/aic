# Selected-port continuation: what changed and what remains

I continued the approved two-actor handoff work. No new actor, RL, or world-model training was started, and no reserved final scene was opened.

## Results

- On **three new SC development resets** covering requested ports 0 and 1, a frozen coarse RGB locator plus a frozen original-resolution 160×160 crop refiner put the selected port inside its image crop in **147/147 camera views**. In 15, 16, and 18 reviewed clear approach frames, the triangulated port-frame lateral p95 errors were **0.230, 0.175, and 0.149 mm**. This is a clear-view position diagnostic, not an insertion result. [Images, per-frame results, and commands](../experiments/2026-10-02-selected-port-handoff-execution.md#continuation-native-sc-crop-second-target-and-grouped-visibility-review).
- I tested an observation-only idea for rejecting a wrong visual target: require all three camera predictions to agree when reprojected, with all frozen in-frame scores at least 0.5. Using an exploratory 2 px disagreement limit, it accepted **49/49 reviewed clear SC triplets** from the three new resets and **0/20 provisionally hidden** examples from an older three-scene review. Three high-score hidden cases disagreed by over 18 px; a fourth had only two views, which agreed despite being wrong. This is a promising diagnostic, **not a validated safe handoff**. The threshold was chosen on development data; hidden labels have one reviewer and mostly easy offscreen cases. [Exact machine result](/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-02_selected_port_handoff/sc_selected_port_clearview_diagnostic_v4.json).
- An original-RGB near-contact montage shows the plug overlapping the requested opening in both new port-0 resets near 18 mm TCP-to-opening distance. I left those views **uncertain** as visibility labels. On eight triplets from the three new resets, the exploratory rule accepted **2/2 clearly visible port-1** views and **1/6 uncertain plug-overlapped port-0** views. The uncertain view is not a scored false accept; it shows the visibility definition still matters. [Montage](/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-02_selected_port_handoff/sc_new_scene_near_contact_audit_v1.jpg) · [per-triplet record](/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-02_selected_port_handoff/sc_new_scene_near_contact_consistency_v2.json).
- The three-camera coarse-plus-crop component took **23.57 and 31.55 ms p95** in two warmed five-card tests. Those times exclude camera transport, JPEG decode, ROS, and both actors, so complete live inference still needs measurement.

## Decision and next bounded experiment

Only the SC **clear-view position** subtest passes. The existing transport arrival refit still fails the frozen SC route comparison; the safe verifier, visibility under hard occlusion, SC target identity under wrong-port requests, SFP perception, new-scene orientation, complete live latency, and autonomous full insertion remain open. No handoff or policy checkpoint was promoted.

Next, use original RGB from complete **new development scene groups** to challenge the proposed verifier with selected-port occlusion, a visible neighbor but hidden requested port, and the saved SFP wrong-port case. Label each camera as identifiable, unidentifiable, or uncertain using the original image; keep uncertain views out of the binary gate calculation. Replay the frozen transport pauses and measure **false accepted handoffs and retained valid arrivals**, grouped by reset. Budget: at most one workday for checked labels and diagnostic evaluation, at most two GPU-hours for inference, and no policy retraining. Continue to actor integration only if there are **zero known wrong-port/hidden accepted handoffs** while valid visible arrivals remain available and complete live p95 stays below 300 ms. If the requested opening cannot be labeled reliably or the verifier loses valid arrivals, repair the visibility definition or observation path before further training. Final scenes remain sealed.
