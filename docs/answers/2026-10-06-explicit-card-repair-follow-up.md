# Explicit requested-card repair follow-up

I tested two bounded repairs using the existing grouped SFP camera data and updated the main status, handoff, dataset, experiment and artifact documents. **Neither repair passed its development gate.** I did not enable automatic handoff, insertion or RL.

First, I trained a native-image crop model that predicts the selected card's rail anchor and **both** port openings together. This used one shared image backbone, 10,631 unique card/frame crops across 70 training reset groups, and the full 2,000-update budget. On ten separate development groups, its strict rail-and-pair check accepted only **5/119** observations and found an early pose in **2/10** groups. Those five accepted poses had low error, but five is too few to establish useful performance. The selected-port prediction alone had 36 accepted points with zero wrong; requiring three correct point predictions mostly discarded them. An earlier full-image pair model had failed for a similar coverage reason, so I stopped this branch rather than loosening the guard on the same scenes.

Second, I tested whether measured TCP and the board model's RGB-predicted rail positions could estimate where the requested opening should lie in each image. The small prior trained on 56,074 train request-camera views for 2,000 updates. On 355 held-out development views it missed the projected opening by **111 px p95**, and was closer to the requested opening than every neighboring same-port opening in **92.96%** of views. It missed the fixed **30 px / 95%** feasibility gate, so it cannot safely reject neighboring cards.

The existing single-point native crop detector remains the safest **offline comparison**. Its early availability varied between already-scored scene sets, and autonomous transport still missed its safe buffered arrival gate. The next distinct step is to review original full-row and crop images for whether the requested numbered card is actually identifiable, including occlusion and stable neighbor-card swaps. If it is, train a classifier that directly tests whether a candidate belongs to that card; if it is not, test a safe side view or route change before fitting another network. Use complete reset splits and a frozen abstention gate. The final scenes remain sealed.
