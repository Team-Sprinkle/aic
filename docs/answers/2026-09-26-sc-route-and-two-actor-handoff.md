## 1-2. What “motion planning” actually was

This was a **scripted waypoint teacher**, not MoveIt, OMPL, or a collision-search planner. [CableRouteDiagnostic.py](/data1/chmin/yj/ws_aic/src/aic/artifacts/prod_cheatcode_audit/CableRouteDiagnostic.py) reads exact card and selected-port transforms from Gazebo, calculates the above Cartesian TCP waypoints and offsets, and interpolates each leg with a smooth fifth-order curve at 20 Hz. It sends pose targets through the official Gazebo robot controller. The stock CheatCode or corrective teacher performs final alignment and insertion. Exact simulator geometry is used **only to generate teacher routes**; an autonomous actor never receives it. This waypoint method cannot guarantee collision-free cable motion, which is why the videos and physical checks matter.

## 2-2. Training perception and switching the two actors

There would be **two actors total, both shared across SFP-to-NIC and SC-to-SC** using the task one-hot code:

- **Transport actor:** sees the cameras, robot state, and selected-task code. It predicts complete TCP-body-frame targets while the selected opening is unseen or far away. Its SC training routes must first pass the cable-route checks.
- **Local actor:** sees those inputs plus the *visually predicted* plug-to-selected-port pose. It predicts complete port-frame targets for alignment and insertion. It does not output a correction to another actor's action.

The perception model gets the task code so it knows **which port** to find. “Visible” means enough of that selected opening is actually identifiable in a camera; merely projecting inside the image is insufficient if a cable, card, or robot hides it. Other visible ports are explicit wrong-target examples. During training, simulator geometry provides the selected port's pose and image projection, while depth or rendered masks determine occlusion. When it is offscreen or occluded, label that camera's visibility **0** and **mask its precise pose loss**; never use a fake zero pose. If another camera sees it, train the fused pose from that view. The current SC labeler checks projection in-frame but does **not yet** provide this full occlusion/visibility label.

“Confidence” should mean a **calibrated probability that the pose error is within the required tolerance**, measured against held-out scenes. It is separate from visibility: a visible port can still have a bad pose estimate. A proposed handoff checks the last five 200 ms decisions and enters the local actor only if at least four have a visible, confident, spatially consistent selected-port estimate near the opening. Prefer agreement from two cameras; require stronger evidence from one. Use hysteresis and measured motion to bridge a brief gripper occlusion, then return to transport if confidence stays low. These thresholds are a proposal to validate, not a trained switch. The [full design](/data1/chmin/yj/ws_aic/src/aic/docs/POSE_VISIBILITY_HANDOFF.md) has the labels, loss, and tests.

## 2-3. How many images?

My **starting budget**, not a guarantee, is about **100–200 distinct SC scene/reset configurations** across both selected ports, giving **5,000–15,000 temporally spaced timesteps**, or **15,000–45,000 three-camera images**. Reuse existing successful *and failed* episodes for perception labels, then fill measured gaps in offscreen, occluded, wrong-port, and near-opening views. Many adjacent frames from one run count for much less than new card layouts and starts. The deciding test is held-out scene-grouped visibility errors, false handoffs, and near-port pose accuracy against the 0.5 mm lateral corridor; if those fail, collect targeted scenes or revise the model. No perception or actor training was started in this route test.
