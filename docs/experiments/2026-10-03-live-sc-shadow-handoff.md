# Live SC shadow handoff, rejection, and orientation repair

**Date:** October 3, 2026. **Scope:** transport and shadow handoff only. No insertion actor, new policy training, RL, reserved final scene, or privileged actor input.

## Question and frozen setup

The preceding [geometric repair](2026-10-03-geometric-handoff-repair.md) passed offline SC board-angle and SFP candidate checks. This experiment asks whether a normal SC transport run can pause, inspect **fresh original camera images**, reject unsafe arrival, and safely resume. The transport actor, SC RGB locators, selected-port identity head, native opening refiner, orientation prior, and scene-independent training calibration were frozen. All actions and decisions use RGB, ordinary measured state, and public task code. Gazebo TF enters only the separate post-run scorer.

The new development scene generator used seed `20261003`, indexes 300–309, and the same legal SC reset ranges as the existing collection generator. Four scene/reset groups were selected **before** running the tests: `llb_0300` (port 0, one card), `llb_0303` (port 1, four cards), `llb_0304` (port 0, five cards), and `llb_0309` (port 1, five cards). The exact [selection manifest](/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-03_sc_shadow_handoff_v1/new_development_scenes/selected_shadow_development_manifest.json) carries scene hashes and marks them development-only. No final configurations were opened.

## Runtime decision

The existing late SC visual transport controller proposes a pause near its RGB-estimated selected-port standoff. On proposal, the runner holds the **measured** TCP pose, waits for camera images at least 200 ms newer in simulation time, and applies a selected-port verifier. The verifier requires three positive selected-port locator and spatial-request checks, a native-image opening estimate with ≤2 px three-camera reprojection, at least two reliable board-rail views within 1° of one another, TCP posture within 3° of the board-derived target, standoff within 15 mm, and measured force under 40 N. Invalid evidence returns `invalid` with a reason; it cannot fall back to the learned yaw. Rejected proposals resume transport after a short cooldown. At most three inspections are allowed in this diagnostic. A valid proposal only stops transport; it **does not** command insertion.

The first unchanged-model integration run on earlier development scene `llb_0122` rejected all three proposed pauses: RGB opening standoff was 1.2–1.7 mm, but RGB-derived posture error stayed near 4.5°. Post-run scored TF independently measured a real 4.5–4.6° TCP posture error; this was not a visual false alarm. The held plug tip was about 155 mm above the opening at this transport standoff. The controller's prior yaw was around −2.3°, while the observed board required about +3.3° TCP yaw in this reset. A targeted repair now accepts a reliable board estimate from a `wrong_tcp_posture` rejection as the next visual-servo orientation target, with a 2° per-decision rotation cap. It still requires **another fresh inspection** for acceptance and does not use simulator pose. The same-scene repeat corrected 4.47° to 0.17°, accepted at 1.93 mm predicted standoff, and had no scored contact.

## Latency and gate decision

The ordinary transport proposal p95 ranged **179–191 ms** across the four starts. The first live fresh-image verifier calls took **332, 351, 364, and 334 ms**, above the 300 ms requirement. Later checks in the five-card port-1 run took 251 and 227 ms. These verifier timings include native RGB JPEG roundtrip and perception, but the ordinary proposal timings and verifier timings are separate populations; neither includes the deliberate 200 ms wait for fresh simulation images. The first-call overrun is real and must not be hidden by an aggregate p95 over hundreds of ordinary transport decisions. A duplicate backbone pass was removed and CUDA models were warmed before the four new runs. A matched `llb_0300` replay with additional OpenCV Canny/Hough startup warmup still took **330 ms** on its first check. A stage-timed repeat took 312 ms: about 80 ms for three serial native crop refinements and 95 ms for three serial board searches. Batching the crop network and running all three independent camera searches concurrently reduced a further repeat to 300.03 ms, still without margin.

The final targeted latency repair removed the second lossy JPEG roundtrip **only for the shadow verifier**; the transport actor's image preprocessing did not change. This is a small input-distribution change, so the same four development configurations were rerun and scored independently rather than assuming equivalence. All **4/4** direct-RGB repeats again accepted the requested SC port. The five-card port-1 repeat again rejected disagreeing board views, rejected a 3.24° posture, corrected it, then accepted. Its three inspections took 182/84/87 ms. Across all six direct-RGB inspections, live verifier p50/p95/p99 were **144/176/180 ms**, maximum **182 ms**. Ordinary transport proposal p95 ranged **144–202 ms** across these repeats. No scored contacts occurred.

**Decision:** SC pause/inspect/reject/resume and board-angle correction passed this four-start development screen for target identity, posture, standoff, contact, and verifier inference time **after** the direct-RGB repair. The initial JPEG version failed latency and remains preserved. The complete handoff gate stays **open** because certified hard occlusion and coherent wrong-target cases were not tested, full attached-actor ROS-to-command latency has not been measured, and no insertion actor was attached. SFP has a promising frozen offline two-vote/cross-camera correspondence check but **no validated observation-only arrival stop**; in one prior 100-frame development replay its five-pose lock never formed and the only two accepted single-frame estimates targeted a different port. No SFP live shadow handoff was claimed. Do not train the insertion actor or RL from this result alone. Next, challenge hard SC occlusions/wrong requests with the frozen verifier and repair or establish an SFP arrival proposal on existing recorded transport traces before collecting new SFP starts.

## Bounded SFP arrival diagnostic and stopping decision

The same frozen temporal actor contains a handoff head, but its checkpoint manifest explicitly says that head was fitted **only to SC arrival labels**. The SFP runtime therefore disables the stop by default. A rootless transport-only diagnostic on the existing non-final `card4_port0_cards3_validation` scene confirmed that the default generated no stop. I then enabled an explicit `AIC_SFP_DIAGNOSTIC_STOP=1` flag for **one** matched scene run. This flag only exposes the frozen head's proposal; it does not attach an insertion actor, turn on SFP stopping by default, or read true geometry at runtime.

The head proposed handoff at **16.95 s**, with probability 0.996 after two decisions. Its auxiliary stage-5 probability was only **0.065**. Post-run scene calibration and the recorded measured TCP put the TCP **162.0 mm from the selected card-4/port-0 opening**. That is an unsafe early arrival for this purpose. The head had learned an SC timing/visual cue that did not transfer to SFP. The diagnostic is [archived here](/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-03_sc_shadow_handoff_v1/sfp_arrival_diagnostic_card4port0_optin/); the original [no-stop control](/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-03_sc_shadow_handoff_v1/sfp_arrival_diagnostic_card4port0/) is preserved too. These are **one scene**, so they diagnose this failure rather than estimate a general SFP failure rate.

The SFP branch stops at its arrival gate. The next bounded repair is to derive SFP arrival labels from existing episode-grouped trajectories and recorded failed routes, train or calibrate an SFP-specific pause proposal, and compare it on sealed development groups with post-run selected-port distance and false-stop counts. Require no early false accepted pause and a usable pause near the requested opening before attaching the SFP native-crop verifier. Existing SFP wrong-card/neighbor-port candidate cases remain mandatory negatives. No new SFP rollout collection or actor/RL training is justified by this diagnostic.
