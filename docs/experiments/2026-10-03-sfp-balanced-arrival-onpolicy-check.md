## Autonomous-distribution diagnosis

I froze the best state-only head and replayed it, without changes, on **three existing autonomous SFP transport traces** from the October 1 comparison. Inference read only each proposal's recorded measured state and task code. Exact port geometry was consulted *after* proposals for error measurement.

- `live_sfp_no_history_fixed`: first three-step trigger at 15.2 s, **35.19 mm lateral** and **127.51 mm axial standoff** from the selected opening.
- `live_sfp_only_3000`: trigger at 14.95 s, **37.39 mm lateral** and **126.30 mm axial standoff**.
- `live_sfp_long_history`: **no three-step trigger**, despite some measured positions near the opening; maximum single probability was 0.964.

These are decisive counterexamples to promoting the state-only proposal. The head learned a relationship between teacher state/task and arrival that did not survive autonomous state distribution. It cannot be repaired by simply lowering the threshold: that would increase the chance of stopping even farther from the opening. The 31/36 teacher-audit figure must not be read as an autonomous success rate. The old SFP visual cascade also has documented wrong-card locks, and its native 1,152×1,024 crop stages cannot be fairly tested on these 288×256 agent videos by upscaling them. The existing visual correspondence V5 rejected four saved wrong proposals but still had grouped point-negative errors; [prior evidence](2026-10-03-selected-port-correspondence-continuation.md).

## Fixed-clock native-RGB pause replay

I used the **existing original-resolution 1,152×1,024 wrist images** from the same three autonomous traces and froze all visual weights. A schedule fixed *before reading the images* inspected once per simulation second from 10 through 40 s, 31 checks per run. The cascade used measured TCP, public task code, and the three RGB views to estimate the selected opening. A candidate pause required three-view acceptance plus **predicted** TCP lateral ≤10 mm and axial coordinate in [-100,-70] mm. True port geometry was used only after each prediction to audit it. No actor was handed control.

The cascade alone proposed **one** pause on the long-history run at 21 s: predicted 7.55 mm lateral and -75.91 mm axial; posthoc actual 6.96 mm and -76.91 mm. It proposed **zero** on the other two runs, including their unsafe state-head triggers. On the exact early-trigger frames, three-view reprojection residuals were 55–57 px and the cascade rejected them. The one valid pause had 0.955 mm opening-position error. This is useful for pausing/inspection, not the 0.5 mm final insertion corridor.

The fixed-clock replay exposed a second failure: in `live_sfp_only_3000`, the native cascade made **nine** three-view-consistent locks about **23 mm from the requested opening** at 31–40 s, apparently on a neighboring port. Some falsely estimated lateral error near 2.5 mm while the real selected-port lateral error was about 24 mm. The axial window happened to exclude these candidates because the estimated axial coordinate was about -58 mm. Therefore triangulation agreement and a near-port estimate alone do **not** establish selected-port identity. I replayed the previously frozen request-conditioned correspondence V5 on these points: it rejected **all nine** wrong locks and retained the valid pause (three per-camera scores >0.9999). In the complete 93-check replay, cascade acceptance was 31/10/13 by run; correspondence acceptance was 31/10/4. The final pause counts remained **1/0/0**. Model plus offline image preprocessing measured p50/p95/p99 **132.55/149.60/201.35 ms** over 93 checks; this excludes ROS delivery, holding the robot, and an attached actor, so it is not the full deployment-latency certificate.

The positive and wrong locks all came from the *same requested card 0 / port 1 setting* across three historical autonomous runs. These repeated frames are correlated. The correspondence result supports the planned two-stage visual identity check on this setting but does not close the cross-card/port, offscreen, occlusion, or live handoff gates.
