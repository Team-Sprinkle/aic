# Reusing SC board geometry and rejecting inconsistent SFP candidates

**Date:** October 3, 2026. **Decision:** the two offline perception checks improved, but the automatic transport-to-insertion switch remains disabled. No new actor, RL, Gazebo collection, or final-scene evaluation occurred.

## Why this test changed direction

The preceding [native-crop test](2026-10-03-native-crop-handoff-gate.md) left SC orientation at 3.70° p95 and accepted one wrong SFP candidate triplet in each validation half. Before training another visual corner detector, I found an older [SC board-rail estimator](2026-09-24-sc-native-pose-ablation.md): it detects long, visible board edges in native RGB, uses measured TCP and training-fixed camera/mount calibration, and constrains the port orientation to the board yaw. Its small learned orientation head supplies only a search prior. This is a reusable geometric representation, rather than another generic pooled crop feature. The assumption is specific: SC board roll/pitch and port mount are fixed in these qualification-like scenes, and enough straight rails remain visible. It must reject images when those conditions fail. It uses no scored scene TF at inference.

For SFP, the residual false accepts had one camera's candidate roughly 150–243 px from the requested opening. I added a consistency test between the three RGB-predicted candidate pixels. It triangulates the candidate using the measured TCP and fixed cameras, then checks how closely the reconstructed 3D point projects back into all three images. Two of three native-crop identity votes must also agree. The selected **25 px** cutoff retained 877/881 correct training triplets with zero wrong accepts on the fitting episodes; it was fixed before reporting the two validation halves. The requested port code chooses the candidate slot, but true target coordinates do not enter this rule.

## SC result on the newer one-port development resets

The frozen RGB locator and one-port-aware orientation head produced the coarse pixel and yaw prior. The board-rail stage used the native 1152×1024 RGB. Four development reset configurations are disjoint from one-port fitting resets. The **offline sampler used scored projected-in-frame flags to select evaluation frames**; those flags were never passed to the estimator. The first sparse-label replay sampled 98 synchronized three-camera frames; **62** were within 100 mm of the port, from three resets. The fourth reset had only six sampled synchronized frames, all 142–213 mm away. I therefore repeated the same frozen method on the already saved exact-timestamp labels for the two sparse/problem resets. Combining each reset exactly once gives **87 near-port triplets from all four resets**, with 19 near-port samples from the previously missing port-1 reset. It still needs an observation-only visibility/abstention rule at runtime.

| Same 87 near-port triplets, four resets | Median full orientation error | p95 full orientation error |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Frozen one-port-aware orientation head | — | 4.434° |
| Frozen board-rail yaw plus training-fixed mount | 0.027° | **0.056°** |

The four per-reset near-port board-rail p95 errors were 0.026°, 0.056°, 0.053°, and 0.058°. The board method initialized causal history in all 87 near frames. On the **previously saved** five valid transport stops and one wrong-posture stop, a 3° predicted TCP-posture check retained **5/5** valid stops and rejected **1/1** known false stop. This reuses development stops, so it is diagnostic rather than a fresh independent safety estimate. The same frozen head alone had also passed this six-stop diagnostic; the new evidence is its much lower error on the newer one-port resets.

An offline 98-decision benchmark including three native JPEG decodes, low-resolution locator, orientation head, calibrated geometry, and three board-rail searches took **85.07/100.27/102.39 ms p50/p95/p99** after five warmups. This excludes ROS image delivery, transport/insertion actors, command conversion, and simulator timing. It does **not** certify the required complete live p95 below 300 ms.

## SFP result on the same frozen candidate proposals

The native pair verifier checkpoint, RGB candidate locations, and episode-grouped splits are identical to the preceding crop experiment. Its **offline source selection** used scored distance under 100 mm and all-camera projected-in-frame labels to construct candidate triplets; none of those labels enter the classifier or geometric accept rule. The reference accepts only if all three cameras vote positive. The repair accepts two votes when all three candidate pixels are geometrically consistent to 25 px. Labels are the projected requested versus sibling opening and are used only for scoring after the accept decision.

| Split | Episodes | Correct candidates | Old all-three accepted | New accepted | Wrong candidates | Old wrong accepted | New wrong accepted |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fitting | 19 | 881 | 881 | 877 | 1,229 | 0 | 0 |
| Checkpoint-selection validation | 5 | 87 | 66 | **85** | 423 | 1 | **0** |
| Further development | 5 | 159 | 147 | **159** | 366 | 1 | **0** |

The 25 px threshold is wider than the training positive p95 (15.56 px) and narrower than either previously false-accepted development triplet (about 94 and 99 px). Its development result is promising, but these are **constructed candidate triplets**, not actual accepted transport handoffs. Some wrong sibling candidates can agree across all cameras, so camera geometry alone cannot certify identity. Projection into an image is also not a reviewed label for whether the port is visually identifiable. The exact older `sfp_139` same-card low-resolution failure lacks native RGB and cannot be replayed through this new crop verifier.

## Decision and next bounded gate

The SC orientation and SFP correspondence **component checks** now pass on these development samples. The integrated switch remains off because hard-occlusion identifiability, coherent same-card wrong identity, complete live latency, safe pause/inspect/resume, and local actor behavior have not passed. The next experiment is a **frozen shadow handoff** on new episode-grouped development starts: run transport normally, hold on a proposal, log the new SC posture check or SFP identity/geometry check, and resume safely on rejection. Include reviewed visible/hidden requested-port images and coherent wrong-port proposals; score zero false accepted handoffs, retained valid arrivals, route/force, and complete live p50/p95/p99. Limit to a small fixed set (up to four new SC and four new SFP resets, both requested ports, with a predeclared stop if the first wrong handoff is accepted). If it fails, repair the demonstrated source—visibility, identity, or transport—before any actor or RL retraining. Keep reserved final scenes sealed.
