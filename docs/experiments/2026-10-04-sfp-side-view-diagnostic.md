# Bounded SFP side-view diagnostic

## Predeclared question and scope

Can a small, deliberate side-on wrist-camera view distinguish the requested SFP card and its port where frozen original-view predictors made coherent neighbor swaps? This is a privileged *camera-position experiment*, not an autonomous policy or a trained insertion result. The geometry-guided CheatCode route is used only to reach a safe observation posture. Frozen visual identity models receive original RGB, measured robot pose, calibration and public task code; they never receive true port geometry. Ground truth is used afterward for scoring and optional image annotations.

Reuse at most four existing nonfinal troublesome reset configurations: card 1/port 0, card 2/port 0, card 0/port 1, card 4/port 1 from `/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-04_sfp_fresh_anchor_shadow_v1/plan.json`. These were already development scenes; this experiment is a diagnostic and cannot certify generalization. Keep the four reserved final IK scenes sealed. No training, at most one GPU, at most two hours of sequential Gazebo capture and analysis. Preserve all artifacts and stopped attempts.

At the selected port, stop the guided approach while the plug tip target is 80 mm above the opening. Hold for about 1 s, move the target TCP gradually +15 mm in base-frame y at ≤2 mm commanded increments, hold for 1 s, then move to −15 mm relative to baseline and hold for 1 s. Do not descend or insert during the sweep. Save actual TCP pose, force, camera images and oracle contact stream. Motion labels are privileged for this diagnostic; visual-identity acceptance remains observation-only. The `CheatCodeSideViewDiagnostic` policy resumes ordinary CheatCode after the sweep, but only the three sweep views are identity-test data.

**Pilot safety revision, before the first lateral move:** The card-1/port-0 pilot aborted at the initial 80 mm posture under the 12 N absolute-force rule. Oracle contacts showed only cable/enclosure contact, no card or port contact, and raw wrist force was approximately 20 N throughout the approach. That absolute rule misread the already present sensor load. The corrected guard records force at the baseline view and aborts for an increase >5 N or absolute force >35 N; it also reads the current oracle contact stream at each motion increment and aborts on any pair other than the existing cable/enclosure pair, or on a stale stream. The first aborted pilot is retained and is not counted as a side-view result. This is a targeted safety-instrumentation correction; the standoff, lateral distances, scenes and visual-model rules remain fixed.

The corrected card-1/port-0 sweep completed all three view holds at measured TCP y = 0.2943, 0.3087 and 0.2794 m. Force remained 20.3–20.9 N and the oracle showed only cable/enclosure contact. The card-2/port-0 scene then triggered the **predeclared contact abort at baseline**: cable/task-board contact was already present at the 80 mm posture. No lateral move was made in that scene. Its pilot remains a safety failure; the runner continues to the other fixed configurations without relaxing the contact rule.

The other two scenes also encountered cable/task-board contact, but oracle pair forces were tiny: maxima over the relevant window were 0.0086 N (card 2), 0.00045 N (card 0), and 0.072 N (card 4), while raw wrist load stayed around 20 N. This is brushing of the cable, which is acceptable for this task; the blanket pair-name abort was too conservative. A preserved v3 safety-rule revision allows **only** cable/task-board contact with oracle pair force ≤1 N, while retaining the >5 N wrist-force-rise and >35 N absolute stops. Any new gripper, plug or other collision pair still aborts. The measured TCP must reach each requested side offset within 5 mm after settling. The same three aborted scene configurations are rerun, with the same view offsets and no model or threshold tuning. These repeats are labeled safety-instrumentation repeats, not independent validation.

Score whether a human can identify the requested opening from raw images **without overlays**, then add ground-truth markers in a separate copy for checking the interpretation. Score the frozen all-port, structured, selected-only and native-crop heads on matched baseline and shifted views; report accepted wrong port, correct accept, abstention, 3D error and camera reprojection. A view is potentially useful if at least three of four configurations expose an identifiable requested opening, while motion remains below the force limit. If human-visible views still fool frozen heads, the result supports one view-conditioned identity model on a new grouped training split. If views remain ambiguous or unsafe, stop this path and revise the observation strategy. None of these development results promotes deployment without independent scenes and latency.

## Decision

The ±15 mm side move is physically feasible under the corrected light-cable-contact guard, but **it did not improve requested-port acquisition on the remaining hard case**. The predeclared criterion that at least three of four become clearly identifiable is not certified by raw-image inspection, and the quantitative model comparison shows no new accepted request. Do not train or deploy a view-conditioned identity model on this evidence. Keep SFP transport-to-insertion handoff, autonomous insertion and RL closed; preserve final scenes. The next targeted diagnostic should establish *actual target-face visibility* and per-camera occlusion on existing hard card-2 and card-1 images, then test an observation-only ordinal card/port association or a more informative, collision-audited viewpoint only if the requested face is genuinely hidden. A new model fit needs a separate episode-grouped split and a fixed stop rule.
