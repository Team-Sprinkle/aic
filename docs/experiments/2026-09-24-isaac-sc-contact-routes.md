# Corrected Isaac SC card-contact route probes, September 24

This is a **physics-only, privileged scripted diagnostic** on the constructed five-card SC-to-SC development scene. The scene and repaired grasp/port assets are documented in [the fidelity repair](2026-09-24-isaac-sc-fidelity-fix.md). No autonomous actor, image observation, RL episode, scored insertion, or reserved final scene was used. The high-force traces are incident evidence, **not safe training trajectories**.

## Question and controlled routes

The centered corrected-asset route had no gripper/card hit. We asked whether a deliberately poor route across the five cards can reproduce a named, scene-level obstruction while all card, plug, cable, gripper, and port collisions remain active. The 20 Hz target controller used the true port frame solely to construct controlled routes. All routes used the same scene, initial reset, assets, and final port target. The transport waypoint is expressed in the selected port frame; the three numbers are meters. The across route holds its transport target through step 99, then returns to the port at step 100. This is not a policy benchmark.

The across instrumented run is the clearest incident. From steps 25–99, the gripper moved **6.74 mm** and plug **6.79 mm** while the controller continued issuing up to **20 mm** root-position commands each 50 ms tick. Named gripper/card load stayed above 100 N throughout that 75-step window. At the programmed step-100 reversal, gripper/card contact cleared immediately; the gripper moved **49.0 mm** during steps 100–110. Cable link 2/card contact persisted briefly, until about step 107, then cleared. The final target distance was about 0.10 mm, showing that the scripted return can escape this incident; it does not establish an autonomous recovery success. Peak wrist force was about 535 N in this repeat, an unacceptable load for any promoted controller.

The cable links truly register card contacts in the across and opposite routes, but these contacts occur alongside the gripper collision. We have **not isolated sustained cable/card snag or tension as the primary cause**. This distinction matters: learning from a gripper jam as if it were a cable route failure would teach the wrong recovery. The sampled link sensors cover links 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 19, and 20. The full cable's net-contact summary is also in the JSON, but internal interactions can contribute to it; a scene-filtered named pair is needed for a cable/card claim. Finger net-contact readings also include internal contacts and are not used as card force unless the scene-filtered card value is nonzero.

## Gazebo comparison and next gate

The retained five-card Gazebo route runs show an ambiguous stop, including one nearly stationary cable link close to a PCB, but no named cable/card contact. The saved world includes Gazebo's contact system; the configured ROS bridge exposes `/aic/gazebo/contacts/off_limit`, whose plugin filters the robot against **off-limit enclosure objects**, not arbitrary card or cable pairs. Existing bags therefore cannot turn that visual/geometry pattern into a proven cable snag. A future Gazebo rerun needs explicit contact-pair logging or a controlled contact ablation.

These Isaac probes establish that the corrected assets can generate **gripper/card**, **plug/card**, and **plug/port** blockages, plus cable/card contact secondary to a gripper jam. The gripper/card and plug/card incidents are route-specific constructed development cases; their prevalence under the ordinary or official evaluation distribution is unknown. Before SC visual BC or online SERL, Isaac RGB must work on this host, the distal cable shape and dynamics need Gazebo comparison, and recovery must be evaluated with safe force limits and observation-only control. Do not train on the high-force scripted routes as if they were expert demonstrations. No SC BC or RL training was started here.
