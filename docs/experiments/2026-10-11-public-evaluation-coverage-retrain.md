# Public evaluation coverage repair for route diffusion

Status: complete October 12, 2026; frozen autonomous gate failed, checkpoint not promoted. This is a bounded experiment after the failed three-scene `n400` correction gate. The reserved final IK scenes remain sealed.

## Why this experiment

The earlier [route diffusion comparison](2026-10-11-route-aware-diffusion-transport.md) reached a safe SFP buffer in only one of three reused development starts. Its correction data concentrated on five-card card1/port1 high lateral-offset starts. A complete-reset coverage audit now shows a more fundamental gap in the main teacher data:

| Verified source used by the actor | Training episodes | Main board pose range | Task spread |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| SFP-to-NIC merged source | 481 episodes / 408 reset groups | board y `-0.24` to `-0.12` m, yaw about `2.95` to `3.20` rad | all 1–5 card counts and ten card/port codes appear, but many non-card0/port1 cells have only 3–9 episodes |
| SC-to-SC Low Lateral Bypass | 83 episodes / 83 reset groups | board y `-0.235` to `-0.135` m, yaw about `2.96` to `3.16` rad | 1–5 cards and both SC ports appear, with 5–13 episodes in most count/port cells |

The [released qualification configuration](https://github.com/intrinsic-dev/aic/blob/main/aic_engine/config/eval_config.yaml) has three public anchors: one-card SFP card2/port0 at board `(0.16,-0.21,3.1)`, two-card SFP card4/port1 at `(0.16,0.05,-3.1)`, and three-card SC port1 at `(0.20,-0.15,-1.8)` (x, y, yaw in m/m/rad). The [official qualification description](https://github.com/intrinsic-dev/aic/blob/main/docs/qualification_phase.md) says the exact configuration was used on the cloud qualification platform and that future trial mixes can change. The first anchor resembles existing training poses; the second differs mainly in board y, while the SC anchor has a markedly different board orientation. Existing SC LLB data has **no exact three-card rail0/1/2, port1 anchor layout**. This is a training-coverage finding, not evidence that an autonomous policy can handle these cases.

Machine audit: [`coverage_audit.json`](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-11_route_aware_diffusion_transport_v1/coverage_audit.json). The audit uses whole episodes and released scene YAML; the `initial_port_frame_xyz_mm` values are analysis labels and are never actor inputs.

The older SC source follows the planned Low Lateral Bypass route, whereas the new public-anchor source uses the official CheatCode teacher. A no-contact buffered approach can be a useful transport label, but these are different route styles. The matched actor result will test whether one policy can condition on the observed scene well enough to retain the old route while learning the new board orientation. Any regression cannot be attributed to board-pose coverage alone without separating this route-style change.

**October 12 route-provenance audit:** The collector sets `AIC_TRANSPORT_EVAL_POLICY=aic_example_policies.ros.CheatCode` for *both* families. It does not invoke the SC Low Lateral Bypass planner. Side/overhead samples from `sc_trial3_train_04` (five cards, 10 and 20 s) and `sc_trial3_exact_train_00` (three cards, 10, 20 and 30 s) show a raised orange cable loop at or above card-top height while the plug approaches. A still image does not quantify every cable/card clearance, but these recordings do not certify the required below-card lateral corridor. The SC admission checks measured TCP buffer, force, contact and command tracking; they have **no cable-route or card-top clearance check**. Thus the 22 new SC train episodes must be tagged **CheatCode transport prefixes, route-unverified for LLB**, and the 25%-anchor SC sampling should not be reused as a route-consistent LLB recipe. The frozen coverage checkpoint is diagnostic only; its failed autonomous gate already prevented promotion. The 16 new SFP train episodes did use CheatCode, consistent with the intended SFP teacher style. See [the detailed audit](../answers/2026-10-12-diffusion-route-and-coverage-audit.md).

## Frozen plan and stopping rules

1. Preserve existing SFP and SC teachers and grouped validation. Create 40 deterministic, distinct public qualification-family Gazebo scenes: five near trial 1, fifteen near trial 2, and twenty near trial 3. The SC scenes span 1–5 NIC cards and both selected SC ports. Their random rail subsets happened to omit the exact SC trial 3 `{0,1,2}` NIC mask, so a **separate planned eight-scene SC anchor** with that mask and port 1 is required before retraining. Training/development splits are fixed in the immutable plans before rollout; the public scenes are distinct from the reserved final IK split.
2. Pilot two SFP and two SC runs. Continue only if at least one of each family fully inserts. Save the denominator, including partials and failures. Do not call a partial insertion an expert success. A separately admitted transport-only prefix may be considered only if a measured safe approach is verified and insertion commands are excluded.
3. Extract scored bags with causal camera, measured TCP, controller command, force and reset identity joins. Admit full-insertion, no-scored-contact SFP episodes only if four-command TCP-body chunks and reference tracking pass. For SC, admit **transport only** when there is no scored contact, pre-buffer measured wrist-force norm ≤40 N, and the *measured TCP* reaches the axial `-100..-70` mm / lateral ≤10 mm safe buffer. Cut the label stream at its first buffered visit, excluding all later insertion commands. Mark non-full outcomes separately; never count them as expert insertions. Keep SC physical-tip placeholder explicitly marked absent; the route actor does not consume tip geometry. Both pilot SC transport prefixes had force norm near the 20–21 N standing load, with maxima 21.35 and 21.28 N.
4. Merge admitted labels with the immutable older verified data. Keep source and split manifests. Encode three-camera ImageNet ResNet18 features at the established resolution, preserving the exact runtime preprocessing and action frame. Mark new teacher rows as `official_anchor_teacher`; the actor does not receive the marker.
5. Continue the existing 14.29M-trainable-parameter velocity diffusion model from the paired-request checkpoint. Give verified public-anchor scenes a bounded 25% sampling mass **within each family**, retaining 75% for broader old scenarios. The matched control uses the unchanged old cache, same initialization, learning rate, seed, batch size and 6,000-update cap. This comparison tests the data addition plus its declared sampling recipe; it does not isolate the sampling weight alone. Select the coverage fit by old and new grouped validation together; report results separately by family and source. The control fit started on GPU 4 while single-GPU teacher collection uses GPU 3.
6. If offline labels and latency are valid, compare the control and coverage model autonomously on the same previously frozen, nonfinal public-family development starts. SFP gate: at least two of three requested-port safe-buffer visits (axial `-100..-70` mm, lateral ≤10 mm), no worse scored contacts. SC gate: at least two of three within the configured standoff threshold without scored contact. Complete inference p95 must stay below 300 ms. No handoff, insertion actor, RL or final-scene opening follows automatically.

The pilot collector is `scripts/collect_official_anchor_route_coverage.py`; label admission is `scripts/prepare_official_anchor_route_labels.py`, SC causal materialization is `scripts/materialize_sc_scored_route.py`, and immutable source merge is `scripts/merge_official_anchor_route_labels.py`. The model sampler in `scripts/train_route_aware_diffusion_transport.py` has an explicit `--anchor-mass` flag. This record will be updated with actual episode counts, commands, checkpoints, offline and autonomous results.

## Retrain and frozen autonomous result

The corrected source union passed its duplicate-episode and complete-reset split checks. It contains **497 SFP and 105 SC training episodes (602 total)**, representing 424 and 105 distinct reset groups (529 total). These include the 16/22 newly admitted training episodes. Grouped development has 39 SFP and 26 SC episodes, including 4/5 new ones. The three-camera feature cache has **49,023 training and 7,319 grouped development rows**. The coverage actor and unchanged-data control both used the same paired-request initialization, 14,291,904 trainable diffusion parameters plus 11,176,512 frozen ResNet18 parameters, batch 48, learning rate `3e-5`, seed `20261014`, and a 6,000-update cap. The coverage actor allocated 25% of each family's sampling mass to the public-anchor rows; the control had no new rows. The coverage best checkpoint was at update 6,000 (control: update 5,000). The model uses 12 denoising steps at inference.

On grouped **teacher observations**, old SC first-action p95 changed from 2.81 mm in the control to 3.01 mm in the coverage actor. New SC p95 was 11.00 mm on the sparse checkpoint-selection samples, so the new orientation remained difficult. A broader held-out-teacher-state phase diagnosis on 5 new SC development episodes is more informative: in the near transport region (axial −200 to −100 mm), first-action median error improved **4.8→1.0 mm**, p95 **8.1→2.5 mm**, with predicted target-directed lateral motion median 8.3 mm versus teacher 8.1 mm. Four new SFP development episodes similarly improved near first-action median 1.5→1.1 mm. The old-data versus coverage same-image requested-port direction test stayed 13/13, but route-difference median error worsened 8.28→8.83 mm. These offline teacher-state numbers do not imply live success.

The predeclared **six-scene, 12-rollout autonomous comparison** used three public-family SFP and three SC development starts, with each unchanged-data control and coverage actor tested on the same scene. No guide, privileged crop, port TF, geometry or teacher was available to either actor. Port transforms were applied only afterward to the recorded measured TCP. Both actors reached the safe axial −100 to −70 mm / lateral ≤10 mm buffer on **0/3 SFP and 0/3 SC** starts. The coverage actor entered the axial band on all six, but its best lateral offset there was **16.67, 24.80, 26.25 mm** (SFP) and **68.40, 29.42, 45.35 mm** (SC). The control entered that axial band on only two SFP starts and none of the SC starts; its two non-null SFP offsets were 27.79 and 97.35 mm. The coverage actor had zero scored contacts, versus one SFP gripper/card and one SC robot/enclosure contact in control. Coverage complete inference p95 was 249.9–272.9 ms on SFP and **301.7–351.5 ms on SC**, failing the 300 ms limit. The preset promotion gate failed; no handoff, insertion, RL or final-scene evaluation followed.

The bounded trace diagnosis projects *measured* actor TCP and first predicted body-frame command into the selected port frame after rollout. On the exact SC development scene, the admitted teacher route was near axial −102 mm/lateral about 6 mm before cutoff; the coverage actor reached axial −100 to −70 mm at **45.35 mm lateral**. Original side-view frames show the plug near the board but visibly to the side of the selected opening, with no scored snag. Across live SC near-band segments, median first commanded lateral motion was often directed **away** from the port (−1.4 to −4.8 mm along the targetward axis); measured lateral motion was weak or also away. Yet the same model’s commands on held-out *teacher* observations were target-directed. This points to compounding off-teacher-state route drift, not an action-frame conversion failure on verified teacher states. The trace does not establish whether frozen global ImageNet features, insufficient actor-visited correction examples, history, or route-mode ambiguity is the dominant cause. The source also mixes the older SC Low Lateral Bypass teacher with a new public-layout CheatCode teacher; a route-style conflict remains a plausible contributor. The new actor is **not promoted**.

### Decision and next bounded experiment

**Superseded on October 12:** The proposal below preceded the route-provenance audit. Do not run it against the mixed-SC union or checkpoint. The active source is now the [route-consistent v2 union](2026-10-12-route-consistent-data-and-starts.md); only route-verified SC LLB labels may be added.

Do not expand ordinary expert collection or perform another identical BC fit. The observed teacher-state/live gap suggests **actor-visited corrective labels**. A bounded pilot should select a few new *training-split* public-family starts for each family, run the frozen coverage actor for a short prefix, then ask a privileged teacher for a causally joined, collision-checked path back to the safe transport buffer. Keep the actor prefix as context only, never as expert supervision. Before fitting anything, require at least two distinct safe corrective successes per family and verify that the teacher’s first sustained movement has the correct lateral sign, a meaningful measured path, no scored contact, and no force excursion. If that label gate fails, stop and revisit route planning or representation. If it passes, use a small declared corrective sampling mass, the same unchanged-data control and complete-reset split, and the same six-scene autonomous gate. A separate 12→8 denoising-step inference ablation may test the SC latency issue, but only retain it if offline actions and live route behavior remain acceptable. No final split, handoff, insertion or RL before ≥2/3 safe-buffer visits in **each** family and complete inference p95 <300 ms.

## October 12 direct comparison with the older deterministic transformer

After the coverage experiment, we ran the older frozen long-history deterministic transformer on the **same six scene YAMLs**, with the same 40 s simulation budget, three cameras, task request, actor/simulator GPU allocation, no guide, no geometry input, and the same posthoc selected-port scorer. All six rollouts completed; their plan, bags and frames are at `/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-12_official_anchor_transformer_live_v1/`. The three-arm machine result is [`three_arm_comparison.json`](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-11_route_aware_diffusion_transport_v1/official_anchor_retrain/three_arm_comparison.json).

| --- | ---: | ---: | --- | --- | --- |
| Older deterministic transformer | 0/3 | 0/3 | 45.8, 155.4, 132.4 mm | no band, no band, 113.8 mm | SFP 59–67 ms; SC 133–145 ms |

| New coverage diffusion | 0/3 | 0/3 | 16.7, 24.8, 26.2 mm | 68.4, 29.4, 45.3 mm | SFP 250–273 ms; SC 302–352 ms |

All three arms failed safe transport. On these frozen scenes, the **coverage diffusion checkpoint made more useful axial/lateral progress** than the older transformer, especially on the released opposite-side SFP and rotated-board SC layouts. It was much slower and failed the SC latency limit. There were zero scored contacts for the transformer and coverage diffusion; the unchanged-data diffusion control had two. The previous deterministic transformer had lower first-action teacher-state error than the first diffusion fit on an older validation set, while an earlier matched five-card SFP rollout favored diffusion (17.99–20.49 mm versus 55.79–68.89 mm lateral). These facts compare saved policies, not architecture in isolation: the coverage diffusion fit used newer public-layout data and a different training objective. A fair architecture ablation would train a capacity-matched deterministic head on the **same corrected 602-episode union** and evaluate new sealed development starts. No such ablation has been run, and no checkpoint is promoted from this comparison.
