# SFP actor-state stage-aware transport label gate

**Predeclared before the October 10 read-only audit.** The objective is to decide whether saved actor-to-CheatCode continuations provide safe, useful *route-segment* supervision at the actual actor failure boundary. This is not ordinary teacher BC and does not authorize a policy fit by itself.

## Fixed source and split

Use the existing `temporal_cache_late15_v1/rows.jsonl` actor-prefix plus teacher cache, which contains 16 train and six validation episode/reset groups. Do not move a validation group into training or select thresholds from its result. The three October 8 new takeover runs may be audited separately for support, but are not silently merged into the frozen six-group validation set. Read official full-insertion and prohibited-contact scores from their original scored streams; a successful CheatCode return value is insufficient. Cable/NIC-card point-force evidence requires contact-enabled Gazebo replays and remains a separate requirement.

## Stage-aware label definition and gate

The takeover's first teacher command is not a motion label. At 200 ms sampled measured TCP states, find the first 0.4–0.8 s window within four seconds of takeover whose net translation is at least **5 mm**, path/net ratio at most **1.3**, and whose next 0.4 s does not immediately reverse (cosine with the initial window direction at least **0.7**). This window marks the onset of sustained motion after settling. From its start, record the measured polyline to the first **10 mm net** motion within four seconds and require path/net at most **1.5**. Keep the polyline, orientation, delay from takeover, and initial direction, rather than shortcutting directly to a distant endpoint. Check the first segment's direction independently against displacement at the first **20 mm net** point within five seconds of onset; this later point is outside the 10 mm label and is not used to select the onset. Report the cosine and failures by group. These numerical thresholds define a **label-quality diagnostic**, not a claim that every valid detour is straight. This independent check was made explicit after noticing that using the onset filter's immediate 0.4-second cosine as the held-out gate would be circular; the initial audit output was not used for training or promotion.

Before any actor fit require: at least **20 distinct train and six held-out reset groups** with officially successful, no-penalty teacher continuations; usable stage labels in all six held-out groups; at least **5/6** held-out initial directions with cosine ≥0.7 against later safe motion; no wrong requested card; and a small contact-enabled Gazebo subset showing the route labels do not induce cable/NIC-card force above the October 9 10 N point-force gate. If any of these fail, do not train. Diagnose label geometry/coverage, then evaluate the distinct fixed-request candidate-route outcome comparison instead of collecting more identical takeovers.

Only if this label gate passes: fit one matched full-action actor comparison, same base checkpoint, data, grouped split, update budget and seed, adding the stage-aware route supervision to the candidate only. Require held-out route-direction improvement without >10% ordinary SFP/SC action regression, then at most three new nonfinal autonomous starts with ≥2/3 safe buffered arrivals, no worse scored/contact-oracle force, and complete p95 inference <300 ms. Keep automatic handoff, insertion, RL and reserved final scenes closed until the autonomous gate passes.

The October 9 [route diagnosis](2026-10-09-sfp-cable-side-route-repair.md) explains why official no-contact scoring alone cannot certify cable safety. The earlier [fixed-time/20 mm-waypoint failure](2026-10-08-sfp-short-route-waypoint-feasibility.md) motivates the measured-motion onset test.

## Completed audit and two bounded new takeovers

The reproducible read-only audit is `scripts/audit_sfp_stage_aware_takeover_labels.py`. It joined all **22** cached actor-to-teacher episodes to their original scored streams and full-insertion scores. The fixed 16 training/six development scene groups were distinct. All 22 officially fully inserted with zero **official** contact and insertion-force category penalties. A sustained short measured route segment was found in **16/16 train and 6/6 development**. The independent first-motion versus later-20-mm direction gate passed **5/6** development groups; the sixth did not reach 20 mm net within five seconds. Do not quote the preliminary `audit.json` 6/6 immediate-direction result: it reused the onset-selection condition. The corrected independent audit is [`audit_onset_context.json`](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-10_sfp_stage_aware_label_gate_v1/audit_onset_context.json).

This finding is narrower than a valid *actor-boundary* action label. The motion onset occurs **0.20–2.01 s after takeover**; measured TCP had already moved **more than 5 mm in six of 22** cached episodes (up to 20.21 mm). A route label attached to the later teacher observation cannot silently be attached to the earlier actor observation. The stored measured polyline and onset orientation are potential supervision for a *stage-aware sequence*, but a causal action for the intervening takeover-to-onset interval has not been verified.

The October 8 three-run train-only pilot was audited separately from high-rate controller streams. Two full-insertion runs have short stage labels and one partial-insertion run is excluded; their scene hashes do not overlap the 22 cached groups. One full pilot has a later-direction cosine of **0.519** and cannot be treated as a straight corrective heading. A [separate audit](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-10_sfp_stage_aware_label_gate_v1/pilot_audit_v2.json) retains all three, including the failure.

Exactly **two** new, previously unused four-card *training* resets were then selected from the existing verified paired-request source: port 0 scene `d2cb5178…` and port 1 scene `40244bef…`. They ran **sequentially on GPU 3** in rootless Gazebo with the frozen actor for 15 s followed by privileged CheatCode, with the read-only oracle contact plugin enabled. Both officially fully inserted, with no official contact penalty. Their measured stage labels began 0.402/0.594 s after takeover, with 1.76/4.35 mm boundary-to-onset TCP movement and independent later-direction cosines **0.833/0.774**. This brings the *bookkeeping* training count to **20 distinct successful, short-segment-usable resets**, with six separate development resets. The run logs, exact configs, bags, extracted camera/controller streams and oracle contacts are under `/home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-10_sfp_stage_labels_card4_port{0,1}_v1/`; the extracted summaries are [`port 0`](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-10_sfp_stage_aware_label_gate_v1/card4_port0_audit_v2.json) and [`port 1`](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-10_sfp_stage_aware_label_gate_v1/card4_port1_audit.json). No model weights changed.

The contact check changes the safety conclusion. For port 0, the sampled cable-link/NIC-card point normal force after takeover peaked at **2.00 N**. For port 1, it was **1.00 N during the 0.96 s label**, **2.92 N in the first five seconds**, but later rose to **55.52 N at simulation time 26.55 s**, while the route still achieved official full insertion. Thus the official score does not certify a force-safe continuation. The contact plugin reports point normal impulse divided by step time at 100 Hz and can miss shorter events; it is not a net wrench. Exact per-window events and peak pairs are in the [machine decision](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-10_sfp_stage_aware_label_gate_v1/decision.json) and each run's `corrected_contact_timeline.jsonl`. This single high-force success does not mean every short stage label is unsafe; it prevents treating the entire continuation as a safe teacher route under the frozen 10 N cable/NIC-card rule.

## Distinct route-ranking feasibility check

The proposed alternative was to compare multiple short routes **from the same actor state** and learn which outcome is safer. Before new collection, I audited the five October 9 side-route replays. All five used the **same evaluation configuration hash** and request, but their measured actor takeover TCP positions differ **11.83–31.07 mm pairwise**; orientations differ up to **3.09°**. A high-sweep route passed only one of three starts. Those outcomes cannot isolate route choice from changed robot/cable starts. The repository search found no existing exact Gazebo cable-state restore path. There is no matched-state route-outcome training set yet, so fitting a route ranker from these five traces would be misleading. The pairwise table and exact hashes are in the [machine decision](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-10_sfp_stage_aware_label_gate_v1/decision.json).

I also rendered and visually inspected the [five-run takeover gallery](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-10_sfp_stage_aware_label_gate_v1/route_takeover_camera_gallery.jpg), with the [exact original-frame paths/times](../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-10_sfp_stage_aware_label_gate_v1/route_takeover_camera_gallery.json). The *diagnostic* overhead view shows much more of the orange cable loop; it is **not an actor input**. The three wrist input cameras mostly show the card row, plug neighborhood and only a proximal orange cable segment near the image edge. That is an observability warning for a cable-risk model from the present three images, not a measured classifier failure: five changed-start examples cannot establish whether camera history plus force is sufficient. Test a cable-risk predictor against robot-state/route-only baselines on new reset groups before relying on it; if the distant cable state is required, consider an available wider view or a safe observation maneuver.

## Decision and next repair

**Both training gates remain closed; do not fit or deploy an actor from this material.** The stage audit meets the numeric count and 5/6 direction checks, but lacks a verified causal action sequence from the *actual* takeover state in the six groups with substantial pre-onset movement and cannot certify the full cable-safe teacher continuation. The route-ranking branch lacks state-matched counterfactuals. No automatic handoff, insertion, RL or final evaluation ran.

The next bounded engineering question is whether the simulator can **reproduce or restore the complete actor takeover state, including cable pose/velocity and contact state**, with sufficiently close measured robot and cable geometry. Budget **one nonfinal reset, two no-action restores, one GPU, at most two hours of simulator/tooling time**. Before testing, freeze a fidelity gate: takeover TCP position within **2 mm**, orientation within **1°**, measured proximal cable-link position RMS within **5 mm**, and first-second wrist-force trace p95 difference within **2 N** between restores. These are *proposed engineering tolerances*, not measured achievements; the starting controller target and joint velocities must also be restored or explicitly rejected. Do not train a ranker unless the two restores pass and the same action from that restored state produces comparable early motion/force. If exact restore is unavailable, revise the route experiment toward an *on-policy, state-conditioned cable-risk predictor and feedback controller*, trained/evaluated on whole reset groups without claiming paired counterfactuals. Before any new BC fit, represent the full takeover-to-onset sequence (including waits and turns) and validate it in contact-enabled Gazebo; do not attach a later segment to the earlier actor frame. The preserved failed gates are the stopping evidence.

Gazebo Sim's [EntityComponentManager API](https://gazebosim.org/api/sim/7/classgz_1_1sim_1_1EntityComponentManager.html) exposes `SetState` for serialized entity/component state, and [Gazebo logging](https://gazebosim.org/api/sim/8/log.html) records/playbacks entity poses. These are possible building blocks, **not evidence of an exact live cable/contact-state restore**: pose playback alone is not the counterfactual physics continuation needed by the ranker. The local AIC launcher has no such verified restore path, so it requires the no-action fidelity test above before use.
