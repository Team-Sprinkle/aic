# Transport reliability continuation: causal history, target selection, and late standoff

Date: October 1, 2026. This is a supervised, observation-only continuation of the [five-way comparison](2026-10-01-transport-five-way-comparison.md). It uses rootless Docker and at most GPUs 0–2 concurrently; GPU 0's older process was left untouched. No reserved final scene, runtime teacher pose, RL, or insertion actor was used.

## Failure hypothesis and matched tests

The previous temporal head saw the **teacher's prior four-command chunk** in training but its own proposal history during deployment. The exact held-out ablation in `outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_transport_reliability/prior_action_audit.json` increased first-action p95 from **2.27 to 29.54 mm SC** and **2.62 to 31.89 mm SFP** when that prior input was zeroed. This is evidence of heavy dependence on a field whose training and deployment distributions differ.

I trained matched 6,000-update long-history heads, keeping all data, split, labels, and architecture fixed. The original-input head selected update 6,000 with held-out first-action p95 **1.90 mm SC / 2.28 mm SFP** and did not plateau. The no-prior head selected update 2,750 and stopped after update 4,000 for the prescribed validation plateau, with p95 **5.60 / 6.90 mm**. Commands, manifests, logs, histories, checkpoints and summaries are in `outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_transport_reliability/history_{previous,no_previous}_6000*`. The no-prior model's offline fit is worse, but its train/live input now matches.

On the existing non-final paired SC five-card start, the no-prior actor first reached **6.94 mm and 0.86°** at 47.25 s, and its closest point was **4.36 mm and 0.91°** at 47.5 s. Before first satisfying 10 mm / 3°, its measured path was **26.06 mm p95** from the recorded MoveIt teacher polyline. It then continued to run, drifting **94.63 mm** from that near-goal point and ending 90.03 mm from the standoff. The run is `live_sc_no_previous/`; it has an autonomous trace, posthoc path plot, five-camera MP4, official score, latency, and terminal observation. No insertion was attempted. This shows a near-success on one development start, not reliability.

Training the same no-prior actor with an SC route-stage auxiliary loss improved held-out first-action p95 to **4.92 mm**, but its matched live SC run ended **337.38 mm** away, never closer than 204.99 mm, and scored a collision penalty. This variant is rejected (`history_no_previous_stage_aux/`, `live_sc_no_previous_stage_aux/`). A second variant added handoff supervision; held-out sampled handoff counts looked good, but the phase-supervised actor had already failed live. A separate linear handoff head trained on the **frozen no-prior action weights** was tested instead. It stopped a live rollout **36.07 mm** from the goal: an unsafe early handoff. It is rejected (`frozen_actor_handoff/`, `live_sc_frozen_handoff/`). The frozen-stage-only probe classified route phase poorly enough to trigger stage 5 early on 8 of 21 held-out episodes even at its best checkpoint; it is not used for control (`frozen_actor_stage/`).

## Observation-only selected-standoff pose probe

The first 3-camera frozen-ImageNet + ordinary-state + task-code MLP predicts the complete teacher transport standoff relative to the measured TCP. Its geometric training label is derived from the recorded final MoveIt subgoal; no simulator geometry is a runtime input. On 21 scene/reset-grouped SC validation episodes, translation p95 is **5.63 mm** when the true TCP is within 50 mm and orientation p95 is **1.03°**. However, swapping only the selected-port code on the same image changes its predicted target by just **16.64 mm median**, versus about **74 mm** true physical separation in the paired reference scene. This first probe is rejected for target selection (`sc_goal_probe/`, `sc_goal_probe_calibration_v2.json`).

To force the public task code to identify the correct SC port, `scripts/build_sc_counterfactual_standoff_labels.py` makes **training-only** alternate-port labels from each recorded board/rail configuration. Each training image is seen with either SC port code and the corresponding target. The new matched probe selects update 4,750 of 5,000; near-50-mm translation p95 is **5.52 mm**, orientation p95 **1.08°**, and same-image port-code swap changes the predicted target **77.51 mm median**. ## Live late-transport test and gate

An optional late transport mode is implemented in `RunTemporalTransport.py`: the unchanged shared body-frame actor follows the route until the observation-only standoff predictor reports <35 mm and <3° for three causal decisions. Then a position-filtered **direct complete TCP-body target** moves at most 8 mm per 200 ms decision toward the predicted pre-insertion standoff. This is a bounded transport target, not an action residual, true geometry input, or insertion command. The actor can stop only after the predicted remaining distance is <=6 mm for three decisions. The original policy remains the controller before the trigger and for all SFP scenes. The live test must show a smooth route, no premature activation, final measured <=10 mm / <=3°, acceptable contact, and p95 latency <300 ms on several SC development starts. If it fails, the mode is not promoted. The rootless launcher records the probe checkpoint, trigger/step settings and SHA256 hashes.

The first live servo run on `llb_0005` **failed**: nearest measured standoff was 42.6 mm, the final position was 201 mm away, and orientation ended 45.5° off. The predicted target in base coordinates moved as the robot moved: it was already about 20 mm wrong at activation and around 127 mm wrong by 65 s. The servo is therefore disabled by default and not promoted. The exact run is `outputs/experiments/2026-10-01_transport_reliability/live_sc_goal_servo_llb_0005/`.

The decisive check was teacher images versus actor-generated images. On held-out teacher views the selected-port contrastive probe had 5.52 mm p95 translation error within 50 mm of the standoff; on 67 comparable frames from two entirely held-out five-card **actor rollouts**, the same checkpoint had **35.53 mm p95**. This distribution shift explains why good offline teacher metrics did not translate into a safe visual servo. `scripts/cache_sc_onpolicy_goal_features.py` synchronized 1,843 actor decisions to three recorded native JPEG cameras and attached the teacher standoff only as an offline label. Its manifest keeps four complete 1/3-card scenes for adaptation and two complete five-card scenes for validation. A single bounded MLP adaptation selected update 2,250/3,000 and reduced held-out actor-view near-50-mm p95 to **19.94 mm**, while teacher-view near-50-mm p95 regressed from 5.52 to 7.11 mm. Same-image selected-port flipping still moved the target about 75 mm, so port-code response was retained. The 19.94 mm error remains too large for a 10 mm transport handoff; no live retest or further routine collection was authorized by this evidence. Artifacts: `onpolicy_features/manifest.json`, `onpolicy_goal_adaptation/summary.json`, and their exact command/log files.

The earlier high-resolution SC locator, native crop, keypoint and ray-solve system remains a plausible targeted alternative. It measured submillimeter position on its own fresh development scenes, but those were recorded expert trajectories; it has **not** been checked on these actor-generated transport frames. Its orientation arm also had a frozen-scene failure before a board-line correction. A complete camera calibration, target selection, latency, and new-scene check is required before connecting that system to control.

## Bounded previous-command robustness test

The action head's teacher-prior dependence motivated one matched augmentation: mask each previous command token with probability 0.5 and add Gaussian noise of 0.5 normalized action units in training only. Architecture, 60 s visual history, episode split, action labels, and 6,000-update cap match the prior action-only transformer; validation scores both clean teacher-prior and all-zero-prior inputs. It selected update 2,000, stopped at 3,250 for plateau, and gave held-out first-action p95 **4.78/6.39 mm SC/SFP** with teacher priors, **5.84/7.61 mm** with zero priors. The prior-free actor's earlier teacher/zero metrics were 5.60/6.90 mm for SC/SFP. Thus the augmentation made the SC input somewhat more tolerant than the unaugmented teacher-prior head, but it did not establish better autonomous transport; the matched live checks are recorded in `history_mixed_previous/` and `live_sc_mixed_previous_*`. The stopping rule for promotion is a higher 10 mm / 3° handoff rate and lower route deviation on complete held-out SC starts without a safety or latency regression.

Step 5, full insertion, RL, and reserved final scenes remain gated.

## Live outcomes of the bounded continuation

The previous-command augmentation was rejected on the matched five-card `llb_0074` scene. It came no closer than **206.4 mm** to the standoff, reached a teacher-path deviation of **102.8 mm p95 even before its closest point**, and ended 530.8 mm away with an official collision penalty. The earlier no-prior actor on this same start came within **19.0 mm**, had 25.2 mm path p95 before that point, and then drifted away. Thus corrupting teacher prior commands in this manner did not solve closed-loop covariate shift; the good offline score was misleading. Exact trace, scored result, model hashes, and diagnostics are in `live_sc_mixed_previous_llb_0074/`.

The six no-prior actor rollouts showed a narrower, repeatable problem: their nearest standoff occurred around **43–55 s**, but each kept acting until the 70 s cap and ended 85–187 mm away. A posthoc, episode-grouped check of a 30 mm, three-decision goal-probe signal on the six recorded traces found an appropriate late trigger in four cases, including **both held-out five-card adaptation-validation scenes**; the two three-card traces never came within its threshold and did not trigger. The teacher standoff only scored the trigger after the fact (`onpolicy_goal_stop_posthoc.json`). This check did not establish performance on a new rollout.

An optional **stop-only** mode now uses the adapted observation-only standoff probe as a terminal signal while leaving the action transformer unchanged. It issues no visual-servo correction and is disabled by default (`AIC_SC_GOAL_STOP_ONLY=1`, 30 mm predicted distance, <5° predicted orientation, three consecutive decisions, at least 15 s). On `llb_0074`, an autonomous live replay stopped at 51.2 s with a **13.8 mm** posthoc final standoff error and **1.92°** orientation error. The corresponding time-cap baseline ended **84.7 mm** away. Route p95 before stop improved from 25.2 to **18.9 mm**, and complete inference p95 was **193.9 ms**. This improves transport termination but still misses the required 10 mm handoff corridor; it is neither insertion nor a promoted policy. Trace and metrics: `live_sc_stop_only_llb_0074/`.

The first previously unused five-card validation start, `llb_0009`, exposed an upstream route failure: the same unchanged actor came no closer than **38.1 mm**, with **47.4 mm** path p95 before nearest and a 126.2 mm final error. The stop signal did not fire. This was not a stop-timing failure, and increasing the stop threshold would merely end a wrong route (`live_sc_stop_only_llb_0009/`). On a second previously unused five-card start, `llb_0184`, the stop fired near the approach and ended **24.9 mm / 1.80°** from the standoff, with **25.5 mm** route p95. The new-start pair is mixed: stopping prevents late drift where the route is close, while route generalization remains open.

## Route correction from recorded actor observations

A targeted DAgger-style supervised diagnostic uses the six previously recorded no-prior actor rollouts. Four complete one/three-card scene groups are adaptation training; two complete five-card groups are held out. The MoveIt path is read **only** to create offline complete TCP-body action targets from each measured actor state; it is absent from runtime inputs. A first nearest-waypoint match was rejected before training because overlapping path segments left the match around waypoint 200 of approximately 300 even after the actor approached the final port. The retained label version uses monotone alignment constrained by the episode's offline closest-approach time, then four 0.2–0.8 s plan lookahead targets. Samples more than 80 mm off the matched path are excluded. This aligned 884 training rows and 501 held-out five-card rows (`onpolicy_route_labels_v2/`). The closest-approach time and plan are label-generation privileges and cannot be used by the autonomous actor.

Fine-tuning the full transformer with equal route-correction and original teacher-replay losses reduced held-out correction-action p95 from **64.7 to 35.9 mm** but regressed teacher SC/SFP p95 from **5.38/7.25** to **9.55/9.34 mm**. It failed the retention gate and no checkpoint was promoted (`onpolicy_route_correction_v1/`). A single reduced correction weight of 0.25 selected update 2,500/2,500: held-out correction-action p95 **34.81 mm**, teacher SC/SFP p95 **6.71/7.53 mm**. This met the preset loose retention gate (SC ≤9, SFP ≤10 mm), but training had not plateaued and offline action fit does not prove route improvement. A paired live check on the two previously unused five-card starts is required before selecting or extending it (`onpolicy_route_correction_weight025/`).

## Continued route and perception diagnosis (October 1, later runs)

The full-head fine-tune had already failed teacher-replay retention. These results show why another blind supervised fine-tune is not justified by offline imitation loss alone.

The first live dual-port servo on held-out `llb_0009` used GPU 1 for Gazebo and GPU 2 for inference, leaving the unrelated GPU 0 process alone. Complete inference p50/p95/p99 was **231/284/305 ms**; p95 met the 300 ms limit, but this exceeds the 200 ms nominal decision interval and dropped 252 of 1,252 scheduled chunk members. The visual target remained stable within roughly 1–2 mm of the teacher's posthoc standoff, and the servo activated at 33.85 s. The robot approached to **18.0 mm** at 34.9 s, then drifted to **61.6 mm final** with **23.8°** orientation at closest. The target-to-measured TCP direction in logged base coordinates was correct, so this was not a body/base frame inversion. That component shift may be gravity projection; it is **not evidence of cable snag**. The official scorer reports no disallowed contact and no excessive force. A single **servo-only gain** ablation changed Cartesian translation stiffness from 90 to 1,200 N/m after visual activation, with damping 80. It did not alter the learned actor, target or pre-trigger gains. On the same held-out scene, pre-trigger route variation delayed activation until 63.95 s, so this is not a perfectly matched same-state force test. Once active, measured TCP moved 57 to 4 mm from the visually estimated standoff in about three simulation seconds and stopped at **0.54 mm posthoc terminal position error**. However, the actor's inherited orientation ended **9.57°** from the teacher's transport pose, and full-route deviation was still **56.4 mm p95**. The scored episode had no insertion because this is a transport-only runner. Complete inference p95 was 286 ms, with 266 stale chunk members dropped. Position improved substantially on one scene; reliable transport and the full 10 mm / 3° handoff gate remain unproven. Run: `live_sc_visual_standoff_llb_0009_v5_servo_gain/`, with command, scene, model and calibration hashes.

A training-only mean SC transport orientation was fitted from the six calibration plans without using held-out plans as an input. Posthoc angular errors of this fixed prior on the two held-out scenes were **2.53°** and **1.51°**. The two-port point separation alone is not a reliable yaw cue because the individual port placements vary. The fit script, exact six/two split, quaternion, and held-out scoring are in `scripts/fit_sc_visual_transport_orientation.py` and the calibration JSON. This is a specialized SC transport prior, not a general port-pose estimator or insertion policy.

**Current decision:** The action-only transformer remains the route baseline; the dual-port locator plus high-gain visual standoff controller is a promising late-transport diagnostic, disabled by default. Do not promote from the one position-only handoff. Test the orientation-enabled controller on both held-out five-card development starts and at least one different card count; compare complete measured 10 mm / 3° handoff rate, route p95, force and latency against the frozen baseline. If it cannot stabilize the corridor without off-route motion, investigate the 200 ms decision/284 ms inference mismatch and route-following state distribution before further model sweeps or RL. Final scenes, local insertion, and RL remain gated.

## Bounded matched guard decision test (running)

Because Gazebo repeats vary before the guard first fires, one success cannot establish a reliable route fix. `scripts/run_sc_transport_z_floor_match.sh` freezes the same `llb_0009` development scene, actor, perception/servo checkpoints, camera preprocessing, 80 mm visual trigger, rootless GPUs 1+2, and 70 s simulation budget. It runs six new autonomous transport repeats in alternating order: three with the optional 180 mm measured-TCP floor and three without. The existing fast no-floor run failed one full handoff; two earlier floor-enabled runs passed but only one exercised the guard. Each new run keeps exact scene/model hashes, full trace, scored bag, camera frames, and posthoc teacher-path metric. No reserved final scene is opened.

The **decision rule** is predefined: keep the guard as a candidate only if it fires in a low-route trial, improves full 10 mm / 3° handoff frequency and route p95 relative to the matched control, and causes no scored contact, excessive force, or p95 inference over 300 ms. A single guarded wrong low loop or substantial route degradation keeps it disabled. Even a favorable result on this one scene requires a new-layout check and cannot establish general reliability or insertion. If the guard effect is absent or inconclusive, stop repetitions and prioritize a route-level controller/representation repair; do not resume blind action-head sweeps or RL.

**Matched guard decision:** All six new repeats completed; raw outcomes and exact hashes are in `zfloor_matched_comparison.json` and `matched_zfloor_{control,guard}_{1,2,3}/`. Controls reached the full transport gate **3/3**, with route p95 **26.6, 17.1, 20.8 mm** and measured minimum Z **189.9, 180.6, 170.6 mm**. Guarded repeats reached it **2/3**, with route p95 **57.9, 33.1, 67.1 mm**; the guard fired **0, 32, 70** times and kept measured minimum Z **199.2, 177.4, 176.1 mm**. All six complete inference p95 values were below 195 ms. The third guarded run reached 0.87 mm terminal position error but was 3.78° off, so it did not pass the joint gate. No run had scored disallowed contact or excessive force. The old unguarded low-loop failure and the separate guard-exercised success remain preserved; the new matched set does **not** show a reliability gain. The height guard therefore remains **disabled by default and unpromoted**. Further same-scene repeats are stopped. The remaining route spread calls for a route-level representation or correction, not an assumption that a vertical bound solves lateral route control.

The orientation failures have a narrower pattern. In the accepted SC teacher plans, goal TCP roll is about 151–152° and pitch near zero across the inspected one/three/five-card layouts, while yaw shifts by several degrees. ### Bounded early-route target-size test

Posthoc traces show that a five-card fast-path failure first deviated around 10 s and had route errors of roughly 42, 69 and 94 mm at 20, 30 and 40 s, before visual activation. A successful recent-stop run had roughly 31, 16 and <1 mm at those times. Before 30 s, the actor requested TCP translation targets up to **44–46 mm**, while measured speed was far smaller. A matched frozen-controller ablation lowered the previsual translation target cap from 60 to **30 mm**; after visual activation the 8 mm direct target stayed unchanged. The hypothesis was that shorter targets reduce overshoot and route drift.

The 30 mm cap passed on five-card `llb_0009`: first gate **0.59 mm / 2.87°**, route p95 **19.46 mm** versus **27.57 mm** at 60 mm, terminal **0.74 mm / 2.06°**, inference p95 **193.55 ms**. A different four-card, port-1 start `llb_0193` disproved general improvement: the capped run still passed the final pose gate, but route p95 rose from **19.31 to 42.06 mm** and previsual TCP Z fell from **176.9 to 155.5 mm**. Inference p95 was 191.63 ms and the score recorded no bad contact. The cap is **rejected as a general route fix**; retain the 60 mm default. The paired posthoc summaries are `visual_transport_cap_comparison.json` and `visual_transport_cap_fourcard_comparison.json`. Scene, model and source hashes are in each run. Gazebo repeats vary, so one run per setting cannot isolate a small effect, but the large opposite-direction route change precludes promotion.

The SC teacher episodes used Cartesian stiffness `[90,90,90,50,50,50]` and damping `[50,50,50,20,20,20]`, matching the previsual actor rollouts. A simple gain mismatch is therefore unsupported. The teacher followed absolute base-link Cartesian plan targets; BC labels converted its recorded targets into complete TCP-body targets and runtime converted them back. Earlier numerical frame roundtrips passed. The remaining failure is closed-loop route robustness: locally plausible early actions sometimes carry the actor into states unlike the teacher scenes. The late visual controller often recovers the standoff but cannot guarantee the cable-safe corridor after a wrong early route.

**Corrected route-label audit and bounded representation test:** The existing cache *already* used stable per-phase MoveIt subgoals; the earlier 44.46 mm route-head error was not caused by a moving-target label. Within-episode stage-goal spread is 0.00036 mm at p95 after reconstructing those goals in the base frame. The distinct bounded test changed the high-level waypoint representation from current-TCP-relative 9D to a fixed-base 3D waypoint, while retaining all action targets as TCP-body commands. Its input was the same frozen three-camera RGB feature, measured state, public task code, and elapsed time. The route plan was only a training label. Both probes used the 83/21 complete-episode grouped split and 12,981/4,477 SC train/validation rows. A constant stage-mean waypoint had p95 **44.6–76.3 mm** by phase. The 256-wide MLP achieved **94.35%** held-out stage accuracy, **20.94 mm** known-stage waypoint p95, and **24.03 mm** p95 when selecting its own stage. One wider 512D/.05-dropout version completed 3,000 updates, selected update 2,700, and improved to **95.02%** stage accuracy, **20.60 mm** known-stage p95, and **22.36 mm** predicted-stage p95. Late route phases 3–5 remained **21.0, 22.2, 23.9 mm p95** even when the true stage was supplied for scoring. Both miss the predeclared **20 mm joint gate**, so this branch is stopped without live control. Artifacts: `sc_base_waypoint_probe{,_w512}/summary.json`, checkpoints, logs, exact command files, and `scripts/train_sc_base_waypoint_probe.py`. The waypoint is a high-level diagnostic target, not an absolute-coordinate action policy. Final scenes and RL remain closed.

**Decision and next bounded repair:** The existing action transformer plus late visual correction remains the best *development* transport candidate, but early route reliability is not established and no insertion policy is enabled. Another width or action-cap sweep would not target the late-stage visual ambiguity. The next experiment should first audit the three wrist-camera views at phases 3–5 on correct and wrong routes: determine whether the selected port and the near/far ends of the card row are visible and whether a task-conditioned locator can identify the safe outside lane. Use existing teacher and actor frames with episode-grouped labels only. If these landmarks are observable, train a small multi-view landmark/corridor head and require held-out corridor p95 <=10 mm, correct selected-port sign, and no early false route switch before wiring a measured-TCP waypoint controller. Bound this audit/training to two hours on one GPU and stop if the landmarks are unobservable or the offline gate fails. The 20 mm route p95 and 10 mm / 3° transport handoff, force, and <300 ms live latency remain the promotion gates. No new ordinary teacher collection or RL is justified by the current evidence.
