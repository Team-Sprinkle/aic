# Native crop requested-card and paired-port repair

## Frozen hypothesis and data audit

The [joint-camera peak matcher](2026-10-06-sfp-joint-camera-candidates.md) failed because a neighboring card can produce a low-reprojection, temporally stable wrong pose. An older **full-image** 15-channel slot/pair model failed with only 1/136 accepted development observations; repeating that architecture would not be justified. This attempt keeps the newer **original-image 480 px crop centered by frozen RGB rail predictions** and trains one shared crop backbone to output three points for the selected card: its projected rail anchor and both port openings. The public requested card code chooses the crop and conditional embedding; requested port 0/1 selects which of the jointly predicted openings is returned. Simulator CAD projections supervise these outputs, but do not enter inference or crop selection.

Data audit on the existing 70 complete training reset groups: 21,262 present-request proposal rows; per-card request counts 6,082/4,020/3,028/3,006/5,126 for cards 0–4. After deduplicating the two port requests for each card/frame, sample cards with inverse-frequency weights. Across all 63,786 training request-camera views, both ports are inside the 480 px crop in 21,032; long-range frames often do not contain them. In the first three saved seconds, both are inside in ≥2 cameras for **2,438/3,094** request rows. Train in-crop flags separately from point coordinates. The rail label is a CAD projection, not independently reviewed visual visibility; its meaning must not be inflated.

The architecture initializes from the 2,000-update single-point native crop checkpoint and replaces its one-point heatmap/flag heads with a shared three-point head. One camera crop is encoded **once**. Train on the 70 groups for at most **2,000 updates or 120 minutes on one GPU**, with ten complete reset groups for checkpoint selection. All candidate requests are task balanced by card. Frozen development acceptance: triangulate all three predicted channels when their three camera flags are ≥0.5 and reprojection ≤5 px; require both predicted ports 15–35 mm apart, and predicted rail point within 40 px of the independent RGB rail crop center in at least two views. Score selected-port pose only after these tests. The fixed checkpoint objective penalizes wrong accepted ports first, then missing early groups, then accepted pose error.

Development gate: zero wrong accepted ports, ≥9/10 early groups and accepted selected-port 3D p95 ≤20 mm. If it fails, diagnose the labeled point errors and abstention reasons on development without tuning on the two already-scored additional ten-scene sets. If it passes, collect a new grouped nonfinal ten-request evaluation, preserving final scenes and measuring full live latency before any handoff. Transport safe buffered arrival remains a separate failed gate; no insertion actor or RL is enabled by this perception result alone.

This serialization correction did not change the model, data, losses or gate. `fit_v2/` finished all **2,000 updates** on 10,631 unique card/frame crops from 70 reset groups. A read-only decomposition found 36 selected-port-only accepted points (zero wrong, 2.23 mm p95); the predicted rail agreed with the RGB crop center on 115/119 rows, but both paired ports yielded valid 3D predictions on only 27/119. Requiring all three channels and the 15–35 mm pair gap makes coverage collapse. Stop this three-point guard; its very low conditional error is based on five points and does not meet the handoff gate. Before another image model, test one cheaper **observation-only port-region prior**. The raw port-minus-rail pixel offset varies by more than 200 px across long routes, so a constant offset is unsuitable. Fit one small shared function from **RGB-predicted rail endpoints, requested card/port code, camera identity and measured TCP pose** to the projected requested-opening offset. Training labels use only the 70 fit groups; the ten checkpoint-development groups assess accuracy. The independent additional groups remain closed for this design. Predeclared feasibility criterion: held-out p95 pixel error **≤30 px** for requested ports that project in-frame, and the predicted prior is closer to the requested opening than to any neighboring-card same-port opening on **≥95%** of valid development views. A failure stops this coordinate-prior idea. Even a pass only justifies a subsequent frozen geometric gate test on new nonfinal groups; no simulator port pose enters the runtime prior.

## Coordinate-prior result and decision

The small two-layer prior completed **2,000 updates** on **56,074** train request-camera examples from the same 70 complete reset groups. Checkpoint selection used **355** in-frame requested development views from ten other reset groups. The best update (1,600) had **49.1 px median and 111.3 px p95** error, and its predicted point was closer to the requested opening than to every other same-port card opening in **92.96%** of views. Both fixed feasibility bars (≤30 px p95, ≥95% identity separation) failed. The prior is too broad to gate neighbor-card candidates and was not used in a port-acceptance replay. Its input included measured TCP and *RGB-predicted* rail endpoints only; true port projections were labels and scores. **Decision:** Neither the three-point local head nor the state/rail coordinate prior clears its development gate. Do not open another new test set for either or reweight the same losses. The single-point crop detector remains the safest offline candidate, but its early coverage differed between two already-scored ten-scene sets (6/10 and 10/10). The next distinct question is whether original RGB offers a **visually identifiable selected card** at all approach stages. Revisit the earlier blind-view uncertainty review; label card identity and actual occlusion on a bounded sample of original full-row views from existing saved development and autonomous traces, including neighbor swaps. If a human cannot reliably identify a card in those observations, an active side view or route adjustment is needed before another network fit. If a human can, test a shared full-row + native-crop classifier trained specifically to decide whether a candidate belongs to the requested card. Freeze complete reset splits and abstention criteria before fitting. No automatic handoff, insertion or RL follows this failed branch.
