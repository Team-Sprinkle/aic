# Step 5 handoff: why the shared visual policy has not yet inserted SC cables

**Status as of 2026-09-25:** Steps 1–4 of the [14-step shared SFP/SC plan](2026-09-24-shared-actor-sc-serl-execution.md#ordered-steps-and-pass-criteria) supplied an audited split, bounded Isaac mechanics checks, observation-only pose work, and scored SC demonstrations. **Step 5 is still open.** No evaluated shared policy has reliably inserted from a full-start SC development scene. The supervisor, offline/online SERL, Isaac-to-Gazebo transfer, released production evaluation, and four reserved final IK scenes have not been promoted under this plan. This page is a synthesis for a reader familiar with AIC; the [execution log](2026-09-24-shared-actor-sc-serl-execution.md) and linked machine reports are the source of exact commands and per-run data.

## What step 5 asks us to prove

Train **one** actor for SFP-to-NIC and SC-to-SC. At each decision it receives the three ordinary camera views, measured robot state, task/selected-target code, and available observation-only pose information; it emits a short chunk of **complete target TCP poses**, not a residual correction to a prior action. Successful evaluation starts at the beginning of an episode and must move through transport, alignment, and insertion without CheatCode, true plug/port coordinates, a guide, or a recovery rule. The required evidence is scored full-depth insertion on *new episode-grouped development starts* in both task families, including multi-card SC, with acceptable contact/force and full inference p95 below 300 ms. The score's Tier 3 partial credit is **not** a full-insertion event.

The original shared action target was the teacher's recorded TCP goal expressed in the selected port-opening frame. An explicit later ablation instead expresses the **same full teacher target** in the currently measured TCP body frame. That ablation does not ask the model to predict absolute world coordinates and does not add a delta to an older policy prediction. At runtime, its prediction is anchored to the observed TCP and sent as an absolute base-frame controller setpoint. Simulator geometry is used to construct training labels and to inspect scored bags **after** rollouts; it is not an actor input during autonomous evaluation.

## Data and task-code reality

The initial verified pool had **289 scored episodes**, with 250 train and 39 validation, and 268 SFP versus **21 SC** episodes. The original SC experts covered only 1–3 NIC cards, and port 1 appeared only with one card. Subsequently, 11 clean scored SC successes filled all **12** `(0–5 cards, target SC port 0/1)` cells, but some cells have only one example. See the [success matrix](2026-09-24-sc-near-bias-and-success-matrix.json), [admission audit](2026-09-24-sc-bc-admission.json), and [inventory](2026-09-24-shared-actor-sc-inventory.json). Later training-only collections added 31 full successes in a 60-scene SC batch and 3 successful five-card/port-0 lift retries; many attempts still failed and were excluded from successful BC. This is evidence that Gazebo **can** yield CheatCode-based SC insertions in some settings, but not that it supplies diverse, robust expert coverage in every setting.

The current **10D task code is not a unique one-hot bit for every card-count/port setting**. It has two family bits, two selected-port bits, five target-NIC-card-index bits, and one NIC-card-valid bit. For SC-to-SC, there is no target NIC card, so the final six bits are zero even when NIC cards obstruct the route. SC **card count is not explicitly encoded**; the actor must infer obstacle layout from its images. The selected SC port *is* encoded. Therefore, lack of a unique one-hot bit for a five-card SC setting is not a literal missing-category failure, although sparse visual and target-port coverage may still prevent generalization. Conversely, seeing a single RL success would not automatically teach the actor a task code: the success must be represented in replay, attributed to the correct state/action sequence, and learned without losing the supervised behavior.

## What is established, and what remains a hypothesis

**Established:** Some Gazebo SC CheatCode/teacher variants fully insert, so “Gazebo cannot produce SC expert trajectories” is too strong. They are sparse and sensitive to route and start. The shared BC actor can imitate some held-out actions offline yet fails to finish autonomously. In the latest body-target comparison it approaches more safely than the older port-frame arm, but ends centimeters away. Full-start port visibility and far-view pose are harder than local near-port pose. The latest 18-episode teacher-rescue treatment did not improve the two autonomous development outcomes in a meaningful way.

**Unverified candidate 1 — inadequate demonstrations:** One or a few successful routes per SC card/port cell may be too little for the visual actor to learn obstacle-dependent transport and the precise final approach. More nominal CheatCode runs that all follow similar routes may have low marginal value; demonstrations showing successful recovery from the actor's actual state distribution may be more valuable. The failed two-second near-port takeover shows that collecting such *successful* recoveries is itself hard.

**Unverified candidate 2 — task/context representation:** The selected SC port is encoded, but SC card count is only in RGB. The model may confuse obstacle layouts, ignore the task code, or fail to bind the selected port to the image. This needs a controlled ablation with matched episodes and per-cell outcomes. Simply asserting that an unseen one-hot category is unlearnable would misdescribe the actual 10D code.

**Unverified candidate 3 — BC objective or closed-loop distribution shift:** Supervised millimeter-scale target error is a weak proxy when each missed target changes the next observation. The teacher often corrects using privileged geometry, while the autonomous actor must infer that geometry from difficult full-start views. A multimodal route or recovery choice may also be averaged by a single-target loss. The current matched correction test cannot distinguish insufficient recovery diversity, sampling, model capacity, and remaining perception error.

**Unverified candidate 4 — RL plus reward design:** It is plausible that BC alone will not solve underrepresented SC cells and that online interaction could learn insertion, blockage response, and route choice. But an occasional sparse success is not a complete learning signal. A testable RL branch would preserve the BC actor, record actually executed actions and terminal states, and compare reward designs on *new training/development scenes*: true insertion event as the primary success, task-progress shaping that does not reward stopping near the port, lateral/orientation alignment where valid, measured-motion/stall and force/contact costs, and bounded time/retry costs. Separate components are needed so a high partial score cannot masquerade as full insertion. Evaluate whether the agent actually discovers successes and retains SFP behavior, rather than relying on reward curves alone. This is a **proposed hypothesis**, not a result from the current shared-policy plan. Isaac incident simulation must first be checked against the specific Gazebo failure being optimized; sequential Gazebo RL is available but inefficient and may need repair. The planned deterministic retreat supervisor and actor-chosen retry would be compared against no/local/hierarchical retreat once the prerequisite gates are met.

## Decision boundary for whoever resumes this work

First, preserve the latest negative matched comparison and inspect the two policy recordings together with the scored bag's measured TCP/plug path, force, and contact identities. Then run one bounded, episode-grouped diagnosis that distinguishes **visual target loss**, **wrong full-target action after a correct observation**, and **missing recovery/route behavior**. If supervised additions continue to improve offline errors without producing full-start insertion, reconsider the step-5-before-RL gate explicitly: a tightly bounded RL/reward-design experiment on *training-side short incidents*, with BC warm start and sealed development/final scenes, may be justified as a diagnostic. Do not relabel teacher-assisted or privileged-control successes as autonomous policy successes, and do not use held-out final configurations for reward tuning.
