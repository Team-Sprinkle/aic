# SFP observation-only route target and bounded Gazebo gate (October 9, 2026)

## Decision

**Do not train or deploy a new route actor yet.** The selected-port visual position was causally available at all six saved actor takeovers, and a fixed board-normal prior produced an accurate offline 85 mm buffer goal on these reused scenes. Privileged MoveIt routes reached that buffer in **2/3** frozen Gazebo replays, but the five-card replay stopped under rising force. The cable proximity screen still cannot distinguish that failure from a successful route. A route label that can be called safe and a sufficient episode-grouped actor-state label set are missing. The conditional actor, automatic handoff, insertion, backtracking and RL steps remain open. No final scene was opened.

## What was measured

The six frozen actor prefixes are three development and three training scene/reset groups (2–5 cards). The deployed-input side of the audit uses only three camera observations, public requested card/port code and measured robot state. True port geometry was read only **after** the predicted goal was fixed, for error scoring. The separate MoveIt paths and their Gazebo replays used privileged scene geometry; they are **teacher-route diagnostics**, not autonomous policy results.

The prior two-view visual memory expired after two seconds and had an anchor at only 5/6 takeovers. In the missed case, accepted views were 2.2 seconds apart but only 103 mm of measured TCP travel apart. A causal 150 mm travel-pair rule with the time cap disabled retained the correct requested-port anchor at **6/6** takeovers. Posthoc selected-port position errors were 0.26–1.06 mm. On six earlier matched autonomous routes, the same distance-pair replay gave zero wrong nearest-port anchors among 315 sampled valid anchors (combined 12-route replay: 353 valid samples), with maximum posthoc position error 2.47 mm. These are reused development routes, not an independent generalization claim. Earlier routes still often lost the anchor before the eventual endpoint.

For an 85 mm buffer, pointing from the **current TCP** toward the predicted port produced 7.4–27.6 mm lateral goal error on the six takeovers. A fixed vertical board-normal task prior, selected before reading any true pose for these scenes, produced **0.9–1.3 mm** lateral goal error. A visual port-axis estimate combined with the independent selected-port position was available in 5/6 and produced 0.5–1.7 mm lateral error. The sixth case had no valid visual axis. Full-pose cascade acceptance occurred in only 4/6; one position-rejected cascade had a useful axis, but this is exploratory and has no independent acceptance rule. The nominal-axis result depends on the board being nearly vertical: the six scoring-only true axes varied little, so it must be stress-tested under broader allowed orientations before deployment. The fixed prior is a task-geometry assumption, not per-scene oracle geometry.

All six saved actor-state rigid-arm MoveIt variants found a path, but the rigidly carried cable proxy rejects known safe teacher motion. Signed box distance fixes the old inside-box saturation but still gives negative values on safe teacher and successful Gazebo paths. It cannot certify a cable-safe route. Do not use its five-of-six relative-to-teacher ranking as a safety gate.

## Three preselected real-cable replays

The cases and route variants were frozen in `frozen_route_replay_selection.json` before new Gazebo execution. The 2-card and 3-card privileged routes reached the 85 mm buffer within **4.36** and **3.58 mm**, with no scored contact and peak force changes **2.15** and **1.50 N**. The 5-card route aborted at waypoint 52 during descent: commanded TCP height fell from 0.353 m at waypoint 40 to 0.278 m, while measured height fell only from 0.356 m to 0.339 m. Tracking error rose from 9.3 to 64.2 mm and force change from 6.47 to 12.64 N. The official score said “No contact detected,” which only addresses its scored contact category. The five-camera video shows cable over a card, but the bag has no contact-pair topic. Measured cable-to-card signed proximity stayed around −3.9 to −4.1 mm for link 6 during the high-force interval; a successful two-card replay also had negative proximity. Thus cable drag, another collision, or controller interaction remain unresolved causes. This is **2/3 buffered arrivals**, not 2/3 proven safe routes and not autonomous insertion.

### Separate contact-attribution diagnostic

After freezing the three-run comparison, one **additional** rootless replay of the same five-card scene enabled the read-only Gazebo oracle contact plugin. This did **not** reproduce the high-force abort: its actor takeover was 19.1 mm higher and about 14.7 mm offset in the horizontal plane from the failed replay, its live MoveIt path differed, and it reached the buffer within **3.08 mm** with peak measured force change **1.59 N**. Therefore it cannot attribute the original abort. It did record cable link 8 contacting the task-board top/left collision shapes near the end of the successful route (from simulation time 20.14 s; sampled peak point normal force about **13.5 N**). That physical contact is outside the official scored-contact category. The normal-force estimate uses the existing pinned Bullet impulse correction, is per contact point rather than net wrist force, and 100 Hz snapshots may miss brief 500 Hz events. Cable-board contact and successful buffer arrival can coexist; neither “no official contact” nor a signed-distance overlap proves snag.

The [contact-enabled replay](</home/nas/chmin/aic_archives/2026-10-09_sfp_route_observability_v1/gazebo_case3_oracle_attribution/>) retains `oracle_contacts.jsonl`, `contact_force_summary.json`, `corrected_contact_timeline.jsonl`, the changed actor takeover in `moveit_plan_request.json`, the live plan and the measured replay trace. Its result is a separate diagnostic and does not change the frozen 2/3 comparison.

I then completed the declared **three-start** contact-attribution budget on that same nonfinal five-card scene. None began within 10 mm of the original failed takeover (differences **19.5, 17.1, 22.8 mm**). One reproduced the *failure class*: cable links 4–5 pressed into a NIC card/mount during the final approach, with a sampled peak point normal force of **98.3 N**. NIC cable contact exceeded 10 N at simulation time **20.42 s**, before the measured wrist-force change exceeded 10 N at **20.80 s**. That run reached only **10.44 mm** from the buffer goal, just outside the declared 10 mm gate, and its tracking p95 was **46.4 mm**. The other two reached within **4.37/3.05 mm**, with wrist-force peaks **1.34/1.91 N** and NIC contact peaks **2.56/0.32 N**. Their cable also touched the task board at roughly 15–16 N sampled point normal force. This strongly supports a **cable–NIC-card interference mechanism** for some takeovers, while the exact contact pair in the original uninstrumented abort remains unknown. The nominal retreat route is not reliably safe across actor-prefix variation. ## Revised next gate

The earlier 2/3 buffered-arrival threshold is numerically met, but its **safe** requirement failed under actor-prefix variation. The bounded contact-attribution budget is now exhausted: one of three additional starts reproduced high-force cable–NIC-card interference. The next experiment is a **targeted route repair**, not another repeat of the same nominal retreat route. Use the observed link-4/5 NIC collision to design at most two distinct side-clearance or cable-aware approach variants on this reused five-card scene. Screen both against measured actor takeover cable shapes, then compare each with the frozen retreat route in at most three **new nonfinal** contact-instrumented Gazebo starts per candidate; require 2/3 buffered arrivals within 10 mm, no wrist-force stall and no cable/NIC-card point force above a declared engineering limit. Declare that force limit before execution; the observed 98 N event is clearly unacceptable but the 15–16 N task-board touches on successful routes show that a blanket zero-contact rule is too strict. Stop if neither variant passes; do not fit an actor from unsafe paths. Independently stress the observation-only nominal-axis/visual-axis goal on new episode-grouped scene orientations and requested ports, with zero wrong-port goals. Then obtain at least 20 distinct training reset states and six held-out actor-state labels whose *executed* short continuations agree with the intended safe direction (at least 5/6 held-out cosine ≥0.7 after meaningful motion), as required by the prior [waypoint feasibility gate](2026-10-08-sfp-short-route-waypoint-feasibility.md). Existing six actor-state paths and actor-to-teacher settling labels do not meet that gate. Use a bounded pilot of at most 30 actor-state route queries and six validation takeovers; stop if labels or safety fail. Only then fit the matched route-aware actor and proceed to new autonomous 2/3/5-card testing. Automatic handoff/insertion and RL remain disabled until their downstream gates pass.
