## The idea in plain words

The robot has two movement actors. **Transport** follows the requested task from the initial position, including when the destination is offscreen. It learns the SC route around the cards. When transport thinks it has reached a safe neighborhood in front of the requested port, it **proposes a pause**. A coarse, task-selected camera check can reject an obvious wrong destination or unsafe view before the precise crop work; its positive result authorizes only a held inspection. The final selected-port verifier must then confirm the requested opening from original-resolution images and check the estimated pose, identity, posture and force. If perception cannot identify it, the robot must not substitute another visible port. If it verifies the target and safe waiting pose, the **insertion** actor takes over and predicts port-relative tool targets.

```text
Task code + three wrist cameras + measured robot state
                         |
                         v
TRANSPORT actor: route toward the requested destination
                         |
        arrival head proposes "pause and inspect"
                         |
                         v
COARSE task-selected camera check: reject or allow held inspection
                         |
                         v
Hold the measured TCP pose; refine original-resolution SELECTED-PORT pose
and run the FINAL identity / pose / posture / force verifier
             |                                  |
     invalid / unsafe                         valid
             |                                  |
    resume / seek view / hold          INSERTION actor
```

These decisions are **consecutive**, not competing options. Transport's proposal is not permission to insert. Coarse requested-target perception can reject an obvious wrong stop and decide whether to hold for a precise inspection; it cannot grant final handoff. Precise perception plus posture, route and force checks is the final accept/reject verifier. The insertion actor remains separate because it produces full target TCP poses in an **estimated port frame**, whereas transport produces movement chunks in the moving TCP/body frame. Neither actor gets Gazebo target coordinates at runtime.

**Current implementation versus intended design:** Transport inference runs repeatedly and computes a stop signal from observations; it does not stop merely because a timer or sampling cadence fired. The learned transport stop head uses a probability/streak threshold when explicitly enabled. The development SC visual stop compares an RGB-estimated requested-port standoff and orientation against limits for consecutive decisions. Elapsed-time guards only prevent premature use of a signal. These SC stop checks have made false stops or misses in development, and the complete SFP coarse requested-target gate is **not integrated or approved**. The coarse → held precise check → final handoff sequence above is the intended deployment contract, not a claim that it already works end to end. [Matched stop evidence](../../../docs/experiments/2026-10-02-transport-stop-comparison.md).

An accurate pose **does help decide when to stop**: together with measured TCP pose and the desired pre-insertion offset, it yields a distance and orientation error to the waiting posture. Its precision must match that decision's tolerance; coarse pose can decide when to inspect, while precise pose is needed for insertion. Pose alone cannot prove requested-port identity, visible evidence, safe route, acceptable force, or stable observation. The matched SC replay found a visually estimated stop that was only 1.02 mm from the desired position but was **6.71° misoriented** on a wrong route; this is why the final verifier checks more than position.

## Exactly what the task code does

The 10D public code is `[is_SFP, is_SC, port_0, port_1, card_0, ..., card_4, card_valid]`. For SFP-to-NIC it names a NIC card and port 0 or 1; for SC-to-SC it names SC port 0 or 1. It does not encode obstacle positions, cable shape, or visibility. Transport uses it to choose the route and destination. The new local perception model uses it to answer **one question: “Where is this requested opening?”** The insertion actor uses it alongside RGB, measured state, and the selected-port pose. Other visible ports are distractors, not replacement targets.

## Precise perception: one selected pose or invalid

**Inputs:** three synchronized RGB images near the destination, public task code, measured TCP pose, and fixed per-camera calibration. A low-resolution full view may nominate a region; a crop from the **original-resolution image** refines the requested opening. Crop selection uses RGB and the task request, never true simulator geometry.

**Outputs:** an identifiable-visibility yes/no for the **requested opening in each camera**, and one selected-port 3D position/orientation relative to the measured TCP **if visual evidence supports it**. Otherwise output **invalid / no reliable pose**. No learned confidence probability is in the next experiment. Visibility says whether each image supports locating the requested opening; it does **not** certify submillimeter pose accuracy. Camera agreement, short-window stability, selected-port identity checks, safe TCP standoff/orientation, and force/contact checks are separate handoff conditions. Their combined false-acceptance rate must be measured.

**Training labels:** for each observation and task request, use the requested port's ground-truth pose relative to measured TCP, its projected image keypoints where identifiable, and **actual identifiable-visibility labels** for each camera. Mask image/pose losses when the selected target is hidden or absent; still train the visibility/rejection output. Projection inside the image alone is not visibility. Validate new segmentation/depth or render-derived occlusion labels by inspecting samples because prior auxiliary depth capture had issues. Use same-image, different-task examples: if both SC ports are visible, request port 0 in one row and port 1 in another; if only port 0 is visible but port 1 is requested, the correct answer is **invalid**. Do the analogous card/port cases for SFP. Split by complete scene/reset group.

The current coarse locator instead has 12 candidate **image-location** slots, then selects one for precise 3D estimation. It does not produce accurate 3D poses for all hidden ports. Keep it as the comparison baseline. A selected-port model may internally notice neighboring openings to avoid confusion, but it returns a pose only for the requested one. The new design is a hypothesis, **not yet a measured improvement**.

An earlier [single-keypoint task-conditioned pilot](../../../outputs/experiments/2026-10-02_two_family_perception/conditional_locator_v3/summary.json) already attempted the selected-output idea with a low-resolution view and projected-in-frame labels. It had large held-out native SFP error. The next comparison therefore specifically tests **near-target original-resolution crop refinement and checked visibility/rejection labels**, rather than merely repeating task conditioning.

## Explicit runtime handoff

1. At episode reset, read the task code and preload both actors and perception. Reset their histories. Transport acts on cameras, measured state, and its causal motion/command history. The precise selected-port model does not need to run throughout the route.
2. A transport arrival head proposes **“pause and inspect”** from observations only. Its job is to recognize a safe pre-insertion neighborhood, not estimate the opening to 0.5 mm. Require a stable signal and assess orientation and route quality. Do not use elapsed time or the nearest visible port as the sole trigger.
3. On proposal, command a **hold at the currently measured TCP pose** and take fresh images. Run selected-port perception. Reject the proposal if the requested opening is not identifiable, the selected identity is ambiguous, camera geometry or short-window estimates disagree, the waiting pose/orientation is unsafe, or force/contact or route state is unacceptable. A rejection resumes transport, seeks a better view, or continues a safe hold under a tested policy.
4. On acceptance, pass fresh RGB, measured state, task code, and the estimated TCP-in-port pose to the insertion actor. It outputs complete target TCP poses in the estimated port frame. Convert each target with the current measured TCP and estimated port frame to the controller's base-frame Cartesian command. Continue monitoring observations and force. Do not claim a lock proves correctness; a briefly occluded, previously verified static port may be carried only by an explicit bounded tracking rule.

The **10 mm position / 3° orientation** transport arrival criterion is currently a **post-run test against ground truth**, not a runtime measurement. A runtime gate must use the visual estimate and measured TCP, then be audited against truth offline. Complete live inference p95 must remain below **300 ms**.

## What exists now, and why this revision is needed

- The temporal transport model already has an optional learned handoff-probability head. A frozen SC diagnostic requiring probability at least 0.95 twice after 15 s gave **2 valid, 2 false, and 3 missed** arrivals on seven saved paths. It is not a reliable automatic switch.
- A separate SC late visual-standoff controller gave **5 valid, 1 false, and 0 missed** on those paths. Its false stop was close in position (1.02 mm) but wrong in orientation (6.71°) and route. It remains development-only.
- SFP has no validated observation-only transport stop. In a wrong-target replay, accepted single-frame poses sometimes corresponded to a different physical port about 48 mm away. Multi-camera agreement alone did not guarantee correct identity.
- Current local RGB perception uses a coarse all-candidate locator plus selected-port refiners and temporal locks. Its “in-frame” scores are **not actual visibility**; locks can preserve a consistently wrong identity. The local insertion actor has not shown reliable autonomous full insertion from a normal start. The current transport runner returns when its experimental stop fires; it does **not** automatically start the insertion actor.
- Teacher-assisted insertion and accurate pose on one scene are not autonomous full-episode success. Final scenes remain sealed. This handoff revision does not advance the gated RL or world-model branches.
